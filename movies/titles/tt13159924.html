The genetics

The franchise's superhumans come from Compound V, the corporate substance that gives people powers, and the series extends its satire of corporations manufacturing heroes for profit. The main genetic thread, by this database's criteria, is the laboratory pathogen: in The Woods, a secret facility under the university, Dr. Edison Cardosa, overseen by Dean Shetty, develops a 'Supe virus' meant to kill Supes and experiments on students without consent, leaving Sam Riordan with severe mental trauma. The virus is left as a world-threatening danger at the end of the first season. Science and scientists are shown as tools of a cynical corporate and institutional system that treats young people as products and test subjects. The sources do not describe the genetic mechanism of the powers or the virus; the series is a gruesome satire, not hard science fiction.

The story

This spin-off of The Boys follows young superheroes, or 'Supes', at Godolkin University, an elite school of crimefighting run by the corporation Vought International, where students compete for rankings and a place in Vought's team The Seven. In the first season, freshman Marie Moreau, who can control blood, and her fellow students Andre, Emma, Cate and Jordan uncover the school's secrets: Dean Indira Shetty secretly oversees The Woods, an underground facility where Dr. Edison Cardosa experiments on students such as the traumatised Sam Riordan and develops a virus designed to kill Supes, which the season leaves as an apocalyptic threat. In the second season, under Homelander's rule, the university's new dean, Cipher, preaches Supe superiority and mentors Marie, but he turns out to be a puppet of Thomas Godolkin, the school's founder, a Supe who can possess bodies. With war looming between humans and Supes, the students discover a programme that could change everything. The series was cancelled after two seasons.
