The genetics

Genetics is central to this vampire film. Vampirism is presented as a natural mutation, and the plot hinges on the Brotherhood's secret genetic research to save its kind from extinction by creating a female vampire. While the church publicly bans genetic research and blames it for influenza outbreaks, its scientists inject an engineered virus into pregnant women; the virus mutates, kills the subjects and turns the researcher Edgar into an insane killer who tries to infect a whole district. The scientist is a villain and the institution behind him is hypocritical and ruthless, ready to burn the slum for the greater good, so research in secret, human experimentation and a man-made epidemic all appear as dangers. The ending is ambiguous: the experiment has produced the first female vampire, a 'perfect creature' that the hero hides from the Brotherhood. Real viruses can carry genes into cells, but not turn a fetus into another kind of being.

The story

In an alternative, steampunk New Zealand, a genetic mutation that appeared three centuries earlier causes some boys to be born vampires. They form the Brotherhood, a church that protects humans in exchange for blood, but no female vampire has ever been born and no new vampire has been born for seventy years. When women are found killed by bites, detective Lilly Squires works with Brother Silus, who reveals that the killer is his brother Edgar. Edgar had worked on the Brotherhood's secret research to create a female vampire with a virus injected into pregnant women; the virus mutated, turning the women into violent psychopaths and killing them, and it drove Edgar mad. Edgar escapes and contaminates the slum's water supply with his blood. Defying the Brotherhood, Silus saves Lilly, who kills Edgar, and they find the baby girl the virus produced, the first female vampire.
