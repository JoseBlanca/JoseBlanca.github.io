The genetics

No genetic science appears; the film is a comedy about adoption and the search for biological roots. Mel believes that finding his birth parents will tell him who he is, to the point of being unable to name his own son until he knows. The answer the film gives favours nurture: his biological parents and his hostile biological brother turn out to be eccentric drug makers who flee to Mexico, and the journey ends with Mel recognising the people who raised him as his parents. The film takes the pull of biological origins seriously enough to build a road movie around it, but treats the hope that ancestry will explain one's identity as a comic illusion. Knowing one's biological parents can tell a person about some inherited traits and family medical history, but much of who a person becomes is shaped by upbringing and experience.

The story

Mel Coplin, a young New Yorker raised by loving but neurotic adoptive parents, refuses to name his newborn son until he has found his biological parents. Tina, an attractive but incompetent adoption-agency employee, finds a name in the agency's database and joins Mel, his wife Nancy and the baby on a trip to San Diego, which turns into a chaotic tour of the country. The first supposed mother is the wrong woman, and a trucker in Michigan turns out only to have handled the adoption. Two ATF agents, a gay couple, join the party, and Mel and Nancy each flirt with someone else. In New Mexico Mel finally meets his real parents, ageing hippies who gave him up while in jail for making LSD and still make it. In the end Mel names his son and calls his adoptive parents his parents.
