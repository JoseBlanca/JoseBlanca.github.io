The genetics

Genetics is mentioned rather than used: Dr. Holliston is a geneticist, but the experiment that drives the story is not genetic. In an artificial womb he grows a dog and then a human foetus to adulthood in a very short time, using a growth hormone, a drug to stop the resulting rapid ageing, and later foetal pituitary extracts. The film presents his attempt to benefit mankind as a scientist's terrible mistake that produces an amoral, murderous woman, and one reviewer saw it as a retelling of Alraune, equating artificial birth with a lack of conscience. Real hormones cannot compress years of development into days.

The story

Dr. Paul Holliston, a widowed geneticist who runs a clinic from his home, saves an unborn puppy from a dog he has run over by placing it in an artificial womb and speeding its growth with an experimental hormone. The dog grows up in days, learns astonishingly fast and becomes aggressive. Hoping to help mankind, Holliston repeats the experiment on a human foetus taken from a suicide victim. It rapidly becomes a brilliant young woman, Victoria, whom he educates and introduces as a graduate, and with whom he begins an affair. But Victoria's cells start ageing rapidly; to save herself she poisons Holliston's suspicious sister-in-law, destroys his research and kills to obtain foetal glands, finally taking the unborn baby of Holliston's daughter-in-law and killing his son. Now old, she crashes her car, and as she is taken away in labour she reveals she is carrying Holliston's child.
