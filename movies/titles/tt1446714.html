The genetics

Genetics frames the film's question about the origin of humanity. The opening shows an Engineer's DNA breaking down and recombining in water, implying that life on Earth, or at least humanity, was seeded with his genes, and the expedition later confirms that Engineer and human DNA match. The same creators have developed the black liquid, a mutagenic biological weapon that rewrites living organisms and that they apparently intended to use to destroy humanity. The scientists on board are archaeologists searching for meaning, while the expedition is driven by a corporate magnate who hopes the Engineers can save him from old age, and by an android who experiments on the crew without their consent. The idea that intelligent beings deliberately brought life to Earth, known as directed panspermia, was proposed by Francis Crick and Leslie Orgel in the early 1970s, but there is no evidence for it, and human DNA fits within the shared genetic ancestry of all life on Earth.

The story

In the distant past, a humanoid alien drinks a black liquid on a lifeless planet; its body dissolves into a waterfall and its DNA breaks apart and recombines. In 2089, the archaeologists Elizabeth Shaw and Charlie Holloway find matching star maps in several ancient cultures and interpret them as an invitation from humanity's creators, the "Engineers". The dying billionaire Peter Weyland funds an expedition aboard the ship Prometheus to the moon LV-223, where the crew find an Engineer structure, dead Engineers whose DNA matches that of humans, and cylinders of black liquid. The android David secretly infects Holloway with it; Holloway sickens and is killed, and Shaw, previously infertile, finds herself pregnant with a creature she removes by surgery. The base proves to be a military installation for a biological weapon intended for Earth. A surviving Engineer kills Weyland and prepares to launch, but the crew sacrifice themselves to stop him, and Shaw leaves to find the Engineers' home world.
