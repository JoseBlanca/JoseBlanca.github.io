The genetics

Genetic engineering underpins the world of the film. Olympus's managers have created bioroids, engineered humanoids who serve humankind and run the city's government, and some sources describe the whole society as bio-engineered for harmony. The plot turns on DNA as a key: Hitomi's genome carries an override code for the supercomputer that runs the city, which makes her the target of the terrorists. The film is ambivalent about this engineered utopia. The heroes defend it and the terrorists are defeated, yet the story gives weight to Charon's view that it is a stifling cage, and reviewers found the film unsure whether to see its genetically managed order as good or bad. The authorities who created the bioroids are not shown as villains. The bioroids, like the replicants of Blade Runner, are fictional; nothing like them exists.

The story

After a third world war, the survivors have built Olympus, a utopian city run by the supercomputer Gaia and inhabited by humans, cyborgs and bioroids, genetically engineered beings who make up most of the population and run the administration. Police officer Charon, embittered after his wife's suicide in what he sees as a golden cage, joins the terrorist cyborg A. J. Sebastian to bring the system down. They raid the facility where bioroids are made in order to steal data on Hitomi, a bioroid whose DNA is a genetic key able to shut Gaia down. The ESWAT officers Deunan Knute and Briareos try to stop them. Charon is killed and Gaia goes offline, but Deunan restarts it with a last shot and Sebastian dies.
