The genetics

The series' genetic element is its weapon: the virus, which the Army of the 12 Monkeys deliberately releases and which kills most of humanity and keeps mutating. The sources available do not describe how or where the virus was made, and the plague serves mainly as the catastrophe the time travellers must prevent. The scientist closest to it is Cassandra Railly, a virologist presented as brilliant and heroic, whose study of the outbreak gives the future its only lead on the plague's origins; the scientists of Project Splinter are physicists working on time travel. As Wikipedia notes, unlike the 1995 film, the Army here is a real organisation, and the series' real subject is predestination versus free will. Its virology is a plot device rather than an exploration of real biology.

The story

In 2043, after a plague released in 2017 has killed about seven billion people and keeps mutating towards humanity's extinction, the scavenger James Cole is recruited by Project Splinter, a team of scientists led by the physicist Katarina Jones, to travel back in time and stop the release of the virus by a mysterious organisation, the Army of the 12 Monkeys. His guide is a recorded message left by the virologist Cassandra Railly, whom he finds in 2015; together they try to unmask the Army's leader, the Witness, while their changes to the past ripple through the future. Over four seasons the story widens into time paradoxes, alternate realities and the Army's members, including the mathematically gifted Jennifer Goines. The Witness turns out to be Athan, the son of Cole and Cassie raised by the Army, and the series ends with a definitive resolution of its loop.
