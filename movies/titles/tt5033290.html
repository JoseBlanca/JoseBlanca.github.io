The genetics

The horror rests on a biological weapon of the First World War: parasitic worms engineered in a secret German bunker to kill livestock in Allied countries, which jumped species to humans and drive their hosts to insane violence. Reiner, the German officer in charge, was delighted by this and deliberately bred deadlier strains, intending to destroy Europe and rebuild it under German rule; the film presents him as a sadistic villain. Against him stand Dr. Priest, the British expert who identifies the worms as engineered and pleads for civilian lives, and the German Captain Mueller, who is disgusted by the project and sacrifices himself to destroy it. The film therefore condemns weaponised biology while showing decent people on both sides. Engineering parasites in this way was far beyond the science of the time.

The story

In 1918, Lieutenant Berton, a Canadian tunneller who was once trapped underground for twelve days, is sent with British intelligence officers Major Jennings and Dr. Priest and an American escort to investigate Trench 11, a deep German bunker in the Argonne forest abandoned in the retreat and suspected of being a base for Reiner, a German specialist in chemical and biological weapons. They find German soldiers killed by their own side and, behind a sealed door, crazed men who attack them. Dr. Priest dissects one and finds him full of parasitic worms that he concludes have been engineered. German troops sent with Reiner and Captain Mueller to destroy the bunker capture the Allied team. Reiner reveals that the worms, meant to kill Allied livestock, jumped to humans, and he bred deadlier strains to destroy Europe; he tortures Priest to death. Mueller, disgusted, and Berton work together to blow up the bunker; Mueller stays behind to detonate it, Reiner dies in the collapse, and Berton escapes.
