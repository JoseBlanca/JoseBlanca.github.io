The genetics

This is the film in the series where genetics becomes explicit. The whole society of Chicago turns out to be an experiment: the Bureau of Genetic Welfare believes that past genetic modification damaged human nature and is trying to breed people with a restored, "pure" genome, and Tris is its first fully "pure" result. People with damaged genes are treated as inferior, and Tris finally condemns the division between pure and damaged as no better than the factions. The geneticist-administrators of the Bureau are cold technocrats who justify manipulation, child abductions and memory erasure in the name of genetic welfare, and its leader, David, is the villain. The film thus links genetic engineering and eugenic attempts to correct it to a single critique of social control by genetic labels. The notion that traits such as courage or independent thinking can be "repaired" by restoring a pure genome has no basis in genetics.

The story

The third film in the Divergent series. In a walled, post-apocalyptic Chicago, Tris Prior, Four and a few companions escape over the wall into the wasteland. They are rescued and taken to a hidden, technologically advanced city, the headquarters of the Bureau of Genetic Welfare. Its leader, David, explains that humanity's attempts to fix its problems through genetic tampering sparked the Purity Wars, and that the Bureau runs experiments in isolated cities, Chicago among them, to restore a genetically "pure" population. Tris is the first pure individual to emerge. While she works with David, Four discovers that the Bureau kidnaps children from the wasteland and erases their memories. When David tries to use amnesia gas to wipe the memories of Chicago's warring factions, Tris and Four stop the attack, and Tris broadcasts to the world that Chicago was an experiment in genetic purity.
