The genetics

Cloning and bioengineering are part of the film's background rather than its subject. Umbrella, a pharmaceutical company turned biological weapons maker, manufactures clones of Alice and her allies to populate its outbreak simulations, and some of the clones have false memories and family lives, raising questions of identity: Alice becomes a mother to the daughter of one of her copies. The T-virus and the Las Plagas parasite are engineered pathogens that create zombies and superhuman soldiers. Scientists appear only as anonymous employees of a corporation that no longer has any ethical restraint and is controlled by an artificial intelligence. The film treats cloning as an industrial process that produces adult copies on demand, which is pure fantasy: a real clone develops from an embryo and does not inherit memories. Its relevance to genetics is limited; it is primarily an action film built around video-game settings.

The story

After an Umbrella Corporation attack on the ship Arcadia, Alice is captured and wakes in a vast underwater facility in Kamchatka, a former Soviet naval base where Umbrella mass-produces clones and stages simulated outbreaks of the T-virus in replicas of Tokyo, New York, Moscow and suburban Raccoon City to demonstrate its effects to buyers. During a power failure arranged by her old enemy Albert Wesker, she escapes with his agent Ada Wong while a rescue team led by Leon Kennedy breaks in from outside. In the suburban simulation Alice finds Becky, the deaf daughter of one of her own clones, who takes her for her mother. They are pursued by Jill Valentine, brainwashed by Umbrella's computer, the Red Queen, and by clones of Alice's dead allies. Alice frees Jill and escapes with Becky. At the White House, Wesker restores Alice's powers with the T-virus before a final stand against the Red Queen's monsters.
