The genetics

The film's genetics consists of two blood-group tests that work as simple paternity tests. The first, showing that Zora's blood group is incompatible with her parents', reveals that she was conceived by donor insemination; the second, after Sarah's accident, shows that Hal, the donor named in the sperm bank's records, cannot be her biological father. The story uses these revelations to ask what makes a parent, and it favours nurture: Zora gives her mother the credit for who she is, and Hal becomes a father to her through affection rather than biology. The racial mix-up, a white donor for a Black mother who asked for a Black one, is played for comedy. Blood groups follow simple Mendelian inheritance, so they can rule a man out as the father, though they cannot prove that he is; today DNA tests do both.

The story

Zora Mathews, a gifted African American high-school student, discovers from a blood test that her blood group could not have come from her late father and her mother Sarah, who runs an African American bookshop. Sarah admits that she conceived Zora with donor sperm, having asked for a tall Black donor. With her friend Tea Cake, Zora sneaks into the sperm bank's records and finds her donor's name: Hal Jackson, a loud white car dealer known for his outlandish television commercials. After a rough start, Hal grows fond of Zora and falls for Sarah. When Sarah is badly injured in a bicycle accident, a hospital blood test reveals that Hal cannot be Zora's father after all. By then he has become one: at her graduation, Zora, who has won a scholarship to MIT, thanks both Sarah and Hal as her parents.
