The genetics

In this action horror film, the werewolf curse is reimagined as a mutation: moonlight from a supermoon alters the human genome and turns people into werewolves. Science is presented as the response. The hero, Wesley, is a molecular biologist, and a team of scientists working with the military develops a serum, Moonscreen, intended to block the effect of moonlight on the genome; the first trial fails disastrously, but by the end Wesley believes the serum is a success. The scientists are well-meaning researchers working under pressure in an emergency rather than villains. Genetics serves only as a pseudo-scientific frame for the werewolf legend, and the film does not explain how light could reprogram the genome. The idea of light rewriting the genome and transforming the whole body has no basis in biology.

The story

A year after a supermoon turned people exposed to its light into murderous werewolves, the world prepares for the next one. People barricade their homes, the military cages those who were exposed, and scientists search for a way to reverse the mutation. Wesley Marshall, a former Marine turned molecular biologist, lives with his sister-in-law, Lucy, and her daughter, Emma. He joins Dr. James Aranda's team, which has developed "Moonscreen", a serum designed to stop moonlight from affecting the human genome. During a test on the night of the supermoon, the subjects transform anyway, escape and overrun the facility. Wesley and his colleague Amy Chen inject themselves with the serum, which lasts only an hour, and fight their way home through a city full of werewolves; Amy is killed. Wesley reaches the house as werewolves attack, lets himself transform to kill the dangerous neighbour who has become one, and turns back into a human at sunrise, knowing that the serum works.
