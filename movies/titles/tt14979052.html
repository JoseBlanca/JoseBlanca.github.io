The genetics

The series' epidemic has a man-made origin: the Lytta virus outbreak, or mad person disease, comes from Next, a drug made by a pharmaceutical company as a treatment for pneumonia and then promoted for boosting strength and alertness. The sources do not describe genetic engineering as such; the pathogen arises from a pharmaceutical product. The research side is embodied by Han Tae-seok, a military health officer who runs a facility where infected patients are held under observation, keeps the outbreak secret for as long as he can, and is ready to exploit Sae-bom, whose blood carries antibodies that might yield a cure, because he wants to save his infected pregnant wife. The series uses the epidemic to explore class discrimination and whether the infected remain human. A virus caused by a drug, and spread by bites with such symptoms, is fiction.

The story

In a near future where epidemics have become the norm, a drug called Next, sold as a treatment that boosts strength and alertness, has started an outbreak of the Lytta virus, or mad person disease, which spreads through bites and scratches and causes white pupils, insatiable thirst and a craving for human blood. Police special forces officer Yoon Sae-bom is scratched by an infected trainee and taken to a research facility run by Lieutenant Colonel Han Tae-seok, where she appears to be immune. To get an apartment in a new, class-divided building, she enters a contract marriage with her old friend Jung Yi-hyun, a detective. When a resident who secretly bought Next turns, the building is sealed off; Sae-bom and Yi-hyun try to keep order among frightened, selfish neighbours, while Han, whose pregnant wife has been infected, wants to use the antibodies in Sae-bom's blood to find a cure.
