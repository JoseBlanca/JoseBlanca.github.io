The genetics

Genetic engineering is the source of the monsters. The pathogen, developed by Energyne, is described as a tool for rewriting genes on a massive scale, and the film explicitly links it to CRISPR. Scientists appear on both sides: Kate Caldwell joined the research hoping CRISPR could cure diseases, such as her brother's terminal illness, but was jailed after discovering that the company intended to use it as a biological weapon. The company's executives are the villains, ready to put a city at risk to recover their specimens. The film thus contrasts medical gene editing with its corporate and military misuse. CRISPR is a real technique that uses an enzyme guided by RNA to cut DNA at chosen sites, and the first CRISPR-based therapy was approved in 2023 for sickle-cell disease. However, gene editing is limited by off-target effects and the difficulty of delivering it to cells, and nothing like the film's instant growth of whole animals is possible.

The story

A space station owned by the genetic engineering company Energyne is destroyed after a laboratory rat mutates, and three canisters of an experimental pathogen fall to Earth. One is swallowed by a crocodile in the Everglades, another exposes a wolf in Wyoming, and the third lands in the enclosure of George, a rare leucistic gorilla at a San Diego wildlife park, cared for by the primatologist Davis Okoye. All three animals begin to grow enormously and become extremely aggressive. A former Energyne geneticist, Kate Caldwell, explains that the pathogen was designed to rewrite genes on a massive scale. Energyne's CEO, Claire Wyden, lures the animals to Chicago with a radio signal from the top of her company's skyscraper, and they tear through the city. Davis and Kate obtain an antidote that restores George's personality, though not his size, and George helps them defeat the wolf and the crocodile before the military can bomb the city.
