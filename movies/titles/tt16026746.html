The genetics

Mutation is the premise of the series: mutants are people born with powers, and the hostility towards them works as an allegory of prejudice. The explicitly genetic thread is Mister Sinister, Dr. Nathaniel Essex, a Victorian-era London scientist who experiments on mutants and has enhanced himself with their DNA. In season 1 his schemes revolve around cloning and heredity: he creates an adult clone of Jean Grey, manipulates her and Cyclops into having a child, and seizes the baby, Nathan, because he believes the combined genes of two powerful mutants give the boy unlimited potential, planning experiments to make him invulnerable. Sinister is portrayed as wholly evil, a scientist who treats people as breeding stock and raw material. The science is comic-book invention: real cloning produces an embryo, not an adult copy who can take the original's place, and DNA taken from other people cannot confer their abilities on an adult.

The story

Continuing X-Men: The Animated Series (1992–1997), the show opens a year after Professor Charles Xavier was nearly killed in an assassination attempt, with Magneto now leading the X-Men in a world still hostile to mutants. In season 1 (2024), the episode "Fire Made Flesh" reveals that the Jean Grey who has been living with Cyclops and has just given birth to their son, Nathan, is a clone made by Mister Sinister; the real Jean has escaped from his laboratory. Sinister seizes the baby for his experiments. The X-Men rescue him, but he is infected with a techno-organic virus and has to be taken to the future to survive, while the clone breaks free of Sinister's control and takes the name Madelyne Pryor. The rest of the season follows Bastion's campaign against mutants, including a devastating attack on the mutant nation of Genosha, and ends with the X-Men scattered through time. The nine-episode second season, released in July and August 2026, follows their attempt to return from different eras to the 1990s to stop Apocalypse.
