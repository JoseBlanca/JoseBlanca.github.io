The genetics

The whole drama depends on a fictional prenatal genetic test that predicts a fetus's sexual orientation, and it uses this to raise questions about selective abortion, eugenics and what parents may choose about their children. The genetic test is presented as an extension of real prenatal screening for disease, and the scientist involved, Rob, works within the medical system: he is not a villain, but his professional confidence in the test and his rejection of a gay son put the problem at the centre of the family. The play premiered in 1993, the year Dean Hamer's study linking male homosexuality to a region of the X chromosome was dubbed the "gay gene" by the media; later studies failed to replicate that link. A 2019 study of nearly half a million people found that hundreds or thousands of genetic variants are involved, together accounting for only a modest part of the variation in same-sex behaviour, so a predictive test like the one in the film does not exist.

The story

Adapted by Jonathan Tolins from his own stage play, this television film is set in the near future. Suzanne Gold, from a well-off Jewish family, is married to Rob, a doctor working on the human genome project who tests fetuses for diseases and abnormalities. When Suzanne becomes pregnant, Rob's boss persuades them to have the baby tested, and the result indicates a high probability that their son will be gay. Suzanne considers an abortion, which deeply hurts her gay brother David, whose sexuality the family has never fully accepted, and the crisis exposes what her parents, Walter and Phyllis, really feel about him. Unlike the play, in which Suzanne has the abortion and the family is broken, in the film she decides to keep the baby, although this leads to the end of her marriage, since Rob does not want to raise a gay son.
