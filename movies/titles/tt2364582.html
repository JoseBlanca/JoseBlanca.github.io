The genetics

Over seven seasons the series returns repeatedly to biological enhancement. Coulson owes his life to a secret S.H.I.E.L.D. programme that resurrected him with alien blood; Hydra's Project Centipede injects people with Extremis and alien DNA to make super-soldiers, often with lethal results; and Hydra scientists experiment on captured Inhumans. The Inhuman storyline turns on heredity: powers lie dormant in certain bloodlines until Terrigen crystals trigger a metamorphosis, and the series uses the newly powered as a metaphor for prejudice and fear of the other. Against these misuses stand S.H.I.E.L.D.'s own scientists, above all the biochemist Jemma Simmons, who studies alien and Inhuman biology to heal and protect, so the series presents science as a double-edged sword. Its biology belongs to the Marvel universe: alien DNA cannot be injected to make soldiers, and no crystal activates hidden genes.

The story

Agent Phil Coulson, secretly brought back to life after his death in The Avengers by a S.H.I.E.L.D. project using alien Kree blood, assembles a small team of agents, among them the scientists Leo Fitz and Jemma Simmons and the hacker Skye, to investigate strange cases. They uncover Project Centipede, which creates super-soldiers by combining the Extremis substance with alien DNA, and discover that Hydra has infiltrated S.H.I.E.L.D. Coulson rebuilds the agency as its Director. Later seasons introduce the Inhumans, people whose latent hereditary powers are unlocked by Terrigen crystals; Skye learns she is the Inhuman Daisy Johnson and leads the Secret Warriors against the ancient Inhuman Hive. Subsequent arcs involve android Life Model Decoys and a virtual world, a dystopian future in 2091, the alien Chronicoms and time travel, ending in the team's victory over the Chronicoms.
