The genetics

Human cloning is the core of this television film, based on Mildred Ames's 1981 novel. A dead scientist's genome is reproduced in several girls who are placed with chosen families, each a scientist married to a musician, to recreate the conditions of her childhood and see whether she will turn into the same person. The story is therefore an experiment in nature versus nurture: Anna discovers she is expected to become Zimmerman, but chooses to be herself. The scientists who continue the project see the girls as experimental material and are prepared to kill the ones who do not turn out as planned, and the backstory links the research to Nazi plans for human genetic engineering. The film shares the 1970s and 1980s fascination with cloning seen in The Boys from Brazil. Real clones are genetically identical but develop differently, and inherited memories, as suggested by Anna's dreams, have no basis in biology.

The story

Anna Hart, a twelve-year-old genius at a school for gifted children in New Jersey, is also a kleptomaniac who suffers headaches and strange prophetic dreams. When she sees her exact double, Anna Smithson, on the television news, she starts investigating and discovers that she is one of several clones of Anna Zimmerman, a scientist who died twenty years earlier. Her dreams turn out to be Zimmerman's memories of growing up as a Jewish child prodigy in Nazi Germany. Taken to a facility on Albacore Island for "re-evaluation", Anna finds other Annas kept there and learns that the project's director, Dr. Jelliff, plans to eliminate the failed clones while grooming another girl to become the new Zimmerman. Her neighbour and piano teacher, Michaela, reveals herself as Anna Parkhurst, the first clone, and forces Jelliff to spare all the Annas in exchange for the plans of Zimmerman's "replicator".
