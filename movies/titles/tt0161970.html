The genetics

The killer sharks are the product of a scientist's experiments. Dr. Miles Craven has modified sharks to enlarge their brains, which also makes them more aggressive, so that he can harvest a serum for a miracle cure; reviewers describe the sharks as genetically modified, although one summary speaks of hormones. The serum kills the patients who receive it, and Craven carries on regardless. He is Steven's former colleague, an unethical scientist who becomes the accomplice of a crooked developer and is killed by him, although the film gives him a final sympathetic moment as he claims he is not such a bad man. The plot closely resembles that of Deep Blue Sea, released the same year, in which sharks' brains are also enlarged for a cure. The science is not explained and the premise is not realistic: shark brain tissue is not a source of cures.

The story

In the South African fishing town of Port Amanzi, marine biologist Mark Desantis is thrown to a shark by corrupt police before he can finish an email to his friend, the biologist Steven McKray, about an unusual number of shark attacks. Steven flies out to investigate with Mark's sister Corinne. They meet the hotel owner and developer Lawrence Rhodes and Steven's former colleague Dr. Miles Craven. Dissecting a captured shark, they find that its brain has been enlarged, and they discover that Craven is altering sharks to harvest a serum for a miracle cure, which is killing his patients. Rhodes, who wants to buy up local land cheaply because oil has been found offshore, is using the attacks to drive people away, and the two men arranged Mark's murder. Steven steals Craven's data, and in a final shootout Rhodes kills Craven, flees by helicopter and is eaten by a shark after it crashes.
