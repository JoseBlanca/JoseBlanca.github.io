The genetics

Genetic enhancement of embryos is the subject of the episode. It imagines a society where parents feel pressure to buy genetic upgrades for their children on the black market, despite the procedure being illegal and risky, and it uses the hidden, disfigured son next door to show the price of failure. The story is a cautionary tale about "designer babies", parental ambition and the gap between the promise of a perfect child and the reality of unpredictable biological risk; the scientists who sell enhancements stay in the background as an illicit industry. The invented "genetic rejection syndrome" has no real counterpart, but the episode anticipates later debates: germline editing of embryos is prohibited or tightly restricted in many countries, in part because changes would be passed on to future generations.

The story

Howard and Joanne Sharp are expecting their first child and live in a world where genetic enhancement of unborn babies is illegal but widely bought on the black market. The procedure promises a "perfect" child, but carries a small risk, about 1 in 10,000, of genetic rejection syndrome, which turns the child into a monster. The couple weigh whether to give their baby the advantages of enhancement. They then discover that their neighbours' son, said to have died years earlier, is alive and hidden in the basement, transformed by the syndrome. Horrified, Howard and Joanne try to reverse the enhancement they have already begun on their unborn child, and the episode asks whether they have acted in time.
