The genetics

Cloning serves only as the comic device that justifies the return of Paolo Villaggio's character, who had died in the previous film of the series: the company brings him back through biotechnology because it needs a perfectly docile employee. The idea satirises corporate power, with managers who would rather recreate an obedient worker than deal with assertive young staff, but the film shows no scientists or laboratory and does not explore cloning or genetics further; after the opening, the episodes concern lotteries, family life and class humiliation. In real life a clone would be born as a baby and would share the original's genome, not his memories or personality, so the revival of an adult Fantozzi is pure fantasy. The genetics content of the film is minimal, limited to its title and premise.

The story

On the eve of the third millennium, the Megaditta faces a crisis because its new employees no longer show proper subservience, so its executives use advanced biotechnology to bring back the most servile employee of all time, the late accountant Ugo Fantozzi. Revived, he is given the task of looking after the youngest son of the despotic director Balabam, who turns out to be the family's black sheep because he is kind and generous; Fantozzi escapes with him and other boys from a brutal boarding school that teaches abuse of power, and the boy is later flogged by his father. Fantozzi then plays the SuperEnalotto lottery with former colleagues; his wife Pina secretly fails to submit the ticket that would have won, and Fantozzi, believing himself rich, rents a castle and lives beyond his means with Signorina Silvani until his creditors arrive. Further misadventures follow with his granddaughter Uga, and at a countess's birthday party on New Year's Eve 2000.
