The genetics

This Futurama episode, a parody of Jack and the Beanstalk, is a satire of the debate on genetic engineering. Leela suffers from a genetic condition, and the only one able to cure her is Mom, who modifies plants and animals in a laboratory in the sky. Leela, who opposes genetic engineering on principle, is forced to weigh her convictions against her own need: the episode mocks both the profit-driven industry and the purity of its critics, as Mom's work produces both monsters and real benefits such as a crop that can feed the hungry and a cure for hereditary gigantism. The giant beanstalks recall the real controversy over genetically modified crops, and the idea of gene therapy for inherited disease is grounded in real medicine. Squidification itself is a joke, but the episode's central question, whether to accept a technology one distrusts when it offers a cure, is a familiar one in bioethics.

The story

Leela breaks out in suction cups, and Dr. Tenderman explains that a genetic condition is causing "squidification": her limbs will gradually turn into a mass of tentacles, and only an expensive operation can delay it. Fry sells the Planet Express ship to pay for it, but comes back with two magic beans sold as a miracle cure. Thrown out of the window, the beans grow into a giant beanstalk. Leela climbs it and finds a flying castle where Mom runs a genetic engineering facility, creating giant beanstalks to feed the hungry along with flying fish and a unicorn. Leela condemns the experiments as crimes. Fry and Bender come to rescue her, by now fully transformed into tentacles, and free a giant held in the lab. Mom later reveals that Leela's suction cups helped her engineer the perfect bean and that she has cured the giant of hereditary gigantism, and she offers to cure Leela, who accepts.
