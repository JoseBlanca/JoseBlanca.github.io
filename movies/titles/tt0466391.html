The genetics

The film's premise is a virus engineered to modify human DNA. The geneticist Peter Erfan creates it as a way of enhancing people, but it is used as a weapon and kills an entire special forces unit; only Jane survives, because something in her body changes the virus, which then gives her healing and other enhanced abilities. The scientist is a villain who conceals his identity, uses the military and plans to sell the enhancement to foreign business partners to build an army of superhumans, so genetic modification is shown as a weapon and a commodity rather than a medical advance. Using viruses to carry genes into human cells is a real technique in gene therapy, where modified viruses act as "vectors". In reality, however, they deliver genes to some cells to treat specific diseases, and the risks include immune reactions; nothing like the instant, body-wide transformation in the film is possible.

The story

During a mission in Chechnya, a special forces unit is exposed to a biochemical weapon and then executed by men in hazmat suits. Captain Jane Browning survives both the execution and the infection, and develops rapid healing, increased speed and strength, heightened senses and a photographic memory. Questioned by a psychiatrist, Dr. Graham Knight, and tested by the military, she escapes with the help of a thief, Nick Pierce, when her superior, Colonel Ian Watts, plans to move her to Alaska. Searching for answers, she learns that the virus she was exposed to was designed to alter people's DNA and enhance them, and that its creator, Peter Erfan, is the man who posed as Dr. Knight. Erfan calls her the "new Eve": her body altered the virus, which in turn changed her. He kidnaps her to use her for his project, but she escapes. In the final scene Erfan, who has apparently injected himself, meets Asian business partners to create an army of superhumans.
