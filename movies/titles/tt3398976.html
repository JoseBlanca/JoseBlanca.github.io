The genetics

Genetics is part of the setting rather than the main plot, which centres on combat against the Gauna. Sidonia's society treats human cloning, asexual reproduction and human genetic engineering as ordinary: its people have been modified to photosynthesise, and the ruling elite has secretly been granted immortality. The Encyclopedia of Science Fiction singles out the series' exploration of genetic engineering, immortality and gender, comparing characters whose sex changes to suit their partners with Ursula K. Le Guin's The Left Hand of Darkness. The source does not describe a scientist or a programme behind these technologies; they are presented as part of the world, with the elite's secret immortality hinting at inequality. Photosynthetic humans remain fiction; animals cannot simply acquire a plant's photosynthetic machinery.

The story

In 3394, a thousand years after shapeshifting aliens called the Gauna destroyed Earth, humanity survives aboard huge generation ships built from the planet's remains. The Sidonia, with more than 500,000 inhabitants, may be the last human settlement. Its Japanese-style society is defended by Gardes, giant mechanised weapons armed with beam cannons and Kabizashi spears whose rare tips can destroy a Gauna's core. Nagate Tanikaze has lived since birth in the ship's underground with his grandfather, training every day on an old pilot simulator. After his grandfather dies he emerges to the surface and is selected as a Garde pilot just as the Gauna threaten Sidonia again, and the series follows his battles to defend the ship.
