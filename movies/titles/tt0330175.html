The genetics

The film is a cautionary drama about genetically modified crops. A biotechnology company tests a new corn variety in an ordinary farming community; the crop spreads an unexpected weed, the sheep that eat it become aggressive and addicted to it, and the effects appear to pass through the food chain to the people who eat the meat, causing birth defects and deaths. The scientists behind the corn appear only occasionally and grow worried as the problems emerge, while the company is portrayed as a powerful interest that local people are reluctant to challenge. Sources differ on the details of the modification: one synopsis describes a corn engineered to resist pesticides and grow with little water, while a review mentions a gene from a cold-water fish. The film was made on a very small budget and reviewers found its treatment of the theme thin. The harms it depicts are fictional: there is a scientific consensus that food from currently available GM crops poses no greater risk to health than conventional food, although each new crop must be assessed case by case.

The story

Emily Rasmussen, a young woman who is pregnant and has dropped out of college, returns to the rural sheep ranch of her family to rebuild her life; the father of her child, a politician, refuses to help her for fear of scandal. She notices that the sheep are behaving strangely: they fight viciously, and even injure themselves on the fence wire, to reach a weed that grows from the neighbouring farm's corn. The neighbour has planted a new genetically engineered corn supplied by a large company. After a lamb is born with only two legs and the newborn baby of a friend who regularly eats lamb dies, Emily investigates and discovers that the corn is an experiment in genetic engineering. She tries to warn people about the contaminated meat, but few listen, and she ends up confronting the company testing the corn, her employers and her own family.
