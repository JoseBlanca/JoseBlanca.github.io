The genetics

De-extinction through cloning is the premise, but the film's focus is on what it would be like to be the only Neanderthal alive. The scientists embody two attitudes: Reed is a charismatic boundary-pusher who wants to vindicate Neanderthals, while Barbara, who becomes William's mother, increasingly sees him as a son rather than a subject, and their split dramatises the conflict between research goals and a child's welfare. Faculty and ethicists object to the project, raising questions about consent, identity and loneliness that the film poses more than it explores. The idea has a real-world basis: a draft of the Neanderthal genome was published in 2010. Cloning a Neanderthal, however, is not feasible: no living Neanderthal cells exist, and ancient DNA survives only in short, damaged fragments. Svante Pääbo, who led the genome project, has said that starting from fossil DNA it "is and will remain impossible".

The story

Dr. Julian Reed, a paleontologist eager to overturn the image of Neanderthals as primitive brutes, and Dr. Barbara Sullivan, a physician, secretly carry out the "Neanderthal Project" using DNA from a Neanderthal specimen nicknamed William. Barbara carries the cloned embryo and gives birth to a boy, whom they name William and raise as their son. Their disagreement over whether to raise him as an ordinary child or as a research subject drives the couple apart. William grows up large, strong and reserved, subjected throughout his life to aptitude and IQ tests; he is bullied, struggles with abstract ideas such as metaphor, and is rejected by a girl for his appearance, but he also makes friends and joins the school musical. As a young man, he faces the hostility of the world around him and the knowledge that his kind was wiped out by the species that has now brought him back.
