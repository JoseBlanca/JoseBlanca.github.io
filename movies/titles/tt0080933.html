The genetics

Genetics is a passing suggestion. The only available review notes that the film offers two competing explanations for its killer crabs: radiation from the nuclear plant's spill, which is shown in background scenes, and hints of "genetic tinkering" at the marine laboratory where Dr. McNeal studies crabs. The source does not say which explanation the film favours or what, if anything, the researchers did, and it says nothing about how the scientists are portrayed. The film is a late example of the 1950s giant-creature cycle (Them!, Attack of the Crab Monsters), with the laboratory added as a modern alternative to the atomic origin.

The story

Photojournalist Jan Raines comes to the Florida Keys to write about crab research at the National Marine Biology Institute, led by Dr. John McNeal with his assistant Pete Adams. Pete, an orphan raised by the bar owner Moody, asks her out, but Moody distrusts her: her father, Frank Raines, owns the nearby nuclear plant that has just had a major spill, and was responsible years before for the drunk-driving death of Moody's parents. Crabs across the island start attacking and killing people, and many locals blame a boatload of Haitian refugees. Then a crab the size of a truck appears and tears apart the house where Pete and Jan are hiding. In the climax the survivors, Moody among them, manage to inject the giant crab with a formula that kills it.
