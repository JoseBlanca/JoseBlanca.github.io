The genetics

Genetics underpins the film. Replicants are bioengineered humans made by a corporation, and the story turns on the discovery that two of them conceived a child naturally, which would end the line between manufactured and born beings. Niander Wallace, the industrialist who builds replicants, is a ruthless figure who wants to unlock replicant reproduction to breed slave labour on an industrial scale. DNA records are a key investigative tool: K searches DNA archives, and the birth records have been falsified so that two children appear to share identical DNA despite being of different sexes, a clue that something has been tampered with, since identical DNA would imply the same sex chromosomes. The film is less interested in the science of making replicants than in what it means to be born rather than made. As in the 1982 original, replicants are engineered as whole adults, which is beyond any real biotechnology.

The story

In 2049 Los Angeles, bioengineered humans called replicants are still used as slave labour. K, a replicant blade runner for the police, kills a rogue replicant and finds the remains of a female replicant who died in childbirth, proof that replicants can reproduce, which was thought impossible. His superior orders him to find and kill the child to prevent a war. At the Wallace Corporation, the replicant manufacturer, DNA archives identify the dead woman as Rachael, who had a relationship with the former blade runner Rick Deckard; Niander Wallace wants the secret of replicant reproduction to supply slave labour for colonising space. K finds records of two children born on the same day with identical DNA but different sexes, and comes to believe he is Rachael's son. He finds Deckard, but learns from the replicant resistance that the child was a girl. Realising that she is the memory designer Dr. Ana Stelline, K rescues Deckard from Wallace and takes him to meet his daughter before dying.
