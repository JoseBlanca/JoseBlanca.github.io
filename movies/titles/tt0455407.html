The genetics

As in George A. Romero's 1973 original, which he executive-produced, the threat is a man-made bioweapon: 'Trixie', a prototype virus from the Rhabdoviridae family (the rabies family), escapes when the military plane carrying it for destruction crashes into the town's water supply, and after an incubation period turns people into calculating killers. The government that created it never appears as a scientist; it appears as the soldiers and officials who conceal the weapon, quarantine and execute both infected and healthy residents, and finally destroy the town, so the film's critique is aimed at the state that builds such weapons and the ruthlessness of its containment. The makeup of the infected was based on rabies, tetanus and Stevens-Johnson syndrome. No genetic detail of the virus is given.

The story

In Ogden Marsh, Iowa, Sheriff David Dutten is forced to shoot a resident who walks onto a baseball field with a shotgun, and soon another man burns his wife and son alive in their farmhouse. David and his deputy Russell find a crashed military plane in a swamp and suspect the water supply, which David shuts off against the mayor's wishes. Communications are cut and the military quarantines the town; David's pregnant wife, Judy, the local doctor, is wrongly flagged as infected because of a fever. David and Russell rescue her and her assistant Becca as the troops abandon the infected and execute civilians. Becca is killed, and a captured government employee reveals that the plane carried 'Trixie', a prototype biological weapon on its way to be destroyed. Russell, himself infected, sacrifices himself at a roadblock. David and Judy escape as the military destroys the town with a huge explosion, but a satellite marks them, and nearby Cedar Rapids, for 'containment'.
