The genetics

Genetics is the hidden mechanism behind the film's apparent miracle. Father Tedeschi, a geneticist turned priest, has been trying to create a clone or descendant of Jesus from DNA recovered from a relic, using nuns as unwilling surrogates. He is the religious version of the mad scientist, convinced of his mission and indifferent to the women he uses, with the backing of the convent's hierarchy. The film uses genetics to recast the Annunciation as reproductive coercion, linking the misuse of science to the control of women's bodies, and its final act turns on Cecilia's refusal to bear a child forced on her. The premise is far beyond real science: any DNA on a 2,000-year-old object would be degraded, fragmentary and almost certainly contaminated by later handling, and no technique could use such material to create a viable embryo.

The story

Sister Cecilia, a young novice who believes God saved her life when she survived drowning as a child, is invited by Father Sal Tedeschi to join a convent in the Italian countryside that cares for dying nuns. Soon after taking her vows, she discovers that she is pregnant despite being a virgin, and the convent begins to venerate her as a new Virgin Mary. Her health deteriorates, a jealous nun tries to drown her, and her friend Sister Gwen, who protests, is mutilated and killed. Searching the convent at night, Cecilia finds her own file, which includes her karyotype. Tedeschi reveals that he was a geneticist before he became a priest, and that he has been using DNA taken from the convent's relic, a nail said to be from Christ's crucifixion, to impregnate nuns in the hope of bringing about a new messiah; earlier attempts produced malformed foetuses. Cecilia kills her captors, escapes and, after giving birth, destroys the baby.
