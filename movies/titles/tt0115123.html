The genetics

Genetics enters the series mainly through its subject, lethal epidemics and bioterrorism, and through one of its leads, Dr. Kimberly Shiroma, a specialist in molecular genetics and pathology who is presented as highly competent. The team fights natural and man-made pathogens and the conspiracies of a shadowy organisation called The Dawn. The available sources describe the premise rather than individual episodes, so the details of any engineered agents, genetic tests or experiments are not known; critics dubbed it a 'mutant-disease-of-the-week series'. The government scientists are the heroes, but their science shares the stage with religion: early episodes resolved hard-science plots through prayer and the power of a 'healthy soul'. Shiroma left the series after eleven episodes.

The story

This American science fiction series, which ran for one season of 19 episodes on UPN in 1996–1997, is set during the 'Plague Wars', a global rise in lethal diseases. A US government task force, the Office of Biocrisis Management, investigates chemical and biological threats, including natural, criminal and extraterrestrial epidemics and bioterrorist attacks. The original team includes the virologist Dr. Edward Marcase, who survived Ebola as a child and treats disease as a mystical quest; the molecular geneticist Dr. Kimberly Shiroma, formerly of the World Health Organization, who blames Marcase for the death of her fiancé; the security expert Michael Hailey; and the team's leader Dr. Daniel Cassian. Early episodes mixed hard science with spiritual resolutions. After eleven episodes Marcase and Shiroma were dropped, Cassian became the lead, and the rebellious physician Dr. Brian Taft joined a more action-oriented show.
