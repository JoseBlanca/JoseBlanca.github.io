The genetics

Despite its title, this rock opera is more about biotechnology and transplant medicine as business than about genetics in the strict sense. GeneCo turns the body into merchandise: organs and surgical enhancements are sold on credit, and debtors are killed to recover them, a satire of commercial medicine, debt and cosmetic surgery addiction. The corporation's scientists and surgeons serve power and profit, and its founder uses medicine to manipulate and kill. The genetic element lies mainly in Shilo's story: she believes she has inherited her mother's blood disease and is defined by it, until she discovers that her symptoms were caused by her father's "medicine". Her final song, "Genetic Emancipation", celebrates her decision that her parents' actions and her inheritance do not dictate her future, a rejection of genetic and family determinism.

The story

In 2056, after an epidemic of organ failures has killed most of humanity, the megacorporation GeneCo sells organ transplants on credit, and Repo Men hunt down clients who miss payments to "repossess" their organs. GeneCo's terminally ill founder, Rotti Largo, despises his three spoiled children and plans to leave his fortune to 17-year-old Shilo Wallace, daughter of his late former fiancée Marni. Shilo has supposedly inherited her mother's rare blood disease and is kept indoors by her father Nathan, who believes he killed Marni with a treatment he created; in fact Rotti poisoned it and blackmailed Nathan into becoming GeneCo's chief Repo Man. Shilo meets a grave robber who sells an addictive painkiller extracted from corpses, and Blind Mag, a singer whose GeneCo eyes will be repossessed when she resigns. At the opera, Mag gouges out her own eyes and is killed, Rotti reveals that Nathan has been poisoning Shilo's medicine to keep her at home, and shoots him. Shilo refuses the company and leaves.
