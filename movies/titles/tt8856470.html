The genetics

Cloning is the theme of the film, a computer-animated remake of the 1998 original. Mewtwo is created from Mew's DNA, recovered from an eyelash, by a scientist working for a criminal organisation that wants a weapon, and the film follows his anger at being treated as an experiment and his question of whether a clone is inferior to the original. His clone army of Pokémon, copied from the trainers' own, faces the originals in a pointless battle in which neither side can win. The film's message is that origins do not determine worth: what counts is how one chooses to live. The scientists are ambitious and serve a criminal patron, and they pay for their hubris with their lives. Cloning from an eyelash would require cells with intact nuclei, and a clone would be genetically identical to the original, not stronger, so Mewtwo's superior powers are an engineering fantasy.

The story

This computer-animated remake of Pokémon: The First Movie (1998) begins when Giovanni, leader of Team Rocket, hires the scientist Dr. Fuji to create a living weapon by cloning the legendary Pokémon Mew from an eyelash. The clone, Mewtwo, gains consciousness and, enraged that the scientists see him only as an experiment, destroys the laboratory with his psychic powers, killing Fuji. After learning that Giovanni wants to use him as a weapon, Mewtwo escapes and plots revenge on humanity. He invites the world's best trainers, including Ash, Misty and Brock, to New Island, defeats their Pokémon with clones and copies them to build a clone army. Mew appears and the originals fight their clones. When Ash is turned to stone stepping between Mewtwo and Mew, the tears of the Pokémon revive him. Moved, Mewtwo accepts that one is judged by one's choices, not one's origins, and erases everyone's memories.
