The genetics

Genetics has a minor place in this monster film, whose central creation is a machine rather than an engineered organism. Apex Cybernetics, a technology company, builds Mechagodzilla, a robot controlled through the neural networks salvaged from the severed head of King Ghidorah, and its secret laboratories are also breeding Skullcrawlers, reptilian monsters of the franchise, as one review notes. The attempt to turn Titan biology into weapons ends in disaster when Ghidorah's consciousness takes over the robot. Apex's executives embody the corporate scientist who serves ambition and profit, while the Monarch scientists who study and protect Kong, such as Ilene Andrews, are shown sympathetically. The film raises no scientific questions about heredity or genetic engineering, and its Titans and their biology are pure fantasy; its interest for this collection lies in its portrayal of a company trying to create and control monsters.

The story

Five years after Godzilla defeated King Ghidorah, Kong is held by the agency Monarch in a dome on Skull Island, watched over by the scientist Ilene Andrews and Jia, a deaf orphaned girl who communicates with him in sign language. When Godzilla attacks a facility of the technology company Apex Cybernetics, a conspiracy podcaster and two teenagers start investigating. Apex's chief executive, Walter Simmons, recruits Nathan Lind, a former Monarch scientist and Hollow Earth theorist, to lead an expedition, guided by Kong, into the Hollow Earth, the Titans' original home, to find a power source. In fact Apex wants the energy for Mechagodzilla, a giant robot controlled through the neural networks of Ghidorah's severed head, which it is building in Hong Kong. Godzilla, drawn by the machine, fights Kong in Hong Kong. When Mechagodzilla is activated, Ghidorah's consciousness takes it over and it kills Simmons; Godzilla and Kong join forces to destroy it.
