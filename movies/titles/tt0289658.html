The genetics

Human reproductive cloning is the subject of the film, approached through its social reception rather than its science. The story imagines the announcement of the first cloned human baby and focuses on the media frenzy, public outrage and professional backlash that follow, and on the damage they do to the doctor who performed the procedure and to the child. The scientist is a physician presented as the target of controversy rather than as a villain. Made in 2001, a few years after the cloning of Dolly the sheep, when claims of imminent human cloning were in the news, it reflects the debate of its time. Monkeys were first cloned by the Dolly method in 2018, but human reproductive cloning is prohibited in many countries, including Australia and Canada. The story

Also known as After Amy, this television film follows Linda Sinclair, an ambitious reporter who gets the scoop on the birth of the first human clone. As the story breaks, a media and public furore develops around the pregnancy and the unborn child, and Dr. Amanda Gordon, the physician responsible for the cloning, sees her reputation and career put at risk. The film follows the consequences of the coverage for everyone involved, including the baby, and the reporter's growing ethical doubts about her role. It is based on "Carbon Copy: Meet the First Human Clone", a 1998 short story by Richard Kadrey published in Wired magazine.
