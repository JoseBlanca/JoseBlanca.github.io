The genetics

Genetic engineering underpins the film's world but is never explained on screen. The Kildren are humans designed not to age, created to serve as disposable pilots in a staged war, and the film suggests that when they die they are replaced by new individuals carrying traces of their predecessors' memories and manner. Their arrested development is the core of the story: they live in an endless adolescence with no past and no future, unable to grow up, and the film treats their condition as a metaphor for repetition and stagnation. The engineers and corporations behind them remain invisible, which makes the system seem impersonal and inescapable. Scholarly readings have discussed the film's depiction of engineered adolescence and endless war as a critique of a society that sustains conflict as a commodity. Arrested ageing has no real genetic equivalent in humans, although researchers study the genetics of ageing in model organisms.

The story

Mamoru Oshii's animated film, based on Hiroshi Mori's novels, is set in an alternate world at peace, where two private corporations, Rostock and Lautern, stage a real air war to give the public a sense of conflict. Their pilots are the Kildren, genetically engineered humans who remain adolescents and never age. Pilot Yūichi Kannami arrives at Area 262 to replace a pilot who has died, and grows close to the base commander, Suito Kusanagi, who is also a Kildren but has a daughter who passes as her younger sister. Kannami begins to sense that he has no real past, that new pilots resemble the dead, and that his memories may belong to his predecessor. Kusanagi admits that she killed that predecessor at his own request. Kannami dies challenging the Teacher, an invincible enemy ace and an adult, and a new pilot with Kannami's mannerisms arrives, continuing the cycle.
