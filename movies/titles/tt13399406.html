The genetics

Genetic engineering creates the monsters and drives the plot. The Triassic Corporation, which develops biological weapons, has genetically modified dinosaurs to be intelligent, nearly invulnerable predators, and its CEO stages their escape so that their killing of mercenaries can serve as a sales demonstration for buyers who want them as super-soldiers. Its scientists are no better: Dr. Kelso is willing to murder colleagues to protect the dinosaurs' egg and get credit for her research. The film, a low-budget sequel to Triassic World from The Asylum, offers a crude critique of corporations that commercialise genetic engineering for military ends, and reviewers compare it to an economy-budget Jurassic Park. The story

Jordan Friedman, CEO of the Triassic Corporation, unveils his latest products: genetically modified predatory dinosaurs, highly intelligent and nearly invulnerable. Two Allosaurs being moved from a research centre to Los Angeles escape and hide in an industrial zone, killing a guard. Mercenary leader Luis, with Friedman's employees Dr. Elaine Kelso and Simone, sends his team into a warehouse to subdue the animals with tranquilliser darts before they reach a residential area, but the dinosaurs start hunting the hunters. The escape was no accident: Friedman released them deliberately and films the fight as a demonstration for buyers at an online auction of the creatures as super-soldiers. The mercenaries die one by one. The Allosaurs have laid an egg, and Kelso, determined to present her research, shoots Luis and Simone to protect it, before she is eaten. Joy lures the last Allosaur into an explosive trap, and Friedman meets his comeuppance.
