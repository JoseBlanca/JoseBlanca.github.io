The genetics

Genetics is central to the second half of the film. Exposure to the alien fluid rewrites Wikus's body, and MNU's laboratory finds that his partly alien DNA allows him to use alien weapons, which only respond to alien biology. MNU has long been carrying out vivisections and other brutal experiments on the aliens in search of this capability, and its executives treat Wikus as a source of genetic material to be harvested. The scientists are portrayed as instruments of a corporate weapons programme, indifferent to the suffering of their subjects, human or alien, in a story that uses the aliens' treatment to evoke apartheid-era forced removals in South Africa, such as those of Cape Town's District Six. The biology is not meant to be plausible: there is no reason to expect life from another world to share our genetic code, and no fluid could rebuild an adult's whole body. The film's interest lies in its picture of biological research serving discrimination and profit.

The story

In 1982, a huge spaceship stops over Johannesburg, carrying a population of starving insect-like aliens. They are confined to a camp, District 9, which becomes a slum, and humans despise them as "prawns". Twenty-eight years later, the private military company Multinational United (MNU) is hired to evict them to a new camp, and the operation is led by Wikus van de Merwe, a mild bureaucrat. In an alien shack, Wikus is sprayed by a fluid that the alien Christopher Johnson had spent twenty years synthesising as fuel for their ship, and he begins to turn into an alien. MNU scientists discover that his hybrid DNA lets him operate the aliens' biologically locked weapons, and his own father-in-law orders his body harvested. Wikus escapes and allies with Christopher, who reaches the mothership with his son and promises to return to cure him. Wikus, now fully transformed, stays behind.
