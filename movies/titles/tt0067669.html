The genetics

The film is one of the earliest to show cloning used to supply spare body parts, even though the word "clone" is never spoken. The "somas" (Greek for "body") are mindless copies of the senator grown from his DNA and kept at a secret facility so that their organs can be transplanted without rejection. The doctors who run the programme are not mad scientists but employees of a powerful Committee that uses the technology as a means of blackmail, offering survival to influential patients in return for political obedience; the film treats medicine as "a new type of money". In reality a clone would have to develop from an embryo and grow for years, so ready-made adult copies are fiction, but the premise anticipates a real concern: the harvesting of organs and tissues from cloned humans is one of the abuses most often cited by opponents of reproductive cloning.

The story

Reporter Harry Walsh witnesses a car crash and recognises the badly injured survivor as Senator Zachary Wheeler, a likely presidential candidate. When the hospital denies that Wheeler was ever admitted and his editor fires him for refusing to retract the story, Walsh follows the trail to New Mexico. Wheeler, meanwhile, wakes up in a secret clinic run by Dr. Redding and Dr. Layle Johnson, where he has been rebuilt with organs taken from "somas", bodies grown artificially from his own DNA. The price of his recovery is political: a shadowy Committee expects him to serve its interests as senator and threatens to reveal his transplant if he refuses. Walsh reaches the base at Los Alamos, finds a spare Wheeler soma and, mistaking it for the senator, escapes with it. Eventually Walsh and Wheeler meet and agree to expose the Committee, while its members look forward to their next "client".
