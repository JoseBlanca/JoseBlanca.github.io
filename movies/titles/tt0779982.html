The genetics

Genetic engineering is the source of the horror in this comedy, which plays on New Zealand's sheep-farming identity. Angus, the farmer, wants to breed the perfect sheep and present it to investors, while the scientist Dr. Rush carries out the work in a farm laboratory; both are driven by ambition and commercial interest, and their creation goes disastrously wrong. The environmental activists, who want to expose the experiments, unwittingly trigger the outbreak, so the film satirises both biotech enthusiasm and protest culture. Genetics behaves here like a virus: a bite transmits the mutation and turns humans into sheep-hybrids, and the cure is amniotic fluid. None of this resembles real genetic modification: engineered changes are inherited by offspring but cannot be passed on by a bite, and the film makes no attempt at plausibility, using the premise for gore and satire.

The story

Henry Oldfield, who grew up on a New Zealand sheep farm, developed a crippling fear of sheep after his older brother Angus played a cruel prank on him. Fifteen years later, he returns to sell his share of the farm to Angus, unaware that Angus has been running secret genetic experiments on the flock with the scientist Dr. Astrid Rush. Two environmental activists break into the lab and accidentally release a mutant lamb, which bites one of them and then other sheep. The bite spreads quickly: the docile flock becomes a horde of carnivorous killers, and bitten humans begin to turn into monstrous human-sheep hybrids. Angus presses on with a presentation of his new breed to international investors, who are devoured. Henry, the activist Experience and their friend Tucker discover that amniotic fluid from the mutant lambs reverses the transformation, destroy the flock and save the bitten humans, while Angus is eaten by his own sheep.
