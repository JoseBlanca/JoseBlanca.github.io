The genetics

The film, Takashi Miike's adaptation of the manga by Yū Sasuga and Kenichi Tachibana, combines two ideas linked to genetics. First, the cockroaches released on Mars have evolved, in a few centuries, into giant humanoids, a story of accelerated evolution far beyond anything real natural selection could produce. Second, the crew are given injections of insect DNA that let them transform into human-insect hybrids with the abilities of particular species, all of them real insects whose traits, such as the bombardier beetle's chemical spray, are exaggerated for battle. The scientists and officials who planned the mission treat the crew, criminals and outcasts, as expendable test subjects, and the director turns out to have secret aims of his own. Scientifically the idea of gaining another species' traits by injection is fantasy: introducing genes into an adult would not reshape the body in seconds, and insect anatomy does not scale up to human size.

The story

To prepare Mars for colonisation, humans seeded its surface with moss in the twenty-first century and released cockroaches to spread it. In 2599, a crew of misfits and criminals, promised pardons, is sent aboard the spaceship BUGS 2 to exterminate the cockroaches. On arrival they find that the insects have evolved into human-sized, intelligent, humanoid creatures of enormous strength. To fight them, the crew members inject themselves with DNA that gives them the abilities of different insects, from a bullet ant to an Asian giant hornet, a desert locust or a silk moth. One by one they are killed, while further doses make their transformations stronger, and a third dose becomes irreversible. The survivors discover that the mission director, Ko Honda, is communicating with the cockroaches and has his own plans. In the end, only two crew members escape Mars, while on Earth Honda kills a colleague who tries to shoot him.
