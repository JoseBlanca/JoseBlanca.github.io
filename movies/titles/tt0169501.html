The genetics

Ultraviolet treats vampirism as a scientific rather than supernatural phenomenon, and much of its drama turns on biology. The Code Fives run laboratory programmes of their own: they impregnate a woman with genetically crossbred human-vampire sperm, experiment on humans in the search for synthetic blood, and plan to exterminate humanity with an engineered plague, after which they could live on artificial blood and reproduce by live birth. Section V's scientist, Dr. Angela Marsh, studies the Code Fives in turn, and the unit's leader is tempted by their regenerative secrets as he faces terminal cancer. The series pointedly questions both sides: the hunters' methods are brutal, the Church sanctions the abortion of a hybrid foetus, and the vampires argue persuasively for themselves. Critics praised it as one of the most original modern treatments of the vampire theme.

The story

In this six-part British series, Detective Sergeant Michael Colefield investigates the disappearance of his best friend and police partner, Jack Beresford, on the eve of Jack's wedding, and finds that Jack has become a 'Code Five', the series' term for a vampire, a word it never uses. The search leads Michael to Section V, a secret paramilitary unit backed by the British government and the Vatican, led by the priest Pearse Harman and including the haematologist Dr. Angela Marsh and the soldier Vaughan Rice. Despite his dislike of their brutal methods, Michael joins them. The team investigates Code Five schemes that often involve medical experiments: a barrister made pregnant with genetically crossbred human-vampire sperm, a man with a rare blood disorder caused by vampire attempts to make artificial blood. In the final episode the Code Fives' plan is revealed: to bring on a nuclear winter and wipe out humanity with an engineered plague, surviving on synthetic blood and reproducing by live birth.
