Seobok

The genetics

Human cloning and genetic engineering are at the heart of the film. Seobok is a clone made from the DNA of a scientist's long-dead son and engineered to be immortal, so that his body can serve as a resource for prolonging the lives of others. The film presents the people around him as treating him as a laboratory asset rather than a person: the company, its wealthy backers and foreign intelligence services all want to control his genetics, while the scientist who made him keeps an emotional distance. Critics described it as a reflection on scientists playing God, in the tradition of Frankenstein, and on who deserves to live forever and at what cost; the contrast between the immortal clone and the dying agent drives the story. Human reproductive cloning has never been achieved, and a clone would simply be a genetic copy of the donor, with no inherent immortality.

The story

Min Gi-heon, a former intelligence agent living in isolation and dying of a terminal brain tumour, is brought back for one last mission: to escort Seobok, the first human clone, created by the Seoin Research Institute through stem-cell cloning and genetic engineering. Seobok does not age and is effectively immortal, and Gi-heon is lured by the promise that research on him could cure his illness. As soon as the transfer begins, their convoy is attacked, and the two escape together. Seobok, who is seeing the outside world for the first time, asks Gi-heon to take him on a journey, and the two men, one who cannot die and one who is about to, clash and gradually bond. They are pursued by the company's chief executive, by double agents and by American agencies who want either to exploit Seobok's genetics or to eliminate him. Seobok's psychic powers grow, and in a destructive climax he kills the company's chief executive and fights off the military, until Gi-heon is forced to shoot him.
