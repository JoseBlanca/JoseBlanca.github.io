The genetics

There is no genetic science in this comedy. Its subject is biological fatherhood without social fatherhood: a man who never thought of the consequences of his sperm donations finds himself the genetic father of hundreds of strangers who want to know him, just as he is about to become a father in the ordinary sense. The question the film raises is what these young people owe to, and expect from, a man who shares their genes but played no part in raising them. The available sources give only the premise and do not say how the film resolves Diego's dilemma or how it weighs heredity against upbringing. Hundreds of offspring from one donor are an exaggeration for comic effect; real sperm banks and national regulations limit the number of families that can use the same donor, partly to reduce the risk of half-siblings unknowingly meeting.

The story

Diego Costa is a 42-year-old deliveryman for a family fishmonger in Paris who still lives like an irresponsible, blundering teenager. Just as his partner Elsa learns that she is pregnant, his past resurfaces. Twenty years earlier, under the pseudonym 'Fonzy', he repeatedly gave sperm, according to the French Wikipedia as part of a research protocol. He now discovers that he is the biological father of 533 children, 142 of whom want to know who Fonzy is, and a legal battle follows. Everything depends on Diego's choice: to remain anonymous, or to reveal himself to the young people who are his offspring while preparing to become a father. The film is a French remake of the Quebec comedy Starbuck (2011).
