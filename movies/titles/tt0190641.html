The genetics

Cloning and genetic modification are the core of the story. Mewtwo is a clone of Mew, created from an eyelash and enhanced to be more powerful, and much of the film is an existential reflection on whether a being made in a laboratory, and copies in general, have the same worth as the originals. The scientist who creates him is not simply a villain: Dr. Fuji works for a criminal organisation, but his own motive is grief, the wish to bring back his daughter. The experiment ends in disaster, as the creature turns on its makers. The original Japanese version was praised for its treatment of cloning, genetic engineering and existentialism, while the English dub was criticised for removing most of these ethical themes, including part of Mewtwo's origin story. In reality, a clone shares its donor's genome but is a separate individual: the first cloned cat, CC (2001), even had a different coat pattern from her donor because of X-inactivation, a process that genes alone do not fix in advance.

The story

Giovanni, leader of the criminal Team Rocket, recruits the scientist Dr. Fuji to create a cloned super-soldier from an eyelash of the rare Pokémon Mew; Fuji accepts because he hopes the research will let him clone his dead daughter, Amber. The result, Mewtwo, gains consciousness in the laboratory, where the other clones, including Amber's, fail and die. Learning that his creators see him only as an experiment, Mewtwo destroys the lab and kills Fuji and the scientists, then breaks with Giovanni when he realises he is meant to be a weapon. Resolving to take revenge on humans and Pokémon alike, he lures trainers to New Island, including Ash, Misty and Brock, and uses cloning to build an army of copies of their Pokémon. Originals and clones fight until Ash is turned to stone stepping between Mewtwo and Mew. Moved by his sacrifice, Mewtwo accepts that living beings should be judged by their choices, not their origins, and erases everyone's memories.
