The genetics

Cloning is the comic premise. The businessman commissions a copy of himself as a way to delegate his family life, and the story turns on the contrast between the original and the clone, who turns out to be the better son and husband. As in many clone comedies, the copy is created as an adult with the original's appearance but a different character, which serves the moral of the film, that a person's choices and attention matter more than his identity. The scientist, Stephen Raj, plays a supporting role as the provider of the technology and is not the focus of the story. Real cloning creates a new embryo that would grow up as a younger, genetically identical twin, not an adult double. Genetics here is a narrative device for a family drama and comedy and is not explored.

The story

Suryaprakash is an ambitious businessman who wants to become richer than Bill Gates. His obsession costs him his family and social life. Savithri, a college student researching him for a project, falls in love with him, and he marries her, though mostly because she is a good cook. Bothered by the demands of his family, Suryaprakash asks the scientist Stephen Raj to clone him, so that the clone can take care of things at home while he concentrates on his business. The clone soon realises that the problem lies not with the family but with Suryaprakash himself, and sets out to show him what his family is worth. After the death of his mother, Suryaprakash finally gives up his greed and comes to understand the value of his family. S. J. Suryah plays both the businessman and his clone in this Tamil comedy.
