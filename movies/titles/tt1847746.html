The genetics

The film portrays Josef Mengele as a doctor still obsessed with heredity, growth and twins, now applying his methods to an ordinary family. He measures and records Lilith and her relatives, offers hormones to correct her small stature, and is drawn to the twins, echoing his experiments at Auschwitz. Mengele is shown as charming, attentive and persuasive, which makes his eugenic views and clinical detachment more disturbing; the family's consent is won through Eva's worry for her daughter. The film is fiction built on real history. Mengele held doctorates in anthropology and medicine, was an assistant to the geneticist Otmar von Verschuer and, at Auschwitz, studied hundreds of pairs of twins, mostly children. He fled to Argentina in 1949, moved to Paraguay and then Brazil, and drowned in 1979 without ever facing trial; his remains were identified in 1985 and the identification confirmed by DNA testing in 1992. Director Lucía Puenzo adapted the film from her own novel, Wakolda.

The story

Patagonia, Argentina, 1960. A German doctor living under a false identity follows an Argentine family on the long road to Bariloche and moves into the hotel they run, paying six months' rent in advance. He is Josef Mengele, the Nazi physician of Auschwitz. He takes a special interest in the family's daughter, Lilith, who was born prematurely, is small for her age and is bullied at school, and offers to treat her with growth hormones. Her mother, Eva, agrees, hiding the treatment from her husband Enzo, who opposes it. Eva is pregnant with twins, which fascinates Mengele, who fills notebooks with observations on the family. Lilith falls ill from the hormones, and Enzo throws the doctor out, but when the twins are born prematurely, Mengele is the only doctor nearby and begins experimenting on them. A photographer in contact with Israeli agents tracks him, but he escapes by seaplane.
