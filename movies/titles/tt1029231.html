The genetics

Genetics drives both the villain and his schemes. Kaal is Rohit Mehra's biological son, created in an experiment by Dr. Siddhant Arya while Rohit was held captive in the earlier films; he inherited Rohit's powers but was born disabled. To cure himself, Kaal created the Maanvars, human-animal hybrids with animal powers, and when that failed he turned to profit, releasing a virus so that his company could sell the antidote. He also wants to take bone marrow from his unborn nephew and from Rohit. Against him stands Rohit, a scientist hero who makes an antidote from the blood of his naturally resistant son and dies saving him. Inherited powers are a running thread of the franchise: the film ends with Krishna's newborn son showing superpowers. The film borrows freely from American superhero cinema, and its science, from human-animal mutants to solar resurrection, is fantasy.

The story

Six years after Krrish, the scientist Rohit Mehra lives in Mumbai with his son Krishna, the masked hero Krrish, and Krishna's wife Priya, a television reporter. Rohit is developing a solar device to bring dead tissue back to life. Meanwhile Kaal, a disabled genius with telekinetic powers, has created human-animal mutants called Maanvars and releases a deadly virus in Namibia and then Mumbai so that his company can profit from the antidote; his shape-shifting henchwoman Kaya murders scientists who threaten to expose him. Rohit finds that the virus does not affect his family and makes an antidote from Krishna's blood, which Krrish spreads over the city. Kaal kidnaps the pregnant Priya to use her unborn child's bone marrow to cure himself, and Kaya replaces her. It emerges that Kaal is Rohit's son, created by Dr. Arya in an experiment while Rohit was his hostage. Kaal cures himself with Rohit's bone marrow; Rohit dies reviving Krrish, who finally kills Kaal with his father's solar device.
