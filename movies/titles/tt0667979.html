The genetics

Genetics provides the background of this two-part story. In "Double Helix", the geneticist Dr. Nodel studies introns, non-coding sections of genes that he believes hold the key to future evolution, and activates them in himself, starting a transformation that lets him communicate with the aliens who seeded humanity's DNA. "The Origin of Species" follows the consequences: the chosen humans are taken as a new seed population for an alien experiment, and genetic transformation becomes a form of selection. The episode concentrates on suspense aboard the ship rather than on science. The idea that introns contain a hidden evolutionary programme is fiction; introns are spliced out of RNA before translation, and although they play a part in regulating gene expression, they do not hold dormant plans for future humans. The notion that life on Earth was seeded by aliens is a long-standing science fiction theme without scientific support.

How genetics is portrayed

Overall portrayal of genetics
mixed The episode sets the ship's benevolent genetic work, which modifies the survivors' embryos so that humanity can restart, against the hubris of past and future humans whose 'rampant genetic engineering' and quest to 'genetically perfect' the species wiped them out; it ends on babies and a new beginning framed by a warning to learn from history, so both sides carry real weight.
Outcome of the genetic intervention
mixed Genetic engineering has twice destroyed humanity (the ancestors' attempt at perfection and the future humans' infertility virus), but the ship's genetic modification of the survivors' embryos gives humanity a fresh start.
The ship / the ancestors
hero Aim: benevolent (positive). An earlier Earth civilisation that nearly destroyed itself by genetic perfection left the living ship as a safeguard; it genetically alters Hope into an interface and uses the students' genetic material to create babies modified for enough diversity to repopulate. The episode presents them as trying 'to save us'. Affiliation 'other': a vanished pre-human civilisation.
Future humans who practised 'rampant genetic engineering'
villain Aim: ideological (negative). Shown only in backstory through a winged skeleton and a memorial, they engineered themselves until a DNA-destroying virus made humanity infertile and extinct. Their own inscription condemns them: 'we believed we could control nature'.
Dr. Martin Nodel
hero Aim: curiosity (neutral). His self-administered intron activator (from 'Double Helix') made him the ship's interface; here he leads the group aboard and dies when the ship absorbs him, his hereditary Wilson's disease blamed. The students honour him as a leader who gave his life for the mission.

The story

In this sequel to "Double Helix", Dr. Nodel, a scientist whose body has been altered so that he can communicate with aliens who once seeded Earth with their genetic material, boards their spaceship with his son Paul and six students, including Paul's girlfriend Hope. When Nodel touches a glowing post in the control room, he and Paul are consumed by light, and the students fear they have been led into a trap as the ship pulls them through the walls two by two. Hope reads Nodel's journal and touches the post herself; her body begins the same transformation, and a glowing entity speaks to her with the voices of Nodel and Paul. Eventually all the students are left suspended and unconscious in a black void. They wake to find that the ship has landed on a dead planet, and must wonder whether the aliens, who promised them a great experiment in hope, have betrayed them.
