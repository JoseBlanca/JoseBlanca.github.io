The genetics

The threat is a man-made pathogen: a rabies-like virus bred in a clandestine Los Angeles laboratory by an apocalyptic bioterrorist cell that wants to 'cull the human population', and deliberately spread by one of its members with infected lab rats. The government's response is no reassurance: the terminal is sealed with lethal force, and the survivors are treated with experimental antidotes, which fail, by a homeland-security unit that intends to study them. The film presents both the eco-terrorist use of biology and the state's containment as dangerous, and it ends with the virus escaping into Las Vegas. It is a sequel to Quarantine, the US remake of the Spanish film [REC], and gives no details of how the virus was made.

The story

On a night flight from Los Angeles, a passenger is bitten by one of the 'hamsters' that a man named Henry is carrying and turns violently ill, forcing an emergency landing. The passengers are sealed inside the airport terminal by soldiers and supposed CDC scientists. The infection, a rabies-like virus spread by bites, turns people into raging killers, and flight attendant Jenny, the boy George, the paramedic Shilah and others fight to survive. Officers who come in with drugs admit they belong to a Homeland Security bioterrorism unit, that the drugs are experimental antidotes and that the virus came from a quarantined bioterrorist lab in Los Angeles. George exposes Henry as a member of the terrorist cell, which spread the virus to cull humanity; Henry injects himself with the antidote, which fails. Jenny and George kill him, but Jenny is infected and only George escapes through a drainage tunnel, while an infected cat walks towards the Las Vegas Strip.
