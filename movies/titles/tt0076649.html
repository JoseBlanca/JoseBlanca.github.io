The genetics

The genetic element is the 'beast': a mutant human created by Dr. Kratsch through inhuman experiments, which the Italian Wikipedia describes as a subspecies of human being meant as a step towards the 'perfect race', and the English Wikipedia as a genetic mutant and a secret weapon. The film never explains how it was made; it is a pretext for sexual torture. Kratsch is a Nazi mad scientist in the exploitation tradition, cruel and seductive, and the film ends with a Frankenstein-like punishment in which her creation destroys her. It belongs to the 1970s Italian 'Naziploitation' cycle, which drew loosely on the real crimes of Nazi doctors and their racial ideology; critics consider it one of the most reprehensible examples, and it was banned in the UK as a 'video nasty'. The science has no plausibility and is not meant to.

The story

In the last days of the Third Reich, Dr. Ellen Kratsch, a sadistic SS doctor working in a castle held by Nazi troops, has created a dwarfish, incubus-like mutant man whom she keeps caged and fed on aphrodisiacs. She uses the creature to rape and torture female prisoners, alongside electric shocks, beatings and other tortures, to break the local partisan resistance that Captain Hardinghauser has been sent to suppress. Meanwhile the partisans blow up a railway bridge and quarrel among themselves, and a partisan woman, Irene, sleeps with the captain to spy on him. When the partisans finally storm the castle, they hand Kratsch over to her own creature, which turns on her and kills her.
