The genetics

Genetics plays a secondary role in this episode about doping in school sports. The drug Nuriflex 500 is said to be spliced with animal DNA, big cat DNA according to the episode summary on Wikipedia, and the Outer Limits fan wiki describes it as based on animal hormones; the young wrestlers gradually take on animal features and predatory behaviour. The story uses this as a Jekyll-and-Hyde metaphor for steroids and the pressure to win, and its moral concerns parents and coaches rather than scientists, who never appear: the drug comes through Jon, a former champion turned promoter. The idea that taking DNA in a pill transfers animal traits has no basis in biology, since ingested DNA is digested like any other food. The episode's real-life counterpart is the fear of gene doping in sport, the misuse of gene therapy to enhance performance, which the World Anti-Doping Agency added to its list of banned practices in 2003.

The story

Peter Shotwell once dreamed of the Olympics but blew out his knee, while his best friend Jon went on to become an international athletics star. Peter is now the coach of the Lewisborough Lions, his old high school's wrestling team, which sits at the bottom of the league, and his son Morris, a member of the team, resents his failures. Jon offers him Nuriflex 500, a new performance-enhancing drug he says contains scientifically balanced nutrients. Under pressure from the principal and his own son, Peter agrees to a trial. The team soon rises to the top, but the drug has horrific side effects: the wrestlers become aggressive and their eyes and teeth turn animal-like, and they kill a rival coach who threatens to expose them. When Peter tries to take them off the drug, the team, including his son, kills him.
