The genetics

Genetic engineering is the premise of the film, used purely as a pulp device. A secret Nazi laboratory has created flying sharks by genetic modification and a genetically engineered super-soldier that has survived as an army of zombies, and these creations are revived decades later. The scientists are Nazi researchers and their heirs, cast as the classic mad scientists of exploitation cinema, and their work is shown only through its destructive results. The film combines several familiar tropes: Nazi super-science, the dream of the perfect soldier and monstrous hybrid animals. There is a historical name in the background: Hans Kammler was a real SS officer who oversaw the construction of concentration camps, including Auschwitz, and led the V-2 rocket programme before disappearing in 1945, with his fate still debated. Genetic engineering itself did not exist until the 1970s, when the first recombinant DNA molecules and transgenic bacteria were created, decades after the war.

The story

Flying sharks attack an airliner, and the undead Nazi soldiers riding them board the plane and kill everyone aboard. Dr. Klaus Richter, the aged head of Richter Technology, sends his daughter Diabla to the area, where she finds a hidden Nazi laboratory, preserved in the ice since the Second World War, in which genetically engineered sharks are bred. Richter reveals to Diabla and her sister Angelique that the zombie soldiers come from Project Himmelsfaust, a wartime programme started by Dr. Hans Kammler to create a genetically engineered Nazi super-soldier. Once awakened, the sharks and their riders take to the skies and attack major cities around the world, and Richter urges military leaders to act, while a small team of soldiers, fallen in Vietnam, is sent to face the threat.
