The genetics

Genetic enhancement is the secret at the heart of the story, revealed late in the film. Hanna is the surviving product of a covert CIA project that modified the DNA of foetuses to create super-soldiers with "high intelligence, muscle mass, and no pity"; when the project was shut down, the agency eliminated its subjects. The scientists remain off screen, but the programme is shown as a cold state enterprise that treats people as disposable. Much of the film's interest lies in the tension between Hanna's engineered nature and her upbringing: Discover magazine's Kyle Munkittrick called her a "transhumanist hero" who is nevertheless a good-natured person, and said she symbolises the contest between genetics and environment, nature versus nurture. Engineering complex traits such as intelligence or empathy in human embryos remains beyond current science.

The story

Fifteen-year-old Hanna lives in the forests of northern Finland with her father, Erik Heller, a former CIA operative who has trained her since early childhood as an assassin. When she declares herself ready, she activates a beacon that alerts the CIA, and Erik leaves for Berlin. Captured and taken to an underground base in Morocco, Hanna kills a double of the CIA officer Marissa Wiegler and escapes, travelling across Spain and Europe with a British family on holiday while Marissa and the hired killer Isaacs pursue her. In Berlin she learns that Erik is not her biological father: he had recruited pregnant women into a CIA programme in which their children's DNA was enhanced to create super-soldiers, and when the programme was shut down all its subjects except Hanna were killed. Erik is shot dead by Marissa, and Hanna tracks Marissa down and kills her.
