The genetics

The episode turns on the 'XYY defence'. In the 1960s, surveys found men with an extra Y chromosome (47,XYY) overrepresented in high-security institutions, and the idea of a 'criminal chromosome' spread to the press and to some courtrooms. Later, larger studies showed that most XYY men are never diagnosed and are not violent; the condition is associated mainly with greater height and a higher rate of learning and behavioural difficulties, and the modest excess of convictions largely reflects these and social factors rather than aggression. The defence therefore rests on a discredited idea. The episode sets this genetic excuse in the case of a boy raised in foster care, so upbringing is implicitly part of the question. The story

Teenager Chris Pollit, who lives in a foster home, beats a fellow resident, Johnny Lasky, to death. His lawyer, Helen Brolin, seeks a verdict of not guilty by reason of genetic defect: she argues that Pollit is genetically predisposed to violence because he has an extra Y chromosome, so that the prosecution must answer a defence based on his genes rather than on his actions.
