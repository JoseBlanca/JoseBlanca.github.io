The genetics

Human genetic enhancement is the centre of the episode. In the Star Trek universe it is outlawed because of the Eugenics Wars, in which genetically engineered "supermen" such as Khan seized power on Earth; in the episode, a Starfleet admiral cites them to justify the ban. The episode presents the issue through a family drama. Bashir's parents acted out of love and anxiety, wanting a son who could keep up, while Julian feels that his original self was erased and that he is a fraud. The geneticists themselves remain off screen, in an alien clinic beyond Federation law. The story raises questions that remain central to debates on germline editing and enhancement: the distinction between therapy and enhancement, a child's consent, and the risk of discrimination against enhanced people. The writers decided at the last minute to make Bashir genetically engineered, and later episodes developed the idea further. Enhancing intelligence by gene editing is not possible today, because intelligence depends on thousands of genetic variants with small effects.

The story

Holographic engineer Lewis Zimmerman comes to Deep Space Nine to use Dr. Julian Bashir as the template for a new long-term medical hologram. Against Julian's wishes, he invites Bashir's estranged parents, Richard and Amsha, to be interviewed. Speaking to what they think is their son but is in fact the hologram, the parents mention that they had him illegally genetically modified as a child, and Zimmerman and Chief O'Brien overhear. Julian explains that as a young boy he was a poor learner and small for his age, and that before his seventh birthday his parents took him to Adigeon Prime for treatments that enhanced his mental and physical abilities. Because Federation law bars genetically enhanced people from Starfleet and from practising medicine, he prepares to resign. His parents instead make a deal with Starfleet's Judge Advocate General: Richard pleads guilty and accepts two years in prison, and Julian keeps his commission and his licence.
