The genetics

Genetic engineering and cloning are the villain's tools in this comedy. Sue Nahmee, head of a Texas science laboratory, plans to genetically modify the sea creatures of Bikini Bottom so that they can breathe air, then clone them and sell them as toy pets; Mr. Krabs, Squidward and Patrick are experimented on, and SpongeBob is split into thousands of miniature copies. The villain is a commercial schemer, and the heroine, Sandy, is also a scientist, so the film shows science used both well and badly, which one critic praised as an admirably nuanced view of science. Sandy's own heroics, though, rely on courage and family rather than on genetics. The science is deliberately cartoonish: cloning does not split an organism into tiny copies, and making a gilled animal breathe air would take far more than a genetic tweak.

The story

In this live-action and animated SpongeBob SquarePants film, the Bureau Of Official Texas Science (B.O.O.T.S.), the Texas laboratory where the squirrel scientist Sandy Cheeks works, scoops Bikini Bottom out of the ocean. Sandy and SpongeBob travel across Texas to rescue it, helped by Sandy's family of circus performers and chased by agents of the lab's new head, Sue Nahmee. At B.O.O.T.S., Sue imprisons Sandy and reveals that she is a cyborg who wants to genetically modify the people of Bikini Bottom to breathe air and clone them as toy pets, because she longs to cuddle fish; she also reveals that Sandy's life in Bikini Bottom was used to spy on its inhabitants. SpongeBob is split into thousands of tiny copies in a cloning chamber. Sandy escapes and defeats Sue, the copies are fused back together, and Bikini Bottom is returned to the sea.
