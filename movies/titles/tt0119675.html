Mimic

1997 Film

The genetics

Genetic engineering is the central idea. Susan Tyler creates the Judas breed for a humanitarian purpose, to stop an epidemic killing children, and builds in safeguards: the insects are all female and live only six months, so the breed should die out after one generation. The film's point is that these controls fail, and an accelerated metabolism lets the breed pass through tens of thousands of generations in three years, evolve lungs and grow large enough to mimic its human prey. Tyler is a sympathetic scientist who must face the unintended consequences of her work, rather than a villain. The premise echoes real methods of insect control. In the sterile insect technique, used against pests such as the screw-worm fly, mass releases of irradiated sterile males make populations collapse; approaches based on genetically modified insects are still under development. The film's evolutionary leap, however, is impossible on such a timescale and works as a cautionary fable about releasing engineered organisms.

The story

In New York, cockroaches are spreading Strickler's disease, which is killing hundreds of children. Entomologist Dr. Susan Tyler, recruited by CDC deputy director Dr. Peter Mann, uses genetic engineering to create the "Judas breed", a hybrid of mantis and termite that releases an enzyme which makes the roaches starve to death. The epidemic ends, and Susan and Peter marry. Three years later, people start disappearing in the subway, and two boys sell Susan a strange insect that resembles her creation. The Judas insects were all female and designed to die within six months, but they have survived, bred and evolved into a human-sized species that hunts people in the tunnels, folding its body to look like a man in a coat. Trapped underground with a few companions, Susan and Peter realise they must kill the colony's single fertile male. Peter blows up the nest with a gas leak, Susan lures the male in front of a train, and both survive.
