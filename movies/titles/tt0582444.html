The genetics

Genetics enters through the choice of a biological father. Lilith wants another child and turns to her former husband as a sperm donor, hoping in effect for another child like their son Frederick, a comic take on choosing a donor for his traits. The episode treats assisted reproduction, sperm banking and donor choice as ordinary parts of modern family planning, mining them for awkward humour rather than ethical debate. No scientist plays a role in the procedure, although Lilith's new acquaintance, Albert, turns out to be one. Its relevance to genetics is therefore light: it reflects the idea, common in real donor selection, that prospective parents look for desirable traits in a donor, though the heredity of complex traits such as intelligence or temperament is far less predictable than such choices assume.

How genetics is portrayed

Overall portrayal of genetics
neutral Donor choice, heredity and cloning are material for sitcom jokes, and the resolution concerns nostalgia and parenthood ('you can't use the past to fill what's missing'), not the merits of genetics.
Outcome of the genetic intervention
not applicable Nothing genetic is done: Lilith's plan to use Frasier as a sperm donor, chosen with a jokey 'genome square', is abandoned when Frasier declines.

The story

On a flight to Seattle, Frasier's ex-wife Lilith meets Albert, a pale, awkward fellow passenger, and tells him she is going to ask an old friend for a favour. In Seattle she asks Frasier to donate sperm so that she can have another child, appealing to their memories of raising their son Frederick. The two go to a sperm bank, where they talk the matter through and part on good terms. Meanwhile, Daphne takes her mother, Roz and Roz's daughter Alice to a theme park in Canada, leaving Niles with a set of photographs; he and Frasier puzzle over the last one, which Niles mistakes for a picture of Daphne until she reveals that it shows Martin. On the flight back to Boston, Lilith meets Albert again, learns that he is a divorced scientist, and the two discover that they have much in common.
