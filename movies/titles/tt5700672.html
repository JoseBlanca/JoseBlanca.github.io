The genetics

Genetics is not explicitly part of this zombie film, but its outbreak comes from a laboratory: a leak at a biochemical or biotech plant, waved away by a cleanup crew in the opening scene, and the hero's own investment firm turns out to have propped up that plant despite its irregularities to protect its stock value. By this database's criteria, the lab origin makes it a story of a man-made pathogen. The film does not show the scientists or explain the infection; its interest lies in social commentary, on selfishness, class and institutional failure, which Korean critics linked to the Sewol ferry disaster. The biotech industry appears only as the negligent, profit-protected source of the catastrophe.

The story

A truck driver is waved through a disinfection checkpoint after a leak at a biochemical plant, and a deer he runs over comes back to life. In Seoul, Seok-woo, a cynical fund manager who has neglected his daughter Su-an, agrees to take her to her mother in Busan on the KTX train. An infected woman boards and turns into a zombie, and the infection sweeps through the carriages. Daejeon station has been overrun, and Seok-woo learns that his own firm indirectly caused the outbreak by backing a biotech plant with irregularities to protect its share price. Seok-woo, the working-class Sang-hwa and his pregnant wife Seong-kyeong, a baseball player and his girlfriend, elderly sisters and a homeless man fight through zombie-filled carriages, while the selfish executive Yon-suk sacrifices others to save himself. One by one the protectors die; Seok-woo, bitten, throws himself from the train. Seong-kyeong and Su-an reach the military cordon outside Busan, where Su-an's song convinces soldiers not to shoot.
