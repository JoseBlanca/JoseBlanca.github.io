The genetics

Genetics provides both the accident and the dilemma. During transport, the orchids' genetic material acts as a catalyst that merges the DNA of the Vulcan Tuvok, the Talaxian Neelix and the flowers into one stable organism, an idea loosely borrowed from the biological concept of symbiogenesis. The Doctor later reverses it by tagging one man's DNA with a custom radioisotope so that the transporter can disentangle the two genomes. Nobody intends the merger. The ethics of the separation are the subject: the Doctor, who developed the technique, refuses to use it on an unwilling patient, and Janeway does so herself to restore two crew members at the cost of a third person's life. Critics have called the biology laughable: no real process could merge two complete organisms and their memories in this way.

The story

Tuvok and Neelix collect orchids on an alien planet, and when they are beamed back to Voyager a single person materializes in their place. The Doctor finds that the orchids acted as a symbiogenetic catalyst, merging the two men and the flowers at the molecular level. The new being, who calls himself Tuvix, has the memories of both and settles into the crew as tactical officer and cook, outperforming both of his predecessors, although Kes, who loved Neelix and was Tuvok's student, grieves for them. Two weeks later the Doctor finds a way to separate them, using a radioisotope that marks the DNA of one of the originals so that the transporter can tell them apart. Tuvix refuses, arguing that he is an individual with the right to live. No crew member supports him, the Doctor refuses to operate against his will, and Janeway carries out the procedure herself, restoring Tuvok and Neelix and walking away troubled.
