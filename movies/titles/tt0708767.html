The genetics

Cloning and DNA identification drive the plot, but the episode's real subject is faith and political legitimacy. The priests of Boreth use ancient blood preserved on a dagger to clone their messiah, and the deception survives a genetic test precisely because a clone carries the same DNA as the original: the test proves genetic identity, not that the man is the historical Kahless. The scientists here are religious leaders who use the technology to revive belief and strengthen their own influence, and the episode leaves open whether their manipulation is harmful or beneficial for the Klingon people. It also makes the point, familiar from real discussions of cloning, that a clone is a new individual who shares a genome with the donor but not his memories or experience; this Kahless lacks the memories the real one would have had. Recovering an intact genome from centuries-old blood and cloning from it remains well beyond current science.

The story

Going through a crisis of faith, Worf travels to the Klingon monastery on Boreth, where the faithful await the return of Kahless, the legendary warrior who founded the Klingon Empire. After days of ritual, a man appearing to be Kahless materialises before him, and Worf brings him aboard the Enterprise, though he is troubled by gaps in Kahless's memory. Chancellor Gowron, suspecting an impostor planted by the priests to gain power, brings an ancient dagger stained with Kahless's blood and asks for a genetic comparison; the DNA matches. Gowron then defeats Kahless in a duel, and the high priest Koroth admits that this Kahless is a clone of the original. Worf persuades Gowron to install the clone as a ceremonial emperor who can unite the Klingon people while real power stays with the council.
