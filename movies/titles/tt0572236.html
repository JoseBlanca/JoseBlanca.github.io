The genetics

The episode is an ethical drama about creating a person for spare parts. Sim is effectively a short-lived clone grown from Tucker's blood, and the story asks whether a being created to save someone else has a right to his own life. Phlox, the ship's doctor, is portrayed as caring, acting as Sim's father, but he withholds information about a possible way to extend Sim's life; Captain Archer puts the mission first and is willing to sacrifice Sim. Reviewers have singled out the episode as one of the most thought-provoking in the series for this dilemma. The science is invented: the clone ages to adulthood in days and inherits Tucker's memories, neither of which is possible with real cloning. The premise echoes real debates about creating embryos or "saviour siblings" as sources of tissue for others.

The story

During the Enterprise's mission against the Xindi, Chief Engineer Trip Tucker falls into a coma after an engine accident. Doctor Phlox proposes a controversial procedure: growing a "mimetic symbiote" from Tucker's blood to serve as a source of neural tissue. Captain Archer approves it for the sake of the mission. The symbiote, named Sim, ages from baby to adult in days, has a natural lifespan of about two weeks, and develops Tucker's memories and personality, including his love of engineering and his attraction to T'Pol. Sim learns that the transplant will kill him and that an experimental procedure might extend his life, which Phlox had kept quiet. He asks to live out his life, and Archer tells him he would rather Sim agree than be forced. Thinking of his sister, killed in the Xindi attack, Sim accepts the procedure. Tucker recovers, and the crew hold a funeral for Sim.
