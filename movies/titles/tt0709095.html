The genetics

Cloning drives the episode and DNA testing unlocks it. A medical DNA test confirms that the teenage intruder really is O'Neill, genetically, and the small anomaly it reveals is later recognised as the mark of a clone. The cloner is Loki, a renegade Asgard scientist: his species, which reproduces only by cloning, suffers from cellular degeneration, and he abducts humans and makes short-lived, deliberately defective clones of them to study human DNA in search of a cure, returning the originals so that no one notices. Loki is a villain, condemned by his own people for unsanctioned experiments on humans, while the base physician Dr. Fraiser and the Asgard Thor, who repairs the clone's genetic defect so that he can live, are positive figures. The episode then treats the clone as a person in his own right, with a second chance at youth. Real cloning produces a genetic twin born as a baby, not a teenage copy with its original's memories.

The story

An intruder caught using Colonel Jack O'Neill's ID badge at Stargate Command turns out to be a fifteen-year-old boy who claims to be O'Neill and knows everything O'Neill knows. Dr. Janet Fraiser's genetic tests show that he is genetically O'Neill, apart from a small abnormality, and later that he is dying. Remembering a vision of an Asgard and floating lights, the boy suspects an alien abduction; Daniel and Teal'c find eight other people with similar stories, and the Tok'ra Selmak and Fraiser establish that the boy is a clone. SG-1 waits for the abductor to return, and the young O'Neill gets them aboard his ship, where they capture Loki, a renegade Asgard scientist who has been experimenting on humans. Thor arrives, returns the real O'Neill and punishes Loki. He also repairs the clone's genetic defect, and the young O'Neill goes back to high school.
