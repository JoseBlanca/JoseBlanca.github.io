The genetics

The film moves the Frankenstein story into stem-cell and genetic research. Victoria Frankenstein, a geneticist growing organs in the laboratory, adds her dying son's blood, and with it his DNA, to a stem-cell culture, which grows into a humanoid creature carrying William's DNA. The work is driven by a mother's love: she first wants a cure and a donor for William, and later sees the creature as her son and tries to show it love. Unlike Shelley's creator she is presented as humane, but her secret experiment produces a creature that kills four people. The darker figures are Henry Clerval and Professor Jane Pretorius, who want to exploit the creature for weapons research and soldiers, and the ambiguous ending leaves Victoria in captivity under their control. Growing organs from stem cells is a real research goal; a culture turning into a child-like creature with a dead boy's identity is fiction.

The story

In this present-day British television version of Mary Shelley's novel, Dr. Victoria Frankenstein works on the Universal Xenograft Project, growing replacement organs from stem cells, in the hope of saving her terminally ill eight-year-old son, William. She secretly adds William's blood to the stem-cell mixture, and an organism begins to grow in the tank; the team, which calls it 'the UX', lets it live for the sake of science. After William dies, a power cut lets the humanoid creature escape: it kills a girl in the sewers and then a guard and Victoria's colleagues Ed Gore and Andrew Waldman. Captured and moved to a secret laboratory run by Victoria's ex-husband, Henry Clerval, the creature behaves like William, and Victoria comes to see it as her son. She frees it with Henry's help, but armed men kill Henry and seize them both. Her boss, Professor Jane Pretorius, wants the creature as a model for soldiers; the film ends with Victoria teaching it in captivity.
