The genetics

Genetics is at the heart of the film, which follows a geneticist's obsessive attempt to cure his children's inherited disease by creating a human-jellyfish chimera with the jellyfish's capacity to regenerate. Quint is a sympathetic but morally compromised scientist driven by grief: he farms cloned animals and human embryos, works outside the law in a setting where stem cell research is banned, and depends on an unscrupulous financier. His downfall comes not from the science itself but from betrayal, as a colleague steals his work and leaves him to be jailed. Reviewers found the science sketchy, describing it as hints and pseudo-scientific doubletalk. The real Turritopsis dohrnii, known as the immortal jellyfish, is one of the few animals able to revert from its sexually mature stage to an immature polyp, and its life cycle is studied with genetic tools; but transferring such an ability to humans through a chimera is pure fiction. The film also loosely borrows the character names of Henry James's The Turn of the Screw.

The story

Quint, a brilliant but unstable geneticist, works in secret in an abandoned research facility to save his children, Miles and Flora, who have a deadly degenerative genetic disease. He has frozen them in cryptobiosis and also keeps the comatose body of his estranged wife, Jessie, in the facility, while pills allow him to see his family in his mind. His research centres on the DNA of Turritopsis, the "immortal" jellyfish, which he hopes to combine with human DNA in a chimera able to regenerate. Because stem cell research is illegal, he depends on a ruthless backer, played by Kathleen Quinlan, who wants him to cure her own monstrous son. A former colleague and lover, Charlie, visits him and becomes involved in his work. In a final twist, she turns out to have come to steal his research, carrying an embryo out of the lab in her own body, and Quint is arrested for his experiments. The film was first shown at festivals as Chimera.
