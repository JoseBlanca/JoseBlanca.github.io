The genetics

The film adapts Audrey Niffenegger's novel and gives its fantasy premise a genetic label: Henry's time travel is described as a rare genetic disorder. Heredity matters to the plot. Henry's genes make the couple's fetuses time travel out of the womb, causing a series of miscarriages that lead them to consult a renowned doctor and Henry to have a vasectomy, and the daughter they finally have inherits the trait, though in her it is controllable. The film is a romantic tragedy, and genetics works as an explanation for an affliction and its inheritance rather than as a science that anyone practises or questions. There is, of course, no genetic basis for time travel; the film borrows the language of hereditary disease to make a paranormal condition feel like a medical one.

The story

Henry DeTamble, a Chicago librarian, has a genetic disorder that makes him travel through time involuntarily, arriving naked and unable to change major events; as a child he survives the car crash that kills his mother by jumping back in time. In 1991 he meets Clare Abshire, an artist who has known his older self since she was a child, and they fall in love and marry despite his unpredictable disappearances. They are unable to have a child because Henry's condition makes the fetuses travel out of the womb, causing repeated miscarriages, and after seeking a renowned doctor's help Henry secretly has a vasectomy. Clare nevertheless becomes pregnant by a visiting younger Henry and gives birth to Alba, who inherits the ability but learns to control it. Henry learns from the future Alba that he will die when she is five; he is accidentally shot by Clare's father while hunting and dies in Clare's arms.
