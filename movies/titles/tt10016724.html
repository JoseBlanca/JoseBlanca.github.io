The genetics

The series updates the cloning theme of Orphan Black with a fictional technology: a machine that prints complete human bodies. In the backstory, Kira Manning's research on printing human DNA was meant to transform organ transplants, but she used it to recreate her dying wife, despite having opposed printing a whole person because of the history of human cloning. The series explores identity, memory and ownership: who a printed person is, whether she belongs to her creator, and what happens when a wealthy patron sees the technology as a source of power. The scientist is presented as brilliant and grieving, and the billionaire who funds her as the main threat, continuing the original's theme of corporate control over human bodies. Bioprinting of tissues is an active field of research, but printing a complete living human, let alone one with a predetermined body, is far beyond any real capability.

The story

Set in 2052, thirty-seven years after the events of Orphan Black, this spin-off follows Lucy, a woman who wakes up with no memory of who she is and has rebuilt a quiet life when her past catches up with her. She learns that she is a "printout", a human made with a printer developed by the scientist Kira Manning, daughter of the original series' heroine Sarah Manning, and funded by the billionaire Paul Darros. Kira created Lucy from an earlier body scan of her wife, Eleanor, after Eleanor lost her memory to Alzheimer's disease and died. Lucy also meets Jules, a teenage girl who looks exactly like her, printed by someone else. While Lucy searches for her identity with the help of her boyfriend Jack, Kira, who believed the printer destroyed, must confront what she has done and Darros's plans for the technology.
