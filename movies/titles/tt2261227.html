The genetics

Genetics plays a supporting role in this cyberpunk series, whose central technology is digital: minds stored in implanted stacks and moved between bodies. Cloning enters through the wealthy 'Meths', who keep series of clones of their own bodies at the company Psychasec, ready to receive their minds when the old body dies, which is how the murdered Laurens Bancroft returns to life at the start of the story. The clones are thus a privilege of the immortal elite, and the series uses them, together with the trade in bodies of the poor, to criticise extreme inequality. No geneticists appear, and the sources do not say how the clones are made or whether other bodies are engineered. Real cloning produces a genetically identical baby that grows up like anyone else, not a ready-made adult body.

The story

Centuries in the future, people's minds are stored in 'cortical stacks' implanted in their necks and can be transferred to new bodies, or 'sleeves', when they die; only the ultra-rich 'Meths' can afford to live for ever, renewing themselves in clones with backups stored by satellite. In 2384, Takeshi Kovacs, the last surviving soldier of the Envoys, a rebel movement crushed 250 years earlier, is revived in the body of a police officer by Laurens Bancroft, a 300-year-old Meth, who wants him to find out who killed his previous body. Watched by Lieutenant Kristin Ortega, the dead officer's former lover, Kovacs investigates Bancroft's family, the city's brothels and the killers sent to stop him, while remembering his lost love Quellcrist Falconer, the Envoys' leader and inventor of the stacks. In the second season, in a new sleeve, Kovacs searches for Quell on Harlan's World. The series was cancelled after two seasons.
