The genetics

Two genetic elements appear. The main one is inherited character: Brooks treats his compulsion to kill as an addiction, and when he discovers that Jane has killed, he blames himself because his evil seems hereditary and fears it has passed to her. The story favours nature: Jane, raised in a wealthy and loving family, appears to become a killer like her father, and the film ends with Brooks dreaming that she murders him. The second is DNA forensics: the urine Smith leaves at a crime scene gives the police DNA that steers suspicion away from Brooks. As for plausibility, family studies show that antisocial behaviour runs in families partly through genes, but the effect is spread over many genes and depends heavily on the environment; there is no inherited compulsion to murder that a father can pass to his daughter.

The story

Earl Brooks, a respected Portland businessman just named Man of the Year, is secretly the 'Thumbprint Killer', a serial murderer urged on by his imaginary alter ego, Marshall. After two years without killing, he murders a couple in their bedroom but leaves the curtains open, and a neighbour who calls himself Mr. Smith photographs him and blackmails him into taking him along on his next murder. Meanwhile Brooks's daughter Jane drops out of college pregnant, and Brooks realises that she has killed someone on her campus; to shield her, he commits a copycat murder there. Detective Tracy Atwood hunts the Thumbprint Killer while being pursued by another escaped killer. Brooks kills Smith, whose urine, left at the last crime scene, gives the police DNA that points to Smith as the killer, and Brooks remains free and unable to stop.
