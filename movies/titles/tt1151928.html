The genetics

Genetic engineering, combined with other modifications, is how the military programme creates its soldier: I.S.A.A.C. is described as genetically reconstructed, physically regenerative and intellectually superior, a man redesigned as a weapon. The scientists behind him serve a secret military project, use a death-row inmate as raw material and pursue him when he escapes, so the film follows the familiar pattern of the super-soldier story, in which the creation turns against its makers and the hero's task is to expose those responsible. The available sources describe the method only in vague terms, and the film, despite its title, seems to treat genetic and cybernetic enhancement interchangeably. Real research on regeneration or on enhancing human performance is nowhere near producing a soldier of this kind.

The story

I.S.A.A.C. (Intuitive Synthetic Autonomous Assault Commando), played by mixed martial artist Rich Franklin, is the first prototype of a secret military "human weapon" programme. A former death-row inmate, he has been genetically reconstructed and trained into a deadly assassin, with a regenerating body and enhanced intellect. After escaping, he takes deputy sheriff Lindsay Rearden hostage while on the run. Both are pursued by military agents led by the robotics engineer Simon Hart, who wants his creation back, and the two end up working together to expose the group behind the programme to create such super soldiers. The film was directed by John Stead and released in 2008.
