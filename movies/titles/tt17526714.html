The genetics

The Substance uses biology as a metaphor rather than a subject. The drug's promotional video claims that a single injection "unlocks" the user's DNA and starts a new cell division that releases another version of oneself, and the film's body horror follows from that premise: a second, younger body grows out of the first, depends on fluid taken from it and, when the rules are broken, drains and deforms it. Nobody explains how it works, and no scientist appears; the substance is sold anonymously on the black market, with only a voice on the phone to enforce its rules. The film is a satire of the pressures placed on women's bodies and ageing in the entertainment industry, and the "younger self" is presented as part of the same person rather than as a clone, which is why their rivalry ends in self-destruction. The premise has no scientific basis: no drug can make a body grow an adult copy of itself.

The story

On her 50th birthday, the fading Hollywood star Elisabeth Sparkle is fired from her aerobics television show by its producer, Harvey, for being too old. After a car accident, a young nurse gives her a flash drive advertising "The Substance", a black-market drug that promises a younger, more beautiful version of oneself. Elisabeth injects it, and a young woman, Sue, emerges from a slit in her back. The rules are strict: the two must switch consciousness every week, with the inactive body fed intravenously, and Sue needs daily injections of a stabilising fluid extracted from Elisabeth's body. Sue becomes a star and replaces Elisabeth on television, but she keeps extending her time, causing Elisabeth to age rapidly, and the two come to hate each other. Elisabeth tries to terminate Sue but stops halfway; Sue beats her to death, then deteriorates herself and uses the remaining serum to create another body, a grotesque monster that disintegrates on stage at a New Year's Eve show.
