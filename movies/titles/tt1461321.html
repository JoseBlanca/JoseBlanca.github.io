The genetics

Genetic modification of an unborn child drives the case in this Fringe episode. Andre Hughes, desperate for his child to survive a pregnancy his wife's lupus made impossible, altered the baby's DNA, and the boy became a burrowing predator that secretes a paralytic substance and feeds on people. Walter identifies the substance as a product of mutated DNA. Hughes is portrayed as a parent driven by love and grief rather than a cold scientist, and his experiment shows the series' recurring theme of well-meant science with monstrous consequences. The episode reflects real anxieties about germline modification and "designer babies", but grossly exaggerates its effects: no genetic change could turn a human infant into a creature with animal traits within a few years.

The story

In Lansdale, Pennsylvania, a construction worker is dragged into a tunnel under a cornfield by an unseen creature, one of several unexplained disappearances in the area. Still recovering from a car crash, Olivia Dunham returns to work while Walter believes she travelled to a parallel universe; she begins to develop unusually sharp hearing. At the scene, Walter finds a blue paralytic substance that he suspects comes from mutated DNA. The investigation leads to local resident Andre Hughes, who says he lost his wife and baby in childbirth and refuses to give a blood sample. When the bodies are exhumed, the baby's coffin is empty, and Hughes kills himself before he can be questioned. Walter discovers that Hughes's wife had a form of lupus that made her unable to carry a child, so Hughes genetically altered the baby to survive. The child has grown into a creature that feeds on people; it is killed when its tunnel collapses.
