The genetics

The episode treats genetic ancestry as identity. Sulan separates Torres's human and Klingon genomes into two bodies, and each half is shown with a distinct personality: the Klingon aggressive and proud, the human cautious and fragile. The story uses this split to explore Torres's conflict about her mixed heritage, and it ends with her accepting that she needs both. The scientist is a Vidiian doctor driven by a real emergency, the Phage that is destroying his people, but his methods include forced experimentation on prisoners and wearing a murdered man's face. Behind the procedure lies a plausible idea, studying a naturally immune individual to find a cure, but splitting a hybrid into two complete beings is impossible: a person's two parental genomes are combined in every cell, and personality is not divided by ancestry. The notion that behaviour follows species or racial heritage is a Star Trek convention rather than science.

The story

Tom Paris, B'Elanna Torres and Peter Durst are captured by the Vidiians, a species ravaged by a disease called the Phage. Their chief surgeon, Sulan, splits Torres, who is half human and half Klingon, into two separate individuals: a full-blooded Klingon and a full-blooded human. Because Klingons are naturally immune to the Phage, he infects the Klingon Torres to study her genetics in search of a cure. The Klingon Torres is fierce and proud of the heritage she hid as a child, while the human Torres, kept in a labour camp with Paris and Durst, is timid and frail. Sulan, attracted to the Klingon Torres, kills Durst and wears his face. The two halves of Torres join forces to escape and lower the shields so that Voyager can rescue them. The Klingon Torres dies protecting the others, and the Doctor restores Torres by reintegrating the Klingon DNA.
