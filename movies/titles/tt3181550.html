The genetics

Genetically modified food is the starting point of the episode, which first presents both sides of the public debate: Lisa begins as a critic, then becomes convinced that the technology can be beneficial, a relatively balanced position for a comedy. The story then escalates into a mad-scientist parody. Sideshow Bob, the corporation's chief scientist, uses the company's gene-modification techniques on himself, adding traits from animals and planning to absorb the DNA of famous geniuses, and he loses his humanity in the process. The corporation that hires a convicted criminal as its lead researcher is a satire of biotech companies. The episode was broadcast in Spain as "El hombre que crecía demasiado". In reality, intelligence cannot be acquired by taking DNA from other people, and adding animal traits to an adult human is impossible; the real debate on genetically modified crops concerns their safety, regulation and economic effects, which the episode touches on only briefly.

The story

Lisa learns that the vegetables served at school are genetically modified and argues against them at a parent-teacher meeting, but further research convinces her that genetically modified foods can be beneficial. A biotechnology corporation interested in her campaign introduces the Simpsons to its chief scientist, who turns out to be Sideshow Bob, recruited as a test subject in prison. Lisa and Bob bond over their love of Walt Whitman and conduct food experiments together. Meanwhile, Marge is roped into giving a sex-education class to church teenagers. On a museum visit, Bob saves Lisa from a falling display with superhuman strength and reveals that he has altered his own DNA and plans to absorb the DNA of history's greatest geniuses to become the smartest man alive. Enraged, he chases Bart and Lisa with grasshopper legs and killer whale sonar until Lisa quotes Whitman and he realises he has become a monster. He tries to drown himself, but has given himself gills.
