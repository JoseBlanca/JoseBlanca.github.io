The genetics

Like the Jurassic Park films, the series is built on dinosaurs recreated through genetic engineering, and it gives particular attention to hybrids. Dr. Henry Wu appears as the scientist behind the Indominus rex and the Scorpios rex, monstrous creatures made by combining genes from different species, and he is eventually jailed for creating them; the campers also find a facility of baby hybrids, two of which become their companions. Corporate competition over genetic material is a recurring thread: a camper spies for a rival company by collecting dinosaur DNA, and an industrialist demands DNA samples from the island's animals. Scientists are mostly shown working for companies driven by profit, although a behavioural paleoneurobiologist, Mae Turner, eventually sides with the teenagers and the animals. Real de-extinction projects focus on recently extinct species such as the passenger pigeon, and even their DNA from museum specimens is degraded; dinosaurs remain out of reach, so the series' premise is science fiction.

The story

Six teenagers, including dinosaur enthusiast Darius Bowman, win places at Camp Cretaceous, an exclusive adventure camp on Isla Nublar, and are stranded there when the dinosaurs of Jurassic World escape. Fending for themselves across five seasons, they visit a genetics lab where they see the hatching of an ankylosaur, Bumpy, who bonds with one of them; learn from a paranoid scientist about Dr. Henry Wu's role in creating the hybrid Indominus rex; and are hunted by the Scorpios rex, an aggressive earlier hybrid made by Wu with scorpionfish genes. One camper, Sammy, turns out to be a spy for Mantah Corp, a rival bioengineering company to InGen, sent to take DNA samples. After escaping Isla Nublar, the group lands on Mantah Corp's own island, where the company is drugging dinosaurs to make them aggressive and developing mind-control chips to sell to Lewis Dodgson, who also wants DNA samples from the Nublar dinosaurs. The campers sabotage the operation and return home.
