The genetics

The serial tells how the Daleks were deliberately made rather than evolved. Davros, the Kaleds' chief scientist, conducts experiments that a colleague condemns as unethical and produces creatures kept in an incubation room and housed in armoured travel machines; commentators note that the story makes the Daleks "evil by design, rather than evolution" and turns Davros into the mad scientist whose creation destroys him. The genetic element is tied to eugenic ideology: the Kaleds, modelled on the Nazis in uniforms and salutes, seek to keep their race pure by banishing the mutated Mutos. Davros is a villain who removes anyone who resists the project, and the story ends with his creatures exterminating their makers. Critics have read it as a warning that scientists can deliberately bring about the ultimate evil, and as a moral dilemma about whether the Doctor may destroy them. The biology of the Daleks' creation is left vague.

The story

The Time Lords send the Fourth Doctor, Sarah Jane Smith and Harry Sullivan to Skaro to stop the creation of the Daleks. A long war between Thals and Kaleds has left only two domes, and outside them live the Mutos, mutated exiles banished by both sides. The Doctor and Harry meet the Kaled elite, including the scientist Davros, who unveils his "Mark III travel machine", the Dalek. When the scientist Ronson warns that Davros's experiments are unethical and the leadership halts them, Davros helps the Thals destroy the Kaled dome, has Ronson killed and sends his Daleks against the Thals. The Doctor rigs the Dalek incubation room with explosives but hesitates, asking whether he has the right. The Daleks exterminate the remaining Kaleds and then Davros himself, and the Doctor accepts that he has only delayed the Daleks' rise.
