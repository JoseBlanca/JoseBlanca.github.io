The genetics

The documentary deals with the first known case of heritable human genome editing, the clearest real-world example of the scenarios explored in fiction about designer babies. He Jiankui edited the CCR5 gene in embryos so that the children would resist HIV, introducing changes that could pass to future generations. The film presents him through his own words and those of the journalists, scientists and bioethicists around the case, and its title, taken from James Watson's answer to He, points to the influence of senior figures in the field on an ambitious young researcher. Scientists and Chinese authorities widely condemned the work, and He was fired and imprisoned. Reviews were mixed: critics praised its ethical questions, including whether gene editing makes people better or "makes better people", but faulted its structure and its omission of He's release from prison in April 2022.

The story

Make People Better is a documentary about the Chinese biologist He Jiankui, who in 2018 used CRISPR gene editing on human embryos, leading to the birth of twin girls, nicknamed Lulu and Nana, in secret in October 2018. He aimed to make them resistant to HIV by recreating the CCR5-Δ32 mutation. The film follows how the experiment was revealed by Antonio Regalado of MIT Technology Review, how He announced it in YouTube videos, and the worldwide condemnation that followed, which led to his dismissal and a three-year prison sentence in December 2019. It includes interviews with Regalado, the geneticist George Church, the bioethicist Benjamin Hurlbut and He's former public relations adviser Ryan Farrell. Directed by Cody Sheehy and conceived by the biotechnologist Samira Kiani, it premiered at Hot Docs and was released in December 2022. Its title comes from James Watson's reply when He asked him whether editing babies was a good thing.
