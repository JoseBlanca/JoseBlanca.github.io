The genetics

Genetics drives the case. A pharmaceutical subsidiary of Massive Dynamic took a newborn with an unknown genetic disorder that made light deadly to him, declared him dead, and kept him in a laboratory, where researchers implanted animal genes in his skin; he survived light, but his skin now blends perfectly with his surroundings and makes him invisible. The episode presents this experiment on a child as a corporate violation that turns its victim into a lonely, tragic killer, and Massive Dynamic's executive Nina Sharp ends the episode by having Olivia drugged to erase her memory. In contrast, Walter's analysis of the human and animal DNA left on a victim identifies the boy and solves the case. The science is fanciful: real gene transfer between species cannot produce camouflage that makes a person invisible, and pigment cannot simply be transferred from one person's skin to another's.

The story

The Fringe team investigates a man found dead with his skin and hair turned completely white. Walter finds that the pigment has been extracted from his skin and detects both human and animal DNA. The human DNA belongs to a boy who supposedly died a week after his birth in 1989 of a rare condition that made his skin fatally sensitive to light. Nina Sharp admits that the baby was in fact taken to a pharmaceutical subsidiary of Massive Dynamic and experimented on for years, and that he escaped during a fire. Animal genes implanted in his skin let him survive light but also made him invisible. Now an adult, Eugene steals pigment from his neighbours in a condominium to become visible, although the treatment will kill him. Becoming visible at last, he is greeted by a woman he has secretly loved, and he dies smiling. Meanwhile Olivia, worried about her own capacity to feel, is drugged by Nina's agents.
