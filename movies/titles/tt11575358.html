The genetics

The premise is a laboratory accident: a mutant virus escapes from an Israeli institute for microbiological research and starts an epidemic. The virus is a satirical device rather than a subject; its main function is to turn the blockaded Gaza Strip into a refuge and the usual direction of flight upside down, which gives the film its political comedy. The researchers are never characterised, and the sources do not say whether the mutant virus was engineered or how the outbreak ends. Like many outbreak stories, it draws on public fears about pathogens escaping from research laboratories, though its concerns are the absurdities of borders and politics rather than science.

The story

In this British-Palestinian comedy, co-written and directed by Basil Khalil, a catastrophic accident at an Israeli microbiological research institute releases a mutant virus, ARS, which spreads an epidemic across Israel. Because of Israel's blockade, Gaza ironically becomes the safest place to be. Michael, an Englishman, and his Israeli partner Keren try to flee the country, but their plan fails and they end up stranded in the Gaza Strip, where a local trader, Waheed, shelters them with his family. When the media report their presence, they are forced to devise a new escape plan. The film, conceived in 2009 and produced before the COVID-19 pandemic, won the FIPRESCI Prize at the Toronto International Film Festival.
