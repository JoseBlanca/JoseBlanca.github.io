The genetics

This season-one finale introduces one of the central ideas of The X-Files mythology: secret government experiments that combine human and alien DNA to create hybrids. The scientist at its centre, Dr. Berube, is presented as a genome researcher working covertly for the government; the experiments on terminally ill volunteers appear to cure them, and he is killed when he tries to protect one of his subjects. Genetics provides both the evidence (DNA in the flask that cannot come from any earthly organism) and the conspiracy itself, and later episodes develop the themes of hybrids, toxic alien blood and alien foetuses introduced here. Genome sequencing and experimental gene transfer were real and topical when the episode aired in 1994, during the Human Genome Project, but alien–human hybridisation and the "extraterrestrial" DNA signature are fiction.

The story

After a car chase in Maryland, a fugitive, Dr. William Secare, is shot and escapes into the water; the police find that his blood is green. Deep Throat steers Mulder to the case, which leads to Dr. Terrance Berube, a geneticist who worked on the Human Genome Project and who is soon murdered by a government operative. In Berube's laboratory Mulder finds a flask labelled "Purity Control", whose contents a Georgetown microbiologist identifies as containing DNA of extraterrestrial origin; she and her family are then killed. Mulder discovers a storage facility with men suspended in tanks. Deep Throat explains that Berube had been experimenting on six terminally ill volunteers with alien material and had created alien–human hybrids, and that the project worked better than expected. To rescue the captured Mulder, Scully obtains an alien foetus from a high-security facility; Deep Throat exchanges it and is shot dead. The X-Files are closed.
