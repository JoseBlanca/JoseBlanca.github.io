The genetics

The monsters are an unintended product of agricultural genetic engineering. A meat-processing company sells farmers a genetically engineered cattle feed meant to make beef tastier; parasites in the animals mutate after eating it and become fast-growing, flesh-eating creatures that burst out of their hosts. The practitioner is the corporation, represented by its CEO and its lawyer, who silence the vet who raises the alarm: a standard 'sinister corporation' plot in which profit-driven tampering with the food chain unleashes horror. Reviewers noted that the film's genetic explanation is vague and that the story borrows heavily from Arachnophobia. The idea that a feed could make parasites mutate into large predators is pure monster-movie invention, though the fear of genetically modified food it plays on was a live public concern.

The story

In Host, Missouri, a town dominated by the Tender Meats processing plant, new veterinarian Eli Rudkus finds strange larvae in the stool of a cow belonging to farmer Jacob Long. He traces them to a special feed that the company's CEO, Fletcher Odermatt, has sold to local farmers: it is genetically engineered to make the meat taste better, and it has mutated fluke-like parasites. When Eli tries to warn the town at a public meeting, the company's lawyer, Haley Anderson, shuts him down. The larvae grow fast inside animals and people, burst out of their hosts and turn into large bat-like creatures that devour everything in their path. Eli and Haley are forced to work together, the survivors are chased into the sewers, and Jacob's stockpile of weapons helps save the day before the parasites are destroyed in a huge explosion.
