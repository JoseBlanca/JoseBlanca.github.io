The genetics

DNA is the comic engine of this sequel. Sherman treats his personality as something written in his genes: with his girlfriend's DNA-isolation technique he cuts 'Buddy Love' out of his genome, and the extracted material, mixed with a basset hound's hair, grows into a separate person with dog-like habits. Removing it turns out to damage Sherman's brain, so the cure is to take the DNA back, by drinking it. The youth formula, presented in some sources as a genetic modification, rejuvenates his father and, once tampered with, turns a hamster into a giant. The scientists are sympathetic: Sherman is a well-meaning hero whose rash experiment backfires, and Denise is a capable researcher. A college dean and a drug company that want to profit from the formula are the cynics. None of it is meant seriously: personality traits are not stored in a removable stretch of DNA, and drinking DNA does not change one's genome.

The story

Professor Sherman Klump of Wellman College has developed a youth formula and is in love with his colleague Denise Gaines, a DNA researcher. His obnoxious alter ego, Buddy Love, still takes over his body at the worst moments, so Sherman uses Denise's method to isolate and remove the DNA in which Buddy has manifested, ignoring his assistant's warnings. A dog hair falls into the extracted material and Buddy grows into an independent being, who tries to sell the youth formula to Phleer Pharmaceuticals. Meanwhile the extraction makes Sherman steadily lose his intelligence. Sherman's father secretly drinks the formula, Buddy sabotages a demonstration in which a hamster grows to giant size, and Sherman is fired. Finally the formula reduces Buddy to a glowing mass of genetic material, Sherman drinks it to restore his mind, and he marries Denise.
