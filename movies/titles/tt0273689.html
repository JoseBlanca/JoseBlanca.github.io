The genetics

Cloning is the engine of this apocalyptic romance by Thomas Vinterberg. Elena's managers have three exact copies of her made so that the skating star, and the investments built on her, can carry on without her, and the original may be pushed aside once the copies are trained. The film never shows how the clones were made or who made them; it treats cloning as a business operation run by a faceless entourage, and the copies end up shot dead when they are no longer needed. The sources describe the cloning as a metaphor for the commodification of people, part of the film's wider picture of an emotionally frozen world in which people die of broken hearts. Fully grown, instantly trained adult clones are pure fiction; the film is not interested in the science.

The story

In 2021, as the world's climate goes haywire and snow falls in summer, John, a Polish university lecturer, stops in New York to sign divorce papers with his wife Elena, a world-famous figure-skating champion surrounded by a managing entourage. At her hotel he meets a woman identical to Elena, and Elena, frightened, begs him to stay. He learns that her management has had three clones of her made, officially to relieve her of an exhausting career, though she fears they will dispose of her once the clones are trained. Her brother Michael tells John that the clones are to keep the business going while Elena, who has a heart defect, disappears into a new life with him. With Michael's help the couple escape; the clones are shot dead on the ice, and Michael freezes to death. John and Elena die in each other's arms in the cold, while John's brother Marciello, flying over a frozen world, narrates the end.
