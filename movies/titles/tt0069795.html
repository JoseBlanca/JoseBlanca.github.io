Genetics on Screen

Blackenstein

1973 Film

The genetics

The film updates the Frankenstein story for the age of molecular biology: instead of bringing a corpse to life with electricity, the scientist rebuilds a body with an injectable "DNA solution", and an unexplained "RNA problem" accounts both for a patient's tiger-striped leg and for Eddie's transformation into a monster. Genetics is the pretext for the horror rather than its subject, and the terms are used loosely. Dr. Stein is a celebrated, well-meaning surgeon who wants to help a maimed veteran; although his treatments already show odd side effects, the catastrophe is caused by his jealous assistant's sabotage, and Stein dies at the hands of his creation. Eddie's "regression" to a primitive, ape-like state reflects the popular idea of genetic damage as a step back in evolution, not any real mechanism. The film's Nobel Prize nods to recent history: the deciphering of the genetic code earned Marshall Nirenberg, Har Gobind Khorana and Robert Holley the 1968 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.

The story

Eddie Turner, an African American soldier, has lost his arms and legs to a land mine in Vietnam. His fiancée, the physicist Dr. Winifred Walker, turns to her former teacher Dr. Stein, recently awarded a Nobel Prize for "solving the DNA genetic code", whose "DNA solution" and "laser beam fusion" have already reattached a patient's legs and kept a 90-year-old woman looking 50. Stein gives Eddie new arms, but his assistant Malcomb, rejected by Winifred, sabotages the DNA injections for Eddie's leg surgery with contaminated RNA. Eddie regresses into a brutish, Neanderthal-like creature who leaves the house each night to kill and dismember victims, beginning with an orderly who abused him at the veterans' hospital. After he stops Malcomb from raping Winifred, Eddie kills him, Stein's other patients and, in a struggle, Stein himself. He is finally cornered by police Dobermans, which tear him to pieces.
