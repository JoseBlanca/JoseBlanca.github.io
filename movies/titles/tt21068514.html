The genetics

Human cloning is the film's central twist. Anna is a clone produced by two scientists, Peter and Helen, in a military programme to create people immune to a pandemic virus; she is the 96th attempt, given a fabricated identity and a husband who is really a soldier assigned to watch her, and her baby's immunity is the programme's final test. The scientists' aim, saving the human race, is presented as genuine, and Helen eventually sides with Anna, but the project treats clones as disposable trial runs and lies to them about who they are. According to Helen, the military plans to sell the results to the highest bidder, and its doctor and soldiers are ready to take the baby and kill a child to keep their prize. The film ends with Anna escaping with her daughter. Cloning cannot by itself confer immunity, and a clone would not need 96 attempts to share the donor's genome; the film treats it as a trial-and-error breeding programme.

The story

Anna wakes on a remote island in the North Sea with no memory, pregnant, in the middle of a worldwide pandemic. Helen and Peter nurse her, James is introduced as her husband, and Doctor Henry treats her amnesia with photos and videos, but nothing comes back. After James kills two infected visitors from the mainland, Anna is quarantined and tested, and when she tries to escape she meets Joshua, a boy who recognizes her and resembles her, though Helen calls him her son. Drugged by James, Anna wakes in a laboratory and learns that she is a clone, made by Peter and Helen, scientists working for the military to create humans immune to the virus; she is the 96th attempt, and Joshua is the son of an earlier Anna. Peter dies in her escape. After Anna gives birth to an immune daughter, Henry's soldiers kill Joshua and seize the baby, but Anna and Helen kill the soldiers and Henry and sail away with the child towards islands said to be free of the virus.
