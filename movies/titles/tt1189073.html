The genetics

Genetic engineering is the instrument of an obsessive revenge. Ledgard develops a transgenic artificial skin, presented at a medical symposium as a potential treatment for burns, but he has crossed the ethical line by testing his methods on humans, and when the scientific establishment forbids him to continue, he carries on in secret in his own clinic. The film portrays him as a brilliant, respected surgeon who uses his skill to imprison and remake another human being without consent, combining transgenesis with surgery to change Vicente's sex and appearance into a copy of his dead wife. Science here serves personal grief, control and punishment, and the film raises questions about bodily autonomy and identity, since Vera's body is transformed but Vicente's sense of who he is survives. Almodóvar described it as "a horror story without screams or frights". The transgenic skin and the transformation shown in the film are fictional.

The story

Robert Ledgard, a plastic surgeon in Toledo, has created an artificial skin resistant to burns and insect bites, which he calls "Gal" after his late wife, who was disfigured in a car crash and later took her own life. At a symposium he privately admits to having carried out illegal transgenic experiments on humans and is forbidden to continue. In his secluded estate he keeps a young woman, Vera, captive, helped by his housekeeper Marilia, who is secretly his mother. After Marilia's other son, Zeca, breaks in and rapes Vera, Ledgard kills him. Flashbacks reveal the past: six years earlier, Ledgard's fragile daughter Norma was assaulted at a wedding by a young man, Vicente, and later killed herself. Ledgard kidnapped Vicente and, over years of surgery and transgenesis, transformed him into a woman resembling his dead wife: Vera. Finally Vera kills Ledgard and Marilia, escapes, and returns to her mother's shop to say: "I am Vicente."
