The genetics

A weaponised virus is the engine of this South Korean disaster film. The terrorist, a man dismissed by a pharmaceutical company, obtains a mutated hemorrhagic virus from the Middle East, tests it on animals in his flat and releases it on a transpacific flight, where it kills passengers within hours. The film says little about where the virus was developed, and its practitioners are the bioterrorist, presented as a sociopath, and the unnamed researchers whose experimental antiviral drug saves the survivors at the last moment. Much of the story is about the social response to contagion: countries close their airports, the public demands that the plane not land, and the passengers agree to sacrifice themselves. Released after the COVID-19 pandemic, it prompted one critic to ask whether a virus film was brave or sadistic at such a time. The speed at which the virus kills is far beyond that of real hemorrhagic fevers.

The story

At Incheon airport, Jin-seok, a strange young man, asks staff about popular flights, then implants a cylinder in his armpit and boards flight KI501 to Honolulu, which also carries Jae-hyuk, a former pilot traumatised by an emergency landing, and his sick daughter Soo-min. On the ground, Detective In-ho, whose wife is on the same flight, traces an online terror threat to Jin-seok and finds evidence of animal experiments and a decomposing corpse in his flat. Jin-seok releases a virus in the plane's toilet; passengers begin to bleed and die, and the captain succumbs, leaving Jae-hyuk and the co-pilot to fly the plane. In-ho learns that the virus is a mutated hemorrhagic virus linked to Jin-seok's dismissal from a pharmaceutical company. The United States, Japan and a fearful public refuse to let the plane land, and the passengers resign themselves to staying aloft, until an experimental antiviral drug proves effective and the plane lands at a military airstrip.
