The genetics

Human cloning is the premise, played for comedy. The series reworks Frankenstein, with Victor as a modern Dr. Frankenstein and Ian as his Igor, and adds a military motive: the clone is designed as a weapon for the state, and the scientist is driven by vanity and the wish to join the great names of science. Albert emerges as an adult equipped with implanted "memory engrams", but he behaves like an innocent child, so the comedy rests on the gap between the scientist's design and what he has actually created. The scientific side is fantastical: a real clone would be born as a baby and grow up normally, and memories or skills cannot be programmed into a cloned brain. The series is better understood as a satire of scientific ambition and military patronage than as a story about genetics.

The story

This six-part BBC Three comedy follows Dr. Victor Blenkinsop, an arrogant scientist hungry for scientific glory, who creates the world's first human clone for the military as a prototype super-soldier meant to replace Britain's army. The clone emerges from his birthing tube as an adult with the mind of a naive, clumsy and gentle child, more likely to hug than to shoot. To avoid his ruthless paymaster, Colonel Black of MI7, Victor flees with the clone, later named Albert after Einstein, and his loyal assistant Ian to a small village, where he poses as a travel writer. There he meets Rose Bourne, the landlady of the local pub, whose photographic memory and mathematical gifts suggest that a map of her brain could unlock the clone's dormant military "memory engrams". Victor manipulates Rose to scan her brain and builds a device to "fix" Albert, while Black closes in. The series ends on a cliffhanger.
