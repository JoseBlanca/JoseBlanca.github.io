The genetics

Reproductive technology drives the story. The progenation machines take a tissue sample and generate a fully grown soldier within moments, so that each side can breed armies continuously. Jenny is genetically derived from the Doctor, and the episode is mostly about whether that makes her his daughter: he initially rejects her as a mere product of a machine, then comes to accept her as his child. Jenny is effectively a clone of the Doctor, and the episode turns on the difference between genetic origin and family ties. No scientists appear; the technology is used by soldiers who have forgotten why they fight. Producing an adult person from a tissue sample in moments is pure fantasy, but the episode raises the familiar question of whether shared genes make a family.

The story

The TARDIS takes the Doctor, Martha and Donna to the planet Messaline, where human soldiers force the Doctor's hand into a "progenation machine", which within moments produces a fully grown young woman from his tissue: his daughter, whom Donna names Jenny. Humans and the fish-like Hath are locked in a war, each side breeding new soldiers in the machines, and both are searching for "the Source", which the humans' General Cobb believes will let them wipe out the enemy. Donna works out from dated plaques that the colony ship landed only seven days ago: so many generations have been produced in a week that the war's origins have become myth. The Source turns out to be a terraforming device, and the Doctor releases it and declares the war over. Cobb tries to shoot the Doctor, and Jenny dies taking the bullet. After the Doctor leaves, Jenny revives and departs in a stolen rocket.
