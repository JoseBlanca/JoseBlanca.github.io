The genetics

The film uses genetics as the premise for a creature feature in the tradition of Jurassic Park. Genetisharp's technology turns ancient DNA into living animals almost instantly through "flesh regeneration", and the company is a familiar figure: a commercial biotech firm that shows off a spectacular product without adequate safeguards and loses control of it, with the city paying the price. No scientific detail is offered beyond this. In reality, recreating dinosaurs is not possible: DNA degrades over time, and the older and more degraded a sample is, the more fragmented its DNA, which is why even well-preserved woolly mammoth remains, only thousands of years old, yield little usable genetic material. Dinosaurs died out about 66 million years ago, far beyond the survival of DNA, and current de-extinction projects aim instead to edit the genomes of living relatives of recently extinct species.

The story

In Los Angeles, the biotechnology company Genetisharp uses flesh-regeneration machines to create living dinosaurs and pterosaurs from strands of DNA. The creatures escape and spread through the city: a giant Ceratosaurus, raptor-like Carnotaurus, a Spinosaurus that climbs buildings and a Pteranodon. Gabe Jacobs, played by Treat Williams, is among those caught up in the chaos, which culminates in a final battle around the Hollywood Sign. The dinosaurs are finally destroyed when, drawn by a scent, they are lured into a collapsing building. The film was produced by The Asylum and directed by Joseph J. Lawson.
