The genetics

Genetics frames the story as a fictional medical procedure. In the film's future, people who want meaningful work must have their DNA purified of strong emotions, which the ruling artificial intelligence regards as an obstacle to rational decisions; the process involves reliving past lives so that traumas said to be inscribed in the DNA can be processed and removed. This idea of emotional inheritance, carried from life to life in the genes, is the device that links the film's three periods. No scientist appears: the procedure is administered by the AI system, and the film presents it as a tool of dehumanisation, comparable, critics noted, to Huxley's Brave New World. Its success is shown as a loss, since the purified Louis can no longer love, while the failure of Gabrielle's treatment leaves her humanity intact but in anguish. Emotions have a heritable component, but they cannot be edited out of a person's DNA, and memories of past lives are not stored in genes.

The story

In Bertrand Bonello's film, loosely based on Henry James's The Beast in the Jungle, it is 2044 and artificial intelligence runs most of society and considers humans irrational because of their emotions. To get a more demanding job, Gabrielle must undergo a process that purifies her DNA of strong feelings by making her relive her past lives and their traumas. She meets Louis, another candidate, and feels an immediate bond with him. In 1910 France, Gabrielle is a pianist married to a doll manufacturer; she is drawn to Louis but paralysed by a lifelong fear of an unknown catastrophe, and both drown when the factory floods and burns. In 2014 Los Angeles, she is an underemployed model house-sitting an architect's home, and Louis is a misogynist incel who stalks her and finally kills her. Back in 2044, Gabrielle's purification fails, a rare outcome, but Louis's has succeeded: when they meet again, he says he loves her, and she realises he can no longer truly feel it, and screams.
