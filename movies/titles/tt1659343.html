The genetics

Cloning is used here as a technology for mass-producing soldiers. The government's UniSol programme produces clones of its soldiers, gives them implanted memories and conditions them to obey, and Deveraux's separatists need only stolen hardware to make clones of their own. The film's horror lies in the discovery that the hero's identity, family and grief were manufactured, and it treats a clone as a disposable copy that can be killed and replaced by another one. Scientists appear as technicians of this system: Dr. Su, a former programme scientist, now serves the rebels and offers to cut John's emotional ties surgically. The film does not explain its cloning in genetic terms, and its adult clones with implanted memories and regenerating bodies are pure science fiction. Its connection to genetics is limited to the general idea of copying human beings for use as weapons.

The story

John wakes from a months-long coma to learn that his wife and daughter were murdered in a home invasion, and FBI Agent Gorman tells him that the killer he remembers is Luc Deveraux, a former Universal Soldier (UniSol) who now leads a separatist group of rogue UniSols against the US government. Searching for answers, John meets a stripper, Sarah, who claims to have known him in a life he cannot remember, while he is hunted by Magnus, a cloned UniSol sent after him. John discovers that he has superhuman strength and that his severed fingers grow back. At a riverside cabin he finds an exact duplicate of himself, the man Sarah knew, and kills him. Deveraux's scientist, Dr. Su, reveals that John was synthetically created weeks earlier and that his family is an implanted memory. Driven mad, John kills Deveraux's followers and Deveraux himself, then kills Gorman and takes over the group, deploying a clone of Gorman to infiltrate the government.
