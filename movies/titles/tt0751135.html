The genetics

Cloning and the cataloguing of human beings are central to this season opener of The X-Files, whose title is the German word for "master race". The cloned children, identical boys and girls who copy Samantha Mulder, are shown as speechless drones bred for labour, a vision of human reproduction turned into the production of workers, as in a beehive. The episode also suggests that a public health programme, smallpox vaccination, has been used to record the population. The scientists remain unseen; the programme is run by a secret group of powerful men, the Syndicate, working with alien forces, in keeping with the series' mistrust of government. Reproductive cloning of humans has never been achieved, and clones would be ordinary individuals, not drones; the episode's interest lies in its picture of genetics placed at the service of a hidden elite.

The story

In rural Alberta, a telephone lineman dies after a bee sting while five identical boys silently watch. Mulder escapes the Alien Bounty Hunter with Jeremiah Smith, who promises to show him his missing sister, Samantha. In Canada, Smith leads him to fields tended by identical children, the girls all resembling Samantha at the age she was abducted. Smith explains that the clones are mute drones used as workers to tend the fields. Meanwhile, Scully and Agent Pendrell decode the files the Smiths were compiling and find that they catalogue human beings, linked through X to the government's smallpox eradication programme. The Syndicate sets a trap for X, who is shot and dies after writing the initials of a United Nations official in his own blood. Mulder fails to bring back a clone, the fields are abandoned, and the Smoking Man has the Bounty Hunter heal Mulder's dying mother.
