The genetics

The film's secret is a real wartime atrocity. The experiments that Yūsaku witnesses in Manchuria are those of the Japanese army's biological warfare programme, known historically as Unit 731, although the film does not name it: deliberate infection of civilians with plague, vivisection and the burning of bodies, documented in a doctor's notes and a film. The scientists never appear on screen; their work is presented only as evidence of a crime that the hero risks everything to reveal, and the story weighs national loyalty against the moral duty to expose it. The genetic element is thus limited to the pathogen used as a weapon, which places the film among stories of lab-held and weaponised diseases rather than of genetics proper. The film is fiction, but Unit 731 did carry out plague experiments on prisoners and civilians in occupied China.

The story

In Kiyoshi Kurosawa's historical drama, set in Kobe in 1940, the merchant Yūsaku Fukuhara lives a Westernised life with his wife, Satoko, and is warned by her childhood friend Taiji, now a military police officer, that he is being watched. After a business trip to Manchuria with his nephew Fumio, Yūsaku returns with a mysterious woman, who is later found murdered. Suspicious, Satoko learns the truth: in Manchuria the two men witnessed a secret Japanese army unit conducting biological experiments on civilians, including the deliberate spreading of plague, and they have obtained a doctor's notes and a film of the facilities, corpses and vivisections, which Yūsaku intends to make public abroad. Fumio is arrested and tortured. Satoko hides in a crate on a freighter with the film, but she is betrayed; the reel turns out to be one of Yūsaku's amateur films, since he swapped them. In 1945 Satoko is in a psychiatric hospital, and Yūsaku is reported lost at sea.
