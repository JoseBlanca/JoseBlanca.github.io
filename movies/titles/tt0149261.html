The genetics

Genetic engineering is the trigger of this shark thriller. The researchers' goal is medical and sympathetic, a treatment for Alzheimer's disease, but Susan McAlester breaks the rules to reach it faster: she enlarges the sharks' brains genetically to get more tissue, and by doing so makes them intelligent enough to outwit their captors. The film follows the familiar pattern of the ambitious scientist whose shortcut unleashes disaster. It is somewhat more nuanced than most, since the treatment appears to work and Susan admits her responsibility, destroys her research and dies trying to save the others. The science is fanciful, and the film never explains how the sharks' brains were enlarged or how the protein would restore human brain cells. In reality Alzheimer's disease still has no cure, and available treatments offer only modest relief of symptoms.

The story

At Aquatica, a remote research facility at sea, Dr. Susan McAlester and Dr. Jim Whitlock study mako sharks in search of a cure for Alzheimer's disease, harvesting a protein complex from the sharks' brain tissue that reactivates dormant human brain cells. After a shark escapes and attacks a boat, the company sends executive Russell Franklin to inspect the facility. During a test that proves the treatment works, the largest shark bites off Whitlock's arm, and as a storm hits, a rescue helicopter crashes into the facility, which begins to flood. Susan admits that she and Jim genetically engineered the sharks to enlarge their brains, against protocol, to obtain more of the protein, making them far more intelligent. The sharks pick off the survivors as they climb towards the surface, and Carter, the shark wrangler, realises the animals have been flooding the facility to escape to the open sea. Susan sacrifices herself as bait, and the last shark is blown up.
