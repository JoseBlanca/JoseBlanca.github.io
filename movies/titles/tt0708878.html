The genetics

Comparative genetics is the episode's engine. Two Voth paleontologists find that a human skeleton's genome resembles their own, and Voyager's Doctor identifies 47 shared genetic markers and uses them, in a holodeck simulation, to trace the Voth back to Earth's hadrosaurs. The finding contradicts Voth Doctrine, which holds that the species arose in its own region of space, and the scientist who defends it is tried for heresy and forced to recant. The episode is an explicit retelling of Galileo's trial: geneticists and evolutionary scientists are the heroes of a story about truth against dogma, and the villain is the minister who suppresses the evidence. Real genetics does use shared markers to reconstruct ancestry, but the idea that dinosaurs survived the Cretaceous extinction, left Earth and evolved into a space-faring humanoid species is fiction.

The story

On a volcanic planet, Professor Forra Gegen and his assistant Veer, paleontologists of the saurian Voth species, uncover the skeleton of a Voyager crewman and are struck by how closely its genome resembles their own. Gegen sees support for the heretical 'Distant Origin' theory, that the Voth came from a far-off world; the Ministry of Elders rejects it and charges him with heresy. Gegen tracks down Voyager, boards it under cloak and abducts Chakotay as living evidence. On Voyager, the Doctor finds 47 genetic markers shared by Veer and humans, and a holodeck simulation shows that the Voth descend from Earth's hadrosaurs. A Voth city ship captures Voyager. At Gegen's trial Chakotay defends him, but Minister Odala threatens to destroy the ship and imprison its crew, and Gegen recants. He is reassigned, and Chakotay gives him a globe of Earth.
