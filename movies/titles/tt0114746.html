The genetics

Genetics plays a limited role in 12 Monkeys: the catastrophe is a deadly virus created in a laboratory, and the future scientists want a sample of it in its original form so that they can study it and develop a cure. The film's sympathy lies with the virus's victims, but its central twist concerns who releases it. Suspicion falls on the Army of the Twelve Monkeys, a group of animal rights activists led by the virologist's unstable son, but the real culprit is the virologist's own assistant, Dr. Peters, an apocalyptic who has spoken publicly about humanity's damage to the planet. The scientist who creates the pathogen, Leland Goines, is respected and mainstream; it is an insider with access to his laboratory who turns his work into a weapon. The film thus reflects anxieties about biosecurity and access to dangerous pathogens rather than any detailed science, and the virus itself is never described.

The story

In 2035, after a virus has killed most of humanity, scientists send a prisoner, James Cole, back in time to find the source of the plague, believed to be a group called the Army of the Twelve Monkeys, so that they can obtain a pure sample and develop a cure. Sent by mistake to 1990, Cole is committed to a psychiatric hospital, where he meets psychiatrist Kathryn Railly and a patient, Jeffrey Goines. In 1996, he kidnaps Railly, who gradually comes to believe him, and they trace Jeffrey, now leader of the Army and son of the eminent virologist Leland Goines. The Army proves to be a false lead, an animal rights group that frees the animals from a zoo. At the airport, Railly realises that the real culprit is Dr. Peters, the elder Goines' assistant, who is boarding a plane with the virus. Cole is shot dead trying to stop him, watched by his own younger self.
