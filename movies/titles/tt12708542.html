The genetics

Cloning is the heart of the series. The clone army was grown on Kamino from the genes of Jango Fett, with modifications such as accelerated ageing; the Bad Batch are "defective" clones whose mutations give them enhanced senses, strength or intellect and, crucially, immunity to the programming that turned the clones on the Jedi, while Omega and the adult Emerie Karr are unmodified copies who age normally. The Kaminoan scientist Nala Se, complicit in the clone programme, protects the Batch and Omega in an effort to atone. The Empire treats the clones as disposable, kills off the programme and redirects cloning to Project Necromancer, in which Dr. Hemlock experiments on clones and on children with high "M-counts" to prepare a clone of Palpatine. The series sides with the clones as individuals, and presents cloning as a tool of states that use people as material.

The story

This animated series, a sequel to The Clone Wars, follows Clone Force 99, the "Bad Batch", a squad of clone troopers whose genetic mutations set them apart from the rest of the clone army. When Order 66 turns the clones against their Jedi generals, the Batch's mutations let them resist, and they flee the new Galactic Empire together with Omega, a young girl who is an unaltered clone of Jango Fett, the template of all the clones. Over three seasons they work as mercenaries, lose their sniper Crosshair to Imperial reconditioning, and see the Empire destroy the cloning city on Kamino, replace clones with conscripted stormtroopers and turn cloning to new ends. The Empire's Advanced Science Division under Dr. Royce Hemlock experiments on clones and children for Project Necromancer, a secret plan to clone Emperor Palpatine. In the final season the Batch infiltrates the facility on Mount Tantiss to rescue Omega and other child test subjects.
