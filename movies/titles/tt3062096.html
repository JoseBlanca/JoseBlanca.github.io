The genetics

The MacGuffin is a man-made plague. Bertrand Zobrist, a transhumanist billionaire and genius who believes that only drastic measures can stop overpopulation from ending humanity, has created a virus, Inferno, designed to kill half of the world's population, and hidden it to be released after his death. The film presents him as an unstable fanatic with an apocalyptic vision, and his plan is carried on by his lover, the doctor Sienna Brooks, and his followers. The virus also attracts people who see it as a commodity: the WHO official Christoph Bouchard secretly wants to sell it. Against them, the head of the World Health Organization, Elizabeth Sinskey, works to contain the weapon and succeeds. The film thus treats the deliberate creation of a pandemic as an act of ideological terrorism, framed by debates on overpopulation and transhumanism. Unlike the novel, in which Zobrist's virus sterilises a third of humanity, the film's weapon is a killer plague.

The story

In the third film based on Dan Brown's Robert Langdon novels, the billionaire Bertrand Zobrist, convinced that overpopulation will destroy humanity, creates a plague virus, Inferno, to kill half the world's population, and kills himself in Florence while fleeing armed agents. Langdon, a Harvard symbologist, wakes in a Florence hospital with amnesia and a head wound; the doctor Sienna Brooks helps him escape an assassin. In his pocket is a projector showing a modified version of Botticelli's Map of Hell, a clue left by Zobrist. Pursued by the private security firm working for Zobrist and by World Health Organization agents led by his former lover Elizabeth Sinskey, Langdon follows Dante-related clues through Florence and Venice to Istanbul. Sienna turns out to be Zobrist's lover, determined to release the virus, and the WHO's Christoph Bouchard wants to sell it. In the Basilica Cistern, Sienna detonates a charge to release the virus, but Sinskey has sealed it in a containment unit; Sienna is killed and the virus is secured.
