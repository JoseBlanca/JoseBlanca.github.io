The genetics

Genetics drives the whole series. EmbroGen, a sinister company led by Salko, has cloned Jay several times, keeps the clones in an underground laboratory, tests their intelligence and uses one of them to impersonate him; its Eden II project aims to correct the errors of earlier work and create a world of perfect children, a eugenic goal that gives its head the future of the human race in his hands. Jay himself is revealed to be a chimera with two complete sets of DNA, created by his father, Marshall, a company scientist whom the story first presents as part of the plan and then redeems as he sabotages Eden II. Another scientist, Salko's former professor Maggie Wych, works to stop him. The clones are shown as innocent children who want a family, and one of them gives a kidney to Jay's sister. Human chimeras with two cell lineages do exist naturally, but a chimera does not have twice the number of genes.

The story

In this British children's science-fiction series, twelve-year-old Jay Keaton, a gifted boy, overhears his father, Marshall, talking about him with agents of the corrupt cloning company EmbroGen. Afraid that he is the subject of its experiments, Jay steals files from his father's office and runs away, joined by his friend Mel Banks. Pursued by EmbroGen's operatives Lomas and Boyer, the two discover a laboratory full of Jay's clones, are helped by an eccentric former professor, Maggie Wych, and learn from Jay's uncle Harry that Jay is a chimera with two complete sets of DNA, before finding that Harry works for EmbroGen too. Marshall, forced back to work by the company's head, Salko, is made to help with Eden II, a project to create perfect children, and secretly sabotages it. When Jay's sister Fleecey is badly injured, one of the clones, J2, donates a kidney to her. In the finale, Jay and Mel expose the clones to backers and press, and Salko's arrest turns out to be staged by his own operatives.
