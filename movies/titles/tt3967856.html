The genetics

Okja is a satire of the meat industry built around a genetically modified animal. The Mirando Corporation presents its super pigs as a natural, environmentally friendly discovery, raised by traditional farmers around the world, but the animals are genetically modified, and the green marketing hides intensive breeding, painful experiments and industrial slaughter. The scientist figure is Dr. Johnny Wilcox, a zoologist who acts as the company's spokesman, and the company's leaders, the twin sisters Lucy and Nancy Mirando, are shown as cynical, focused on public image or on profit. The film does not reject genetic engineering as such, but asks what it means to create a sentient animal only as a product; Bong Joon Ho said the film was about capitalism, and he and producer Dooho Choi temporarily became vegans after visiting a slaughterhouse.

The story

In 2007, Lucy Mirando, the new head of the Mirando Corporation, presents herself as an environmentalist and announces a "super pig" programme: 26 piglets are sent to farmers around the world, and ten years later the best specimen will be chosen. In 2017, Mija, a girl living in the South Korean mountains with her grandfather, has grown up with the super pig Okja, a huge, intelligent and affectionate animal. When the company's spokesman, the zoologist Johnny Wilcox, chooses Okja as the winner and takes her to New York, Mija sets out to rescue her. The Animal Liberation Front intercepts the transport and, without Mija's real consent, lets Okja be recaptured with a camera hidden on her, recording how she is forcibly bred and sampled for meat in a laboratory. After the footage is exposed at a public event, Okja is taken to a slaughterhouse, where Mija buys her life from Lucy's twin sister Nancy with a gold pig figurine and returns home with Okja and a rescued piglet.
