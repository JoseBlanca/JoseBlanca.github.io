The genetics

Genetics appears through the Capitol's genetically engineered snakes, creatures bred to attack any scent they do not recognise. They are the weapon of choice of Dr. Volumnia Gaul, the sadistic Head Gamemaker, who uses them first to punish a student and then to massacre tributes in revenge for a rebel attack, and Snow's trick of placing Lucy Gray's scent in their tank decides the outcome of the Games. The engineered animals thus belong to the Capitol's apparatus of cruelty and spectacle, and Gaul, who later mentors Snow in ruthlessness, is the film's emblem of science in the service of power. The film is a dystopian prequel about Snow's moral decay; the snakes are one element of its world rather than its main subject, but they drive key events.

The story

In Panem, eighteen-year-old Coriolanus Snow, a student from a fallen Capitol family, is made a mentor for the 10th Hunger Games in the hope of winning a scholarship. He is assigned Lucy Gray Baird, a singer from District 12. The Games are run by Dean Casca Highbottom and the Head Gamemaker, Dr. Volumnia Gaul, who punishes a classmate who stole Snow's idea by making her reach into a tank of genetically engineered snakes. After rebel bombs hit the arena, Gaul releases a swarm of the snakes on the tributes, and Snow secretly slips a handkerchief with Lucy Gray's scent into their tank so that they spare her; she wins. Caught cheating, Snow is sent to serve as a Peacekeeper in District 12, where he betrays his friend Sejanus to the Capitol, kills a girl and loses Lucy Gray. Back in the Capitol he poisons Highbottom and begins his rise under Gaul.
