The genetics

This episode is a classic mad-scientist story with a genetic twist. Professor Lazarus uses a machine that 'instructs' his genes with sound waves to make him young, but the treatment also unlocks dormant genes, which the Doctor calls an evolutionary throwback that humanity had rejected, and turns him into a monster that must drain other people's life energy to keep his unstable DNA in shape. The Doctor and Martha identify the problem by analysing a DNA sample, and the Doctor finally uses sound to disrupt Lazarus's DNA. Lazarus is presented as a vain, ruthless man who wants to defeat ageing for himself, funded by the sinister Mr. Saxon, and the episode frames his fate as nature punishing hubris, while also giving him a moving fear of death. The idea that genes respond to sound, or that an old genome hides a monster, has no basis in real biology.

The story

The Doctor brings Martha Jones home to London, where her sister Tish works for Professor Richard Lazarus, a 76-year-old scientist who promises to 'change what it means to be human'. At the launch party at Lazarus Laboratories, he enters a capsule that uses sound waves to rejuvenate him; the Doctor prevents an overload, and Lazarus steps out young again. Analysing a DNA sample that Martha took when Lazarus kissed her hand, the Doctor finds that the process has awakened something in his DNA that is changing him into something else. Lazarus turns into a giant scorpion-like creature and kills his partner, Lady Thaw, by draining her life energy, which he needs to stabilise his DNA. After several attacks, the Doctor traps him in Southwark Cathedral and uses the amplified sound of the organ to disrupt his DNA; Lazarus falls to his death.
