The genetics

The plot is driven by a man-made bioweapon, as usual in Resident Evil. The A-virus is a designed system: a base virus that can lie dormant in anyone, an airborne trigger that turns carriers into zombies loyal to their master, and a vaccine reserved for the villain's associates. Arias, a vengeful bioterrorist, uses it as a weapon of mass murder and a tool of control, and even creates a vaccine-resistant strain to infect Rebecca. Against him stands Rebecca Chambers, a sympathetic virologist whose research, vaccine and immune blood help the heroes contain the outbreak, so the film contrasts science used for terror with science used for protection. A virus that makes its victims obey one person, or merges two bodies into a monster, is pure fiction, although the film's idea of a dormant infection activated later has loose analogies in real viruses.

The story

In this computer-animated film based on the video game series, agent Chris Redfield of the Bioterrorism Security Assessment Alliance hunts Glenn Arias, a former CIA operative turned arms dealer who seeks revenge on the United States for a drone strike that killed his family at his wedding. In Chicago, Professor Rebecca Chambers is studying a new bioweapon, the Animality Virus, when Arias's associates release it in her lab; she develops a vaccine and links the virus to the Las Plagas parasite. With the help of the troubled agent Leon S. Kennedy, Chris tries to stop Arias's plan to spread the virus over New York. Arias kidnaps Rebecca, who resembles his dead wife, and infects her with a vaccine-resistant strain. The heroes stop the tankers spreading the virus, kill Arias and the Tyrant monster formed from his body, cure Rebecca and distribute the antidote across the city.
