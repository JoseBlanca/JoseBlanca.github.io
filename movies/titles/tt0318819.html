The genetics

The monster is an artificial organism created in the laboratory. Saeko Misaki, a chief researcher at a biomedical institute, synthesizes WXIII from the cancer cells of her dead daughter combined with the 'Nishiwaki Cell' discovered by her father; the creature keeps mammalian and human traits, which the film uses to suggest it is in some sense her child. Her motive is grief: an obsessive attempt to bring her daughter back. The project is funded by a Japanese general and a US colonel as part of a weapons series, and the military conspirators intend to use it to attack strategic targets and spread panic. The film thus pairs a tragic, transgressive experiment with the military exploitation of biological research, and the creature's killings drive the story. Growing a giant intelligent animal from human tumour cells is fantasy; the cell line recalls immortal cancer lines such as HeLa, but the film does not make that link explicit.

The story

In 2000, after a military transport plane crashes into Tokyo Bay, Labors (giant work robots) and people around the bay start being attacked. Two police detectives, the veteran Shinichiro Hata and the young Takeshi Kusumi, investigate with the help of Captain Goto's Special Vehicles Section 2. Clues lead them to suspicious cargo from the crashed plane and, after a couple is killed near the Babylon industrial zone, to a huge creature that hides in the bay and is drawn to the ultrasonic hum of Labor motors. The trail points to a biological weapons project backed by a Japanese Self-Defense Forces general and a US Army colonel, and to Saeko Misaki, a researcher at the Tokyo Biomedical Research Institute whose six-year-old daughter died of cancer. Misaki has grown the monster, called WXIII, from her daughter's cancer cells and calls it 'that child'. In the final battle SV2's Labors strip the creature's armour and Isao Ota kills it with a single virus-warhead round.
