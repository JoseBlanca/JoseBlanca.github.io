The genetics

The episode introduces one of Star Trek's most lasting ideas: humans deliberately improved by science who become tyrants. In its backstory, Khan and his followers were produced by selective breeding to create perfect humans; they took power over more than a third of the planet in the Eugenics Wars, which the franchise's own chronology dates to 1992–1996. The scientists who created them never appear; the story focuses on the creation, whose physical and mental superiority goes hand in hand with a will to rule. Sociologist William Sims Bainbridge argued that selective breeding could not produce supermen so quickly and that direct genetic engineering would be more plausible. Later Star Trek productions adopted that view, recasting the "augments" as genetically engineered, and established that human genetic enhancement is illegal in the Federation, as in the Deep Space Nine episode "Doctor Bashir, I Presume?".

The story

The USS Enterprise finds the SS Botany Bay, a derelict 20th-century ship carrying dozens of people in suspended animation. Their leader, brought aboard for medical care, is identified by Spock as Khan Noonien Singh, a 20th-century dictator and one of a group of genetically altered "superpeople" who once ruled much of Earth. Khan persuades Lieutenant Marla McGivers to help him, revives his followers and seizes the ship, threatening to suffocate Captain Kirk in a decompression chamber until the crew submits. McGivers frees Kirk, who regains control of the Enterprise and overpowers Khan in a fistfight in Engineering. At a hearing, Kirk exiles Khan and his people to the harsh planet Ceti Alpha V; Khan accepts, saying it is better to reign in hell than serve in heaven, and McGivers chooses to go with him. The story continues in the film Star Trek II: The Wrath of Khan (1982).
