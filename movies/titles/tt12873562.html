The genetics

The genetic element is a consumer DNA test that sets the plot in motion. Evie sends off a test kit after her mother's death, and the match with a distant cousin reveals her descent from an English aristocratic family and a hidden interracial relationship in the past. The family uses this genetic link to lure her into a centuries-old pact in which its daughters become Dracula's brides, and a racist butler hates her for her mixed heritage. The film thus plays on the unforeseen consequences of genealogical DNA testing, which can reveal family secrets and put people in contact with relatives they never knew, though here what follows is supernatural horror. No scientists are involved, and vampirism is presented as a curse, not a biological condition.

The story

After her mother's death, Evie Jackson, a struggling artist working for a catering company in New York, takes a DNA test and discovers distant relatives in England. Her cousin Oliver tells her that her great-grandmother, Emmaline Alexander, had a secret child with a Black footman, and invites her to a family wedding at New Carfax Abbey in Whitby. There Evie is charmed by the manor's owner, Walter De Ville, while maids disappear. At the rehearsal dinner Walter announces that he will marry Evie, and the hosts reveal themselves as vampires: for centuries the Alexanders and two other families have supplied Walter with brides in exchange for wealth and protection, and the DNA test allowed them to find a woman of their line. Walter is Dracula. Evie escapes, is recaptured and forced into the wedding, but drinks his blood, kills him and burns the abbey, then sets out to punish Oliver.
