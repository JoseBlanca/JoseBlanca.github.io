The genetics

Human cloning is the premise. A secret project clones a child from DNA taken from the Shroud of Turin, in an attempt to reproduce Christ, and gives him to a woman desperate to become a mother; the result is a soulless Antichrist who brings disasters on the world. The film thus combines two worries about cloning, that copying a person is playing God and that a clone may lack a soul, in an apocalyptic religious frame. The only available source is a brief summary, so little is known about how the scientists are portrayed beyond Dr. Gibson's confession of the secret. In reality, any DNA surviving on an ancient cloth would be degraded and contaminated, far from what cloning would need, and the authenticity of the Shroud itself is disputed.

The story

In this low-budget American thriller, also released as Devil's Angel, Dr. Kimberly Gabriel is a seemingly infertile young woman who will do almost anything to have a child. Through what the film calls the miracle of modern science, she obtains a cloned son, David. All seems well until David's seventh birthday, when disasters begin to strike the earth and appear to come from the boy himself. Dr. Roger Gibson then confesses a long-buried secret: the DNA used to clone David came from the Shroud of Turin, the cloth believed to have wrapped the body of Jesus. The secret cloning project was an attempt to bring about the Second Coming, but the child was born without a soul and is instead the Antichrist. The film presents itself as an interpretation of the Book of Revelation and the End Times.
