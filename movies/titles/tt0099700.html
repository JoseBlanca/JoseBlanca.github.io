The genetics

Genetic engineering is the film's main satirical target. Splice O' Life is a corporate laboratory inside Clamp's tower, a building that parodies late-1980s commercial excess (Clamp was modelled on Donald Trump and Ted Turner), and its head, Dr. Catheter, is played by Christopher Lee as an openly sinister scientist, at director Joe Dante's request. His shelves of gene-altering serums give the second half of the film its set pieces: each gremlin that drinks one instantly takes on new traits, such as a bat's wings, a spider's body, plant tissue, pure electricity or human-level intelligence, producing comic hybrids of gremlin with animal, vegetable or even a female identity. The science is deliberately cartoonish: real genetic modification acts on cells or embryos and takes effect over generations or long treatments, not as a drink that transforms an adult animal in seconds. The film uses the idea to mock both corporate biotechnology and the monster-movie trope of the mad scientist.

The story

After the death of his owner, Mr. Wing, the mogwai Gizmo is captured by scientists from Splice O' Life, a genetic engineering laboratory in the Clamp Centre, a Manhattan skyscraper owned by the eccentric billionaire Daniel Clamp. Billy Peltzer and Kate Beringer, who now work in the building, rescue him, but Gizmo gets wet and spawns new mogwai, which eat after midnight and turn into gremlins. The gremlins overrun the tower, and in the laboratory they drink Dr. Catheter's experimental serums: one becomes a talking, intelligent Brain Gremlin, others turn into a spider-like creature, a bat, a vegetable gremlin, a female gremlin and an electric gremlin, which kills Catheter. As the gremlins prepare to break out into the city, Billy traps the electric gremlin and uses it, together with the building's sprinklers and fire hoses, to destroy the rest, while Gizmo kills the spider gremlin with a flaming arrow.
