The genetics

Shell satirises the beauty and entertainment industries through a fictional genetic rejuvenation treatment. The company's procedure, developed by its lead scientist Dr. Brand and administered by Dr. Hubert, makes ageing clients look young again, but its side effects range from moles and shedding skin to vomiting and, eventually, transformation into monstrous crustaceans. The company, embodied by its glamorous CEO, Zoe Shannon, hides these effects, buys silence and has inconvenient people killed; Hubert, the scientist who reassures patients and later tries to treat them, is a more sympathetic but compromised figure. The genetics is a vehicle for body horror and for criticism of ageism and beauty standards, and critics compared the film to The Substance. In the bitter ending the company survives its exposure. No real genetic treatment can rejuvenate the body, although research on the genetics of ageing is an active field.

The story

Ageing actress Samantha Lake, struggling with ageism and body-shaming, is urged by a producer to try a rejuvenating treatment at Shell, a beauty company that uses genetic technology on celebrities. Encouraged by Chloe Benson, a young actress she once babysat, and by the gentle Dr. Hubert, she goes ahead and emerges looking younger. Her career and social life flourish, and Shell's CEO, Zoe Shannon, befriends her. Then moles appear, Chloe goes missing and Sam starts vomiting black bile on set. When Zoe tries to keep her quiet, Sam flees to Hubert, who has been fired; he treats her, but she finds Chloe locked in his house, turned into a feral creature. Hubert is murdered, and Sam is taken to Shell's headquarters, where lead scientist Dr. Brand explains the side effects. Chloe, now a giant lobster, kills Shell's staff and triggers a procedure that makes Zoe explode. Sam publishes a book denouncing Shell, which still thrives.
