The genetics

The film's conspiracy rests on an engineered pathogen: an MIT scientist, Dr. Ann Kendall, has found proof that the AIDS virus was artificially manufactured and that a government agency enabled its spread, and the agency kills activists, journalists and researchers to bury the evidence. The idea echoes conspiracy theories about the man-made origin of HIV that circulated in the 1980s and 1990s, which have no scientific basis; HIV arose by natural transmission from primates. The film does not say who made the virus, how or why, a gap the Moria review criticises. Science is represented by Kendall, a lonely but principled researcher who becomes the moral centre of the story, set against a sinister state agency that also 'resurrects' and reprograms a hitman by unexplained means.

The story

Harlin Garrett, a mafia hitman left for dead by his employers, wakes with amnesia in a secret facility run by a covert government agency under the menacing Mr. Green, who tells him he died in a fire and was brought back to life. Given the choice of working for the agency or being disposed of, Garrett is trained to kill again. His missions target people connected to HIV research and the gay community: a gay rights activist, whom he is to kill while posing as his chauffeur, and a journalist, both to be made to look like accidents. His key target is Dr. Ann Kendall, an MIT scientist who has found proof that the AIDS virus was artificially manufactured and that the government enabled its spread; he is to seduce her to learn what she knows and then kill her. Instead Garrett falls for Ann, deals with her jealous boyfriend, and turns against Green and the agency to protect her.
