The genetics

There is no genetics in the film; it belongs here as a story that leaves open whether Kevin was born bad or made bad. Told through Eva's memories, it shows a child who seems hostile from birth, which points to an innate nature, but also a reluctant mother who struggles to love him and once injures him, which points to damage done at home. Because Eva is an unreliable narrator, the film never settles the question and gives weight to both: critics read it as a 'bad seed' story, yet it also invites the viewer to suspect the parenting, and Kevin's own final uncertainty about his motives leaves both explanations standing. Research on children with severe conduct problems finds that a child's temperament and the parents' responses shape each other over time, so neither inborn nature nor upbringing alone accounts for such a child.

The story

Eva Khatchadourian, a former travel writer, lives alone, shunned by her neighbours, and visits her son Kevin in prison after he has killed students at his high school. In flashbacks she recalls her reluctant motherhood. From infancy Kevin seemed to target her: he cried incessantly only with her, rejected her affection and behaved like a model son in front of his indulgent father, Franklin, who dismissed her worries. Once, losing her temper, Eva broke Kevin's arm. After the birth of a sister, Celia, the girl's guinea pig disappears and she loses an eye in an accident with drain cleaner while Kevin is watching her, but Franklin defends him. Shortly before turning sixteen, Kevin kills Franklin and Celia at home and then classmates trapped in the school gym with his bow and arrows. Two years later Eva asks him why; he says he is no longer sure.
