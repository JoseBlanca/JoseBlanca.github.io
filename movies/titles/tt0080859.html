The genetics

The film is a social-issue drama about the risks and regulation of recombinant DNA research. Henderson is the ambitious scientist absorbed by the prospect of a breakthrough and dismissive of possible hazards, while the mayor, the townspeople and the visiting Tedeschi stand for public oversight; bringing in a veteran of the Manhattan Project frames genetic engineering as a technology whose consequences could escape its creators. One reviewer singles out the still unsettling idea of an engineered sample being flushed into the water supply. The premise closely echoes real events: in 1976 the mayor of Cambridge, Massachusetts, Alfred Vellucci, tried to ban genetic engineering at Harvard and MIT for fear that researchers could accidentally produce hazardous organisms, which led to months of public hearings with scientists before a panel of residents rejected the ban. As reviewers point out, the techniques that alarmed the public in 1980 have since become routine laboratory tools.

The story

Dr. Tom Henderson, a Nobel Prize-winning scientist, carries out genetic engineering experiments at a college in a small American town, assisted by Dr. Louise Casimir, his former girlfriend, whose husband Pete is a hard-drinking writer. Henderson is close to a major breakthrough and shows little concern for ethics. As questions arise about the safety of the work, the town's new mayor, Frank Bellona, bans the experiments and calls a public hearing, and Professor Leo Tedeschi, a former Manhattan Project scientist, arrives to bring some oversight to the research. Alongside the public dispute, the film follows the strained relationships between Henderson, Louise and Pete. Despite the title, reviewers note that no creature ever appears: the "monster" remains a theoretical possibility, and this CBS television film, directed by Waris Hussein from a script by Ernest Kinoy, is a talk-driven drama rather than a horror story.
