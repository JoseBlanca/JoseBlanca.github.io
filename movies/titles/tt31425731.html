The genetics

The plot turns on a secret military cloning programme. Colonel Kurtz considered Jeff the perfect soldier except for his empathy, which got him court-martialled for refusing to kill a child, so he and the billionaire scientist Simon Maddox secretly collected Jeff's DNA during routine drug tests to mass-produce emotionless clones, incapable of trauma or moral hesitation. CJ, the first successful clone, and the feral clones in the facility are treated as weapons, but the film insists that CJ, who shares Jeff's genes, can develop humanity through love: he refuses to kill Jeff and becomes part of a found family. The scientist and the colonel are comic villains, and the film mocks the military dream of soldiers without feelings. The premise that cloning can produce child soldiers without emotions has no basis in biology: a clone is simply a genetic twin, whose personality would develop like anyone else's.

The story

Brian, a laid-off accountant, has become a stay-at-home father to his bullied stepson Lucas. Hoping to help the boy make friends, he arranges a playdate with Jeff, a hyper-athletic new neighbour, and his strangely strong, emotionally stunted son CJ. At an indoor playground they are attacked by armed mercenaries and flee in a stolen minivan. Jeff, a disgraced former Delta Force soldier, admits that CJ is not his son: he found the boy locked up in the secret facility where he worked as a guard and freed him. A captured employee reveals that CJ is Clone Jeff, the first successful clone made from Jeff's DNA by the billionaire scientist Simon Maddox and Jeff's former commander, Colonel Kurtz, who want emotionless super-soldiers. After Brian is forced to betray them and CJ is recaptured, the two fathers storm a facility full of feral clones. CJ refuses an order to kill Jeff, Jeff blows up the facility, and the two families walk away together.
