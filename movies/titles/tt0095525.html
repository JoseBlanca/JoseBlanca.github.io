The genetics

Genetic engineering appears as the original sin of a seemingly idyllic society. Gandahar's scientists created both the Deformed, mutants born of failed experiments and pushed out of sight, and Metamorphis, a giant brain they abandoned in the sea when it grew too fast and became violent. The threat that falls on Gandahar is therefore the delayed consequence of its own research. The scientists themselves are seen only through these results: they act for the state, experiment without responsibility and discard what does not please them. The film is sympathetic to the outcasts, since the Deformed, despised as undesirable, end up saving the population. Metamorphis's need to absorb other people's cells because his own can no longer regenerate is pure fantasy, but René Laloux's animated film, based on a 1969 novel by Jean-Pierre Andrevon, uses it as a fable about scientific hubris and the treatment of those judged imperfect.

The story

The peaceful land of Gandahar is attacked by the Men of Metal, an army of automata that turn villagers to stone and carry them away. The Council of Women sends the young Sylvain to investigate. On his way he meets the Deformed, a community of mutant beings created by accident in genetic experiments by Gandahar's scientists and then abandoned. With Airelle, a woman he rescues, he finds the robots' base and is taken to Metamorphis, a giant brain living in the ocean, another failed creation of the same scientists. Metamorphis reveals that the Men of Metal come from the future through a portal and asks Sylvain to kill him a thousand years later. When Sylvain wakes from stasis, he learns that the aged Metamorphis has sent his robots back in time to absorb the Gandaharians' cells and prolong his life. With the Deformed's help, Sylvain kills him with a special syringe.
