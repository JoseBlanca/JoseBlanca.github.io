The Post-Modern Prometheus

The genetics

The episode is a comic retelling of Frankenstein in which the creator is a geneticist. Dr. Pollidori's research on Hox genes in Drosophila is based on real developmental biology: mutations in these genes, which control the body plan, can make flies grow the wrong appendages, such as legs where antennae should be. The episode extrapolates from this to a deformed human created in the laboratory, and Pollidori, like Victor Frankenstein, rejects his creation as "a mistake" and refuses to give him a mate. The scientist is the real villain, vain and violent, while the "monster" turns out to be gentle and is finally accepted by the town. His surrogate father's attempts to imitate the experiments with farm animals, and the women impregnated while unconscious, add a darker strand to the comedy. Producing a viable human with such radical body-plan changes is far beyond any real technique, but the fly images reflect genuine experiments from the history of genetics.

The story

Presented as a comic book in black and white, the episode sends Mulder and Scully to rural Albion, Indiana, where Shaineh Berkowitz says she was impregnated while unconscious 18 years ago and has just been attacked again. Locals describe a creature with a lumpy head and two mouths, which her son Izzy has turned into a comic character, "The Great Mutato". The agents meet geneticist Francis Pollidori, who shows them fruit flies with legs growing from their mouths produced by manipulating Hox genes and says such experiments could in theory be done on humans. After Pollidori's wife is attacked, suspicion falls on his father, whom Pollidori then murders. Hunted by a mob, Mutato explains that he is a failed genetic experiment by Pollidori, rescued and raised by the old man, who tried to create a mate for him using farm animals. Pollidori refuses to make him a companion and is arrested; the agents take Mutato to a Cher concert.
