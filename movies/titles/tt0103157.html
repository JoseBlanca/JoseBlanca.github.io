The genetics

The Unborn is a horror film in the tradition of Rosemary's Baby and It's Alive, with genetic engineering in place of the devil. Its fear is of reproductive medicine: in vitro fertilisation, still relatively new when the film was made, becomes the route by which a doctor secretly manipulates embryos in the hope of creating superior children, and the results are psychopathic or monstrous offspring. Dr. Meyerling is a classic mad scientist, running a private fertility clinic and deceiving his patients for his own eugenic project. Reviewers have noted that the film draws on the Human Genome Project, launched in 1990 and much in the news at the time, and a montage pairs a headline about cracking the DNA code with tabloid stories. The film's genetics is vague and never explained. In reality, in vitro fertilisation and genetic modification of embryos are very different things. Editing human embryos for reproduction became a real controversy only in 2018, when He Jiankui announced the birth of gene-edited twins, work widely condemned as a breach of accepted ethical norms.

The story

Virginia Marshall, a children's book author, and her husband Brad have been unable to conceive. They join an experimental in vitro fertilisation programme run by Dr. Richard Meyerling, and Virginia quickly becomes pregnant. The pregnancy goes badly: the regimen Meyerling prescribes makes her ill, and she becomes increasingly unstable and paranoid. She learns that other women treated at the clinic had terrible outcomes, including children who grew up cold and psychopathic, and she comes to believe that Meyerling is secretly carrying out unauthorised genetic experiments on the embryos he implants, aiming to create superior children. Her fears prove justified, and the pregnancy ends with the birth of a monstrous baby. The film was directed by Rodman Flender.
