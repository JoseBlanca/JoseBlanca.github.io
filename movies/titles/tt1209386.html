The genetics

Genetics is marginal in this series, which is about memory rather than genes. Its central technology stores minds on chips and moves them between bodies, and the French Wikipedia notes that artificial bodies and advanced cloning are part of this world. Cloning matters in one plot revelation: the mysterious leader of the Issoudan movement, Dada-sama, turns out to be three aged clones of Warp, Kaiba's true identity, seeking to take his place. The series' questions about identity, the copying and editing of memories, and the exploitation of the poor's bodies by the rich are explored through memory technology, not through genetic science.

The story

In a universe where memories are stored on chips and minds can move between bodies, the rich above a barrier of electrical storms trade bodies and memories for longevity and pleasure, while the poor below sell their bodies to survive. Kaiba wakes with no memories, a hole in his chest and a locket picturing an unknown girl. Pursued by people who want his body, he flees aboard the freight ship Neuron, hiding his mind in a stuffed toy, then in the body of Chroniko, a poor girl who sold her body and was killed by a corrupt doctor; the ship's sadistic sheriff Vanilla falls in love with him and dies protecting him. Meanwhile Neiro, the girl in the locket, works as a terrorist for the Issoudan movement against the trade in bodies and memories. Kaiba discovers that he is Warp, that the Issoudan leader Dada-sama is three aged clones of Warp, and is finally reunited with Neiro.
