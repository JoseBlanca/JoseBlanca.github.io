The genetics

A cloning plot is the twist that turns this feminist martial-arts comedy into a genre caper. The suitor, Salim, is a geneticist, and his mother Raheela runs a secret laboratory in her mansion: the women invited to her party are covertly scanned and tested, Lena is selected for her fertility and strong uterus, and the marriage is a scheme to make her the surrogate for a clone of Raheela. Salim's first wife died carrying an earlier clone. The geneticist and his mother are comic but sinister villains, and the film uses the plot to satirise the pressure on young women to marry well and the way their bodies are valued. Among its acknowledged influences is Get Out, another social satire with a body-snatching twist. Cloning by implanting an embryo made from an adult's cell in a surrogate is the real technique used in animals, though it has never been done in humans, and a clone would be a genetic twin, not a continuation of Raheela.

The story

Ria Khan, a British-Pakistani schoolgirl in London, dreams of becoming a stuntwoman and films martial-arts videos with her older sister Lena, an art-school dropout. At an Eid party at the mansion of Raheela, the queen of their mother's social circle, Lena meets Raheela's son Salim, a successful geneticist, and after a whirlwind romance agrees to marry him and move to Singapore. Convinced that something is wrong, Ria and her friends Clara and Alba spy on Salim and try to sabotage the match, which only drives the sisters apart. Visiting Raheela to apologise, Ria stumbles on a secret laboratory and learns that every eligible woman at the party was secretly scanned and that Lena was chosen for her fertility. At the wedding Ria discovers the full plan: Lena is to carry a clone of Raheela, as Salim's first wife did before dying. Ria, her friends and family fight the guests, defeat Raheela and Salim, and rescue Lena.
