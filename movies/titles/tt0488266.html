The genetics

The series' monsters are products of biological engineering. The Chronos Corporation turns its own employees and soldiers into Zoanoids, people who can transform into powerful creatures, refines them through an optimization process, and uses those who fail it, the Lost Numbers, as expendable test subjects for genetic research. It also experiments on captured people, such as the journalist Masaki Murakami, in programmes to create more powerful beings. The Guyver units themselves are a living alien armour that bonds with its host. Chronos is the villain throughout: a corporation that pursues world domination through human experimentation and hides its secret war from the public. The source used here is brief and does not explain the biology or the final revelations about the origins of Chronos and the Guyvers. Turning people into transforming monsters is fantasy with no basis in real genetic engineering.

The story

The secret Chronos Corporation, which has plans for the world, has biologically engineered employees and soldiers who can turn into powerful monsters called Zoanoids. A test-type Zoanoid escapes with three alien devices that Chronos was studying, the Guyver units, and blows himself up, scattering them. High school student Shō Fukamachi finds one and accidentally activates it; it merges with him as a living armour that gives him great strength and weapons. Chronos sends Zoanoids to recover the unit or cut it from his body, endangering his friend Tetsurō and Tetsurō's sister Mizuki, whom Shō loves. As the battles escalate, hidden from the public by Chronos's influence over politics and the media, Shō meets other Guyver hosts, including the ambiguous Agito Makishima, son of the former head of Chronos Japan, and the journalist Masaki Murakami, used as a test subject by Chronos, and he uncovers the origins of the Guyvers, the Zoanoids and Chronos.
