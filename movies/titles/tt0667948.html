Seeds of Destruction

Spain: Las semillas de la destrucción

2000 Episode

The Outer Limits, season 6, episode 7

IMDb

The genetics

Genetically modified crops are the subject. The episode imagines an engineered corn whose trait escapes into a wild plant and then into animals and humans, causing tumours in those with high growth-hormone levels, and it ends on a warning that the contamination cannot be contained. The scientist-executives of Macroseed are driven by profit, conceal what they know and are ready to kill to protect the product, while the investigator is a local veterinarian. The episode aired amid public controversy over genetically modified crops, including the StarLink corn recall of 2000 and disputed reports of transgenes spreading into traditional maize in Mexico. Gene flow from crops to related plants is a real subject of study, but a transgene jumping from corn to an unrelated weed and then into people, where it causes tumours, has no basis in science. The episode is best read as an expression of the era's anxieties about agricultural biotechnology rather than as plausible science.

The story

The biotechnology company Macroseed chooses the farming town of Hobson to test TX-40, a fast-growing genetically engineered corn that promises bigger harvests and great profits. The local veterinarian, Dr. Linda Andrews, grows suspicious when a farmer brings in a dead cat from his field with grotesque mutations, including a tumour that sprouts a fifth leg. Aggressive tumours then appear in people, including an old man, a teenage boy and her own nephew. Her husband Rex and her lover Harold Zimmer, both Macroseed employees, insist the corn is safe and warn that her inquiries could ruin the town's new prosperity. Obtaining the company's test data, she and the town doctor find that TX-40 has spread from the corn to milkweed and from there to people with high levels of growth hormone. Harold admits he knew, shoots the doctor and tries to kill Linda, but Rex stops him. Workers burn the crops, but a crow carries off a piece of contaminated plant.
