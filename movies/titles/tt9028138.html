The genetics

Genetics and eugenics are the film's subject, and it takes a strongly critical position. It presents modern genetics as at risk of becoming a resurgence of eugenics, argues that the concept of the gene may one day be seen in the same unfavourable light as "royal blood", and claims that genetic research has been and still is misused by powerful groups to justify mistreatment of the least fortunate and to resist progress towards racial and gender equality. Reviewers described it as a call to arms against genetic determinism; Ralph Nader and the sociologist Barbara Katz Rothman praised it, while The Hollywood Reporter's review was more mixed. The history of American eugenics, including forced sterilisation, is well documented. The film's broader claims about contemporary genetics are presented as an advocacy argument rather than a balanced survey of the field.

The story

A Dangerous Idea: Eugenics, Genetics and the American Dream is a feature-length documentary directed by Stephanie Welch and distributed by Bullfrog Films. It traces the idea of biological determinism, the belief that groups and individuals are innately superior or inferior and so more or less deserving of rights, through American history. It argues that this idea was used early in the country's history to deny rights to women, Black Americans and Native Americans, and that in the 20th century belief in an all-powerful "gene" that predetermines a person's worth gave new justification for the eugenics movement and state-sanctioned abuses against the poor and vulnerable, such as involuntary sterilisation. The film then turns to the present, arguing that similar claims are being revived in debates about inequality. Interviewees include social commentators such as Van Jones and Robert Reich, alongside scientists from several fields.
