The genetics

As in the 1982 film, genetics shapes the antagonist: Khan is one of the engineered superhumans of the franchise's "Eugenics Wars", revived three centuries later. This version stresses how the establishment exploits him. A Starfleet admiral wakes him, gives him a false identity and uses his superior intellect to build weapons for a planned war, holding his crew hostage. Khan is both a victim of that manipulation and a ruthless killer who regards ordinary humans as inferior. His engineered biology also drives the climax: his blood has regenerative properties that bring Kirk back from the dead, a fantasy of enhancement as a cure-all. No scientist designs anything on screen; the film's interest is in the danger of powerful people using engineered humans as tools. Real genetic engineering of people remains highly restricted: the only known gene-edited babies, born in 2018, led to a prison sentence for the scientist involved and to tighter laws in China.

The story

In 2259, after Kirk is demoted for breaking the Prime Directive, a Starfleet agent working for John Harrison bombs a secret Section 31 installation in London, and Harrison then kills Admiral Pike and other officers before fleeing to the Klingon homeworld. Admiral Marcus sends Kirk to kill him with 72 experimental torpedoes, but Kirk captures him instead. Harrison reveals that he is Khan Noonien Singh, a genetically engineered superhuman woken by Marcus from centuries of cryogenic sleep and forced to design weapons under threat to his frozen crew, hidden inside the torpedoes. Marcus, who wants war with the Klingons, attacks the Enterprise with the warship Vengeance; Khan helps Kirk board it, then kills Marcus and takes control. Spock tricks Khan, and both ships fall towards Earth. Kirk dies of radiation saving the Enterprise. Khan crashes into San Francisco and is caught by Spock, and his regenerative blood brings Kirk back to life.
