The genetics

Genetic engineering is little more than the pretext for the monsters. The scorpions are hybrids created by a research team, and one scientist explains that they were crossed with DNA from prehistoric giant scorpions, a choice reviewers note the film never justifies. Scientists appear in several roles: an older scientist who mostly delivers exposition, a female researcher who is one of the heroes, and a treacherous colleague who sells out to a rival corporation and endangers everyone to save the creatures. The film thus repeats the familiar pattern of the genetic monster unleashed by commercial greed rather than by research itself. Scientifically it is implausible: DNA from extinct arthropods has not been recovered, and giant body size in insects and scorpions is constrained by how they breathe and by their exoskeletons, not just by their genes.

The story

A low-budget creature feature. After a scheduled flight is cancelled, a group of passengers boards a charter from Melbourne, Australia, to Los Angeles. In the hold is a cargo of genetically engineered scorpion hybrids that a team of scientists is taking to the United States to find funding for their research. A double-dealing scientist on the team, working for a rival company, sneaks into the hold to steal the samples, and turbulence smashes the jars. The scorpions, grown to giant size, attack passengers and crew, kill most of the flight crew and chew through vital wiring. The surviving passengers, led by the pilot and one of the scientists, must fight off the creatures and bring the crippled plane down safely, while the traitor tries to protect the scorpions so that he can sell them.
