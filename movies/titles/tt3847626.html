The genetics

Code of a Killer dramatises a key moment in the history of forensic genetics, with a real geneticist, Alec Jeffreys, as one of its two protagonists. It shows how he discovered DNA fingerprinting at Leicester in 1984 after noticing that a DNA experiment revealed both similarities and differences among members of a technician's family, how the method was first used in paternity and immigration cases, and how it was adapted to old crime-scene samples. The drama also shows the limits of the new science: the slow pace of mass testing and the police's doubts. Its most striking point is historically accurate: the first use of DNA in a murder investigation cleared an innocent suspect, a teenager who had confessed falsely, before it identified Colin Pitchfork, the first person convicted of murder using DNA evidence, in 1988. Several critics found the portrayal of Jeffreys as an obsessive, absent-minded "boffin" a cliché. The series was made with the participation of Jeffreys and Baker.

The story

This ITV drama tells the true story of the first use of DNA fingerprinting to catch a murderer. In 1983 and 1986, two 15-year-old girls, Lynda Mann and Dawn Ashworth, are raped and murdered near Leicester. At the University of Leicester, geneticist Alec Jeffreys discovers a way to produce a genetic "fingerprint" unique to each person, first used to settle an immigration case. Detective Chief Superintendent David Baker, convinced that one man killed both girls, asks Jeffreys to test the DNA of a local teenager who has confessed to Dawn's murder. The test proves that the youth is innocent and that the same unknown man committed both crimes. Baker then organises the first DNA manhunt, asking thousands of local men to give blood samples. The killer, Colin Pitchfork, evades it by having a colleague give blood in his place, but he is caught when the colleague is overheard boasting, and his DNA matches the crime scenes.
