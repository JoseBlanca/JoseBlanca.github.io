The genetics

The film is a warning about bioterrorism rather than a story about genetics. Its weapon is the smallpox virus, eradicated in nature: the CDC traces the outbreak to a strain held in Soviet biological weapons laboratories, and the Dutch Wikipedia summary adds that unemployed Soviet scientists probably sold samples to the highest bidder. The English Wikipedia describes the premise as one man who creates the virus himself and infects himself to spread it, and the sources do not say whether any genetic engineering is involved. Laboratory stocks are the danger; scientists and doctors also appear as the experts who recognise the disease, identify the strain and fight it, often unheeded by officials. Mixing real epidemiology with fiction, the docudrama shows how a lab-held pathogen could overwhelm public health and social order.

The story

Presented as a documentary looking back on events, with mock interviews and stock footage, this BBC docudrama imagines a smallpox attack in 2002. In New York a young woman is first sent home with a flu diagnosis; when she returns covered in pustules, a bioterrorism specialist recognises smallpox and finds more cases, but a proposal for a lockdown and mass vaccination is rejected. A traveller carries the disease to London, and the CDC identifies a strain from Soviet bioweapons laboratories, probably sold abroad by unemployed scientists. The infection spreads worldwide; vaccine shortages, hidden by the British government, lead to strikes, riots, forced quarantine and the breakdown of supplies, and in the developing world hundreds of thousands die each week. Investigators trace the attack to a man who infected himself and spread the virus in New York's transit system, and whose body is found in a subway tunnel. The pandemic is finally defeated after killing 60 million people.
