The genetics

This made-for-television monster film, in the vein of The Fly, turns a well-meant medical experiment into a human-insect hybrid. Dr. Jennifer Allen works with mosquitoes infected with the Gillen virus, which she irradiates and genetically alters in search of a cure. An accident caused by an escaping convict showers him and Allen with chemicals and altered mosquitoes, and both begin to transform into mosquito-human creatures. Allen is a sympathetic scientist who sacrifices herself to destroy the monster, and her final batch of mosquitoes does wipe out the virus, so the film ends with the research vindicated at a terrible cost. The underlying idea loosely echoes real proposals to release modified mosquitoes against disease, but the transformation of people by exposure to irradiated insects is pure B-movie fantasy. The story

In Baltimore, Dr. Jennifer Allen is looking for a cure for the deadly Gillen virus by working with infected mosquitoes, which she treats with small doses of radiation. Ray Erikson, a convict who joins her programme in exchange for a commuted sentence, takes a hostage and forces his way into the experiment room; in the shoot-out an explosion drenches him and Allen with chemicals and genetically altered mosquitoes. Ray turns into a giant human-mosquito monster, kills his ex-girlfriend and goes on a killing spree, pursued by Allen's boyfriend, police lieutenant Tom Randall, who finds that a stun gun hurts it. Allen begins to transform too, and believes the creature wants her as a mate. She releases the last, perfected batch of altered mosquitoes, and when the monster attacks her at the lab she electrocutes it, dying herself. The altered mosquitoes wipe out the Gillen virus.
