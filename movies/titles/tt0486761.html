The genetics

Genetic engineering is part of the premise of the whole Lilo & Stitch franchise: Stitch and the other experiments are artificial creatures designed by the alien mad scientist Jumba Jookiba, and this film's antagonists are a new engineered creature, Leroy, built from Stitch's template, and an army of his clones. The film treats these ideas as cartoon devices rather than science; Leroy is created under duress, and a song acts as a built-in kill switch. Jumba is a comic, ultimately benevolent scientist, coerced into working for the villain, and the story's message is that engineered beings can find a family and a place in the world. For younger audiences it presents genetic creation and cloning as tools of good or evil depending on who uses them. There is no attempt at scientific plausibility.

The story

After capturing and rehabilitating all of Jumba's alien experiments, Lilo, Stitch, Jumba and Pleakley are honoured by the Galactic Alliance; Jumba returns to his laboratory, Pleakley takes a teaching post and Stitch becomes captain of the Galactic Armada, leaving Lilo behind in Hawaii. Gantu frees the villain Dr. Hämsterviel from prison, and Hämsterviel forces Jumba to create a new experiment from Stitch's template: Leroy, a red near-twin of Stitch. Hämsterviel defeats Stitch, sends him, Jumba and Pleakley towards a black hole, and clones Leroy into an army to conquer the galaxy and destroy the other 624 experiments. Lilo, helped by Experiment 625, Reuben, and a reformed Gantu, rejoins her friends after they escape, and in the final battle at Aloha Stadium they disable the Leroys with a shutdown command Jumba secretly built in: Elvis Presley's version of "Aloha ʻOe".
