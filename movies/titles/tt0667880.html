The genetics

Gene therapy misused for behavioural modification is the subject of the episode. A frustrated anthropology researcher, convinced that modern medicine has weakened humanity, compares Homo erectus DNA with modern DNA and injects himself with "dominance genes" from primates, and then secretly injects a colleague with "submissiveness genes", a non-consensual experiment that the episode treats as a violation. Zeller is a variation on the Jekyll and Hyde story: a researcher who experiments on himself to escape his limitations and ends up destroyed by it. The premise rests on a simplified idea of genetic determinism, as if single genes for dominance or submissiveness existed and could reshape an adult's personality within days. In reality, behavioural traits are influenced by many genes with small effects as well as by environment, and gene therapy, first used successfully in 1990 to treat an inherited immune deficiency, aims to correct specific diseases.

The story

Dr. Arthur Zeller is a brilliant but shy researcher in a university anthropology department whom his co-workers ignore and a vagrant regularly bullies for money. He daydreams about unleashing the animal within himself and, after comparing DNA from a Homo erectus specimen with his own, begins a kind of gene therapy, injecting himself with genes for dominance taken from primitive primates. His personality changes: he becomes assertive, but also suffers lapses in which he reverts to the mind of a caveman, attacking his boss and killing and eating a dog. When his colleague Dr. Laura White does not return his affection, he secretly injects her with genes for submissiveness during a routine flu shot. She becomes ill, and Arthur, regaining control, confesses and asks for her help. Together they develop an experimental antidote, but when he admits what he did to her, he realises he has lost her for good. He flees with his syringes and injects himself with a massive dose of the ape genes before Laura can reach him.
