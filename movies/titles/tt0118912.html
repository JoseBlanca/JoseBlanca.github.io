The genetics

Despite its title, genetics plays a limited role. The science consists of reviving an ancient creature from a fossil using an enzyme taken from a beetle, with the aim of cloning it; the film's interest lies in the hunt for the monster rather than in the science. The scientist Wessinger fits the classic profile of the unscrupulous researcher: he betrays his colleagues, works with secret funding from the CIA and private mercenaries, and intends to sell clones of a dangerous creature to the highest bidder, which ends with his own death. The doctor Mattley, by contrast, is a physician committed to the local community. Neither reviving an organism from a fossil nor cloning it is possible with real techniques, since cloning requires living cells or at least well-preserved DNA. The film is a low-budget action film made in the Philippines and openly influenced by Alien, Predator and Jurassic Park.

The story

In the jungles of Borneo, Ash Mattley, a doctor running a village clinic, helps the scientist Carl Wessinger collect a rare beetle whose enzyme he is studying, but Wessinger betrays the team and abandons them in a cave. He later uncovers the fossil of a creature that the local people fear as "Balacau". Two years later, gruesome killings begin in the jungle, and CIA agent Claire Sommers asks Mattley to help her find Wessinger, whose research the agency had funded. At Wessinger's abandoned compound they learn that he used the beetle enzyme to bring the fossil back to life; the creature was an alien that had terrorised the region centuries earlier, and Wessinger planned to clone it and sell it to the highest bidder before it escaped. Pursued by the creature, which can turn invisible, and by Wessinger's mercenaries, Mattley and Sommers survive with the help of the local tribe, and Mattley finally kills Balacau with a rocket launcher.
