The genetics

Genetics is little more than a running gag in this parody. Earlier films in the series established that the killer tomatoes were the product of Professor Gangreen's experiments, and here he wants the DNA of FT, a benign tomato, to build a new tomato army; one review summarises his plan in exactly those terms. Gangreen is the classic cartoon mad scientist: an escaped convict who uses his creations for a ludicrous political scheme, with no interest in science for its own sake. The film is inconsistent even with its own franchise, as it shows fruit and vegetables that are sentient without his intervention, and the tomatoes now speak English. Its jokes are aimed at horror clichés and French stereotypes rather than at biology, and the "genetic" element makes no statement about real plant breeding or genetic modification.

The story

In the fourth and last film of the Killer Tomatoes series, released direct to video, the mad scientist Dr. Mortimer Gangreen escapes from prison and sets up base in his assistant Igor's castle outside Paris, still bent on world domination. An old prophecy foretells that the king of France, Louis XVII, will return to claim the throne, and since Igor closely resembles the only known image of the lost king, Gangreen plans to use his army of killer tomatoes to stage a second French Revolution and crown Igor as a puppet monarch. To build his army, he kidnaps FT, a friendly, intelligent tomato. Michael, an American has-been actor, and Marie, a French woman he falls in love with, try to stop him while the tomatoes, including a giant fire-breathing one, attack France. The real Louis returns and triumphs over Igor, and Gangreen flees in a tomato-shaped hot-air balloon, swearing revenge.
