The genetics

Genetics appears in two comic forms. In the main story, a paternity test confirms that the abandoned baby is Quagmire's daughter, which makes him face fatherhood; the episode then turns on whether he can raise her, and it ends with him letting her stay with the adoptive parents who love her. In the subplot, Stewie, the Griffins' genius baby, clones himself to get a simple-minded servant, and his clone in turn makes an even dimmer clone of Brian. The clones are deliberately inferior copies that deteriorate, lose body parts and dissolve, a running gag rather than a serious argument about cloning. Stewie is presented as a lazy, careless creator, but the episode passes no judgement on him. In reality, clones are genetic copies with the original's capacities, born as babies, and do not degrade in this way.

The story

In this Family Guy episode, Glenn Quagmire finds a newborn girl on his doorstep, with a note from her mother telling him she is his daughter. A paternity test confirms it, and he names her Anna Lee. Caring for her ruins his sex life, and he puts her up for adoption, but he cannot stop thinking about her and goes to take her back; when he sees how happy she is with her adoptive parents, he leaves her with them. Meanwhile, Stewie creates a dumbed-down clone of himself, Bitch Stewie, to serve as his assistant. Brian wants one too, but Stewie lazily lets his clone make it, and Bitch Brian turns out even less capable. Both clones prove unstable, fall apart and dissolve. In a minor subplot, Peter believes he is talking to the ghost of Ronald Reagan on a ham radio, but the voice belongs to the impressionist Rich Little.
