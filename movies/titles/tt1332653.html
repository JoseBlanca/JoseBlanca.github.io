The genetics

This two-part BBC adaptation of John Wyndham's 1951 novel uses biotechnology at two levels. The triffids themselves are an exploited resource, farmed industrially for their oil despite their danger, and the series hints at the human arrogance of relying on a creature that cannot be controlled. The main genetic element is Dennis Masen's plan to genetically engineer a new type of triffid that could neutralise the existing ones, a technological fix for a biological disaster; his son considers the idea absurd, and the experiment fails when the new plant kills its creator. The scientist is a lone, obsessive figure working outside any institution after the collapse of society. In the original novel the triffids' origin is uncertain, but Bill suspects they were bioengineered in the Soviet Union, and some editions link them to the theories of the Soviet agronomist Trofim Lysenko, a proponent of Lamarckism whose ideas were thoroughly discredited. In this version the triffids are instead tied to a story about energy and climate.

The story

In an alternative present-day Britain, triffids, large carnivorous plants able to move and to kill with a venomous sting, are farmed worldwide because their oil has become a clean fuel that ended climate change. Bill Masen, who works with triffids,, whose mother was killed by one of the plants, is in hospital with his eyes bandaged when a solar storm blinds more than 95% of the world's population. With the electric fences failing, the triffids escape and begin to hunt. Bill teams up with radio presenter Jo Playton and encounters groups of survivors, among them a religious colony led by Durrant and the brutal Torrence, who seeks to impose his rule. At Shirning House Bill finds his estranged father Dennis, who plans to genetically engineer a new triffid to neutralise the old ones, but the new plant attacks and kills him. Bill finally remembers a childhood trick, triffid poison applied to the eyes, which lets the group pass among the plants and reach a colony on the Isle of Wight.
