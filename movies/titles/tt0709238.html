The genetics

The episode centres on a genetic experiment on a sentient being. Dr. Beckett's retrovirus suppresses the Wraith's Iratus bug DNA so that their otherwise suppressed human traits are expressed, turning a Wraith into a human, with the side effect of wiping his memory. The Atlantis team, who are the protagonists, are shown in an uncomfortable light: they experiment on a prisoner without consent, invent a false identity for him and debate wiping his mind again when he learns the truth. Michael's question, whether he was "cured" for his own sake or for theirs, is the moral core of the episode, and Teyla and Beckett are shown doubting what they have done. The idea of reversing a species by switching off its non-human genes is fiction, though it loosely echoes real gene therapy using modified viruses to deliver genes. Michael later becomes a recurring villain who conducts his own genetic experiments.

The story

A man wakes up in the Atlantis infirmary with no memory and is told that he is Lieutenant Michael Kenmore, an expedition member rescued from the Wraith. Dr. Carson Beckett gives him regular injections, and he notices that people treat him with suspicion: he can read Wraith writing, Ronon attacks him, and he dreams of seeing himself as a Wraith. Only Teyla shows him friendship. Searching Beckett's office, he finds recordings showing that he was a captured Wraith, transformed into a human by Beckett's experimental retrovirus, which also erased his memories. Feeling betrayed, Michael argues that he has been changed for humans' benefit, not his own. He escapes, killing a guard, and is recaptured. While being moved to another planet for a stronger dose, he takes Teyla hostage and flees through the Stargate as his Wraith body returns. Sheppard and Ronon rescue Teyla, and Michael rejoins the Wraith.
