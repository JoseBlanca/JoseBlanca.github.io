The genetics

Cloning is the premise, but the film uses it to ask emotional and philosophical questions rather than to explore the science. The replacement is a physical duplicate of Cameron that behaves like him and believes it is him, an idea that goes well beyond biological cloning, which copies the genome but not a person's experiences or mind. The story focuses on grief, identity and consent: whether a loving deception is acceptable, whether the copy is the same person, and what the original loses by being replaced. Dr. Scott, the company's scientific leader, is presented as calm and businesslike, and some reviewers found her close to a villain, a provider of an ethically troubling service to the dying. In reality a human clone would be a genetic twin born as a baby, and nothing like this transfer of identity exists, so the film is best read as a meditation on mortality.

The story

In the near future, Cameron Turner, an artist, husband and father, is diagnosed with a terminal illness and keeps it from his wife Poppy, who is still grieving the death of her twin brother in an accident. Dr. Jo Scott, head of a high-tech cloning company working from a secret facility in the woods, offers him a way to spare his family: an exact double, designed to act and behave like him, that will replace him without anyone knowing and will not even know that it is not Cameron. Cameron goes to the facility, meets his replacement and befriends Kate, another terminally ill patient in the programme. Torn between protecting his family and the pain of being erased from their lives, and uncertain whether to tell Poppy, he wavers over whether to go through with the swap. The film was written and directed by Benjamin Cleary and released on Apple TV+ in December 2021.
