The genetics

The genetic element is a courtroom argument: the defence claims that Jono's aggressive behaviour comes from his genes, so that he is not fully responsible for the killing. The series' synopsis calls the strategy bizarre and audacious and notes that, if accepted, it could upset the whole British legal system, since any defendant might blame his genes. The title points to the other side of the debate, the neglect suffered by children in care. The episode adapts the American 'Born Bad', whose defence rested on an extra Y chromosome; the available sources do not say which genetic evidence is used here or how the court rules. Twin and adoption studies show that aggression is partly heritable, but through many genes of small effect that interact with upbringing, and no genetic finding removes criminal responsibility.

The story

A 13-year-old boy is found kicked to death at Euston Station in London. Detectives Ronnie Brooks and Matt Devlin first take him for a runaway, but the autopsy shows he was well fed and healthy, and a press conference identifies him as Danny Jackson, a child in foster care whose mother is in a rehab clinic. Suspicion falls on the mother's former boyfriend, who is thought to have abused the boy, but the trail leads to another child, Jono Blake, who lived in the same care home. At trial, prosecutors James Steel and Alesha Phillips face Steel's old flame, the barrister Beatrice McArdle, who mounts an audacious defence: that Jono is genetically predisposed to violence. The episode is an adaptation of the American Law & Order episode 'Born Bad'.
