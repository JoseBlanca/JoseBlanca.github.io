The genetics

Cloning drives the second half of the serial. Pangol, the last young Argolin, is the only successful child of a cloning experiment carried out with the Tachyon Recreation Generator, and he plans to use the same machine to mass-produce copies of himself as an army for the war-forged empire he wants to build against the Foamasi. The Doctor's sabotage turns the army into harmless tachyon images, and the machine's rejuvenation setting reduces Pangol to a baby. The story frames cloning as fantasy machine technology based on tachyons rather than biology, and it uses it to show a warlike fanatic trying to multiply himself; it is defeated by trickery rather than condemned as science. The parallel rejuvenation plot is presented as tachyon physics, not genetics, and Hardin's faked results make it a story about fraud. Real cloning produces a genetically identical individual that must still develop from an embryo; it does not duplicate adults.

The story

The Fourth Doctor and Romana visit the Leisure Hive on Argolis, a resort built by the Argolins after a war with the Foamasi. The Hive is near bankruptcy, and its main attraction, the Tachyon Recreation Generator, can duplicate and manipulate organic matter. The Argolin chairman Mena brings the Earth scientist Hardin, whose tachyon rejuvenation results are faked, to save her deteriorating people; a test leaves the Doctor aged. Mena's son Pangol reveals that he is the only young Argolin because he was the only successful child of a cloning experiment in the Generator. Driven by hatred of the Foamasi, he uses the machine to create an army of replicas of himself, but the Doctor has sabotaged it and the copies are only tachyon images of the Doctor. When Pangol re-enters the Generator, set to "rejuvenate", he emerges as a baby in the arms of a rejuvenated Mena.
