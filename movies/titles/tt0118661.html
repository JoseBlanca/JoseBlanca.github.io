The genetics

Cloning is a minor plot device in this spy caper, whose main science fiction is weather control. The villain's organisation has produced a clone of Emma Peel, a double that frames her for sabotage, attacks her and is used to confuse the heroes; the Italian sources add that the clone cannot speak. Steed uncovers the trick through photographs of 'failed genetic experiments, including cloning'. Cloning thus serves as a tool of impersonation for a mad scientist and a traitor inside the Ministry, and the clone dies with Father. The film shows nothing of how the clone was made and does not explore the idea further; an adult copy identical to a living person is, of course, beyond real cloning, which produces a baby that must grow up.

The story

In this film version of the 1960s British spy series, secret agent John Steed and the meteorologist Dr. Emma Peel are assigned by the Ministry's spymaster, Mother, to find out who sabotaged Prospero, a secret weather-control project that Peel directed and is suspected of wrecking herself. The trail leads to Sir August de Wynter, a former Ministry scientist who heads a secret organisation whose members wear teddy-bear suits, one of whom looks exactly like Peel. Peel is drugged and hypnotised, then arrested. Steed sees photographs of failed genetic experiments, including cloning, which show that the other Emma is a clone, and finds that Mother's deputy Father is in league with de Wynter. De Wynter demands that world leaders buy their weather from him. Father and the clone die in a balloon crash, and Steed and Peel stop de Wynter on his island.
