The genetics

Like the rest of the Resident Evil franchise, the film rests on the T-virus, a bioweapon developed by the Umbrella Corporation, whose outbreak in Raccoon City is the source of the villain's trauma. Here an advanced strain is spread by a vengeful former mercenary through mosquito-like drones and has also reached marine animals; the villain finally fuses himself with a bio-organic weapon, and his ally Maria Gomez is, according to the Italian Wikipedia, enhanced by a virus. Science also supplies the remedy: Rebecca Chambers, a scientist with the anti-bioterrorism BSAA, produces a vaccine that cures her infected friends, and the team reprograms the drones against the villain. The film is action horror, and its virology and bio-organic weapons are pure franchise fiction.

The story

In 1998, during the Raccoon City outbreak, the mercenary Dylan Blake is ordered to abandon his infected comrades and has to kill his friend. In 2015, agent Leon Kennedy tries to recover a kidnapped DARPA robotics engineer, Dr. Antonio Taylor, while Chris Redfield, Jill Valentine, Rebecca Chambers and Claire Redfield investigate deaths caused by an advanced strain of the T-virus, apparently injected into the victims; an infected orca suggests the virus is spreading in the bay. All the victims had visited Alcatraz, and when the team goes there a zombie outbreak separates them. Dylan, now head of a crime syndicate, has seized Taylor's mosquito-like drones to spread the virus, infects Leon and the Redfields, kills Taylor and prepares to release the drones worldwide. Rebecca's vaccine cures her friends; Leon kills Dylan's ally Maria Gomez, and the team turns the drones on Dylan, now merged with a bio-organic weapon, and kills him.
