The genetics

The episode is built on the season's central project: Dr. Carson Beckett's retrovirus, designed to turn a Wraith into a human, which the expedition sees as a weapon against its enemies. Here the retrovirus has the opposite effect on a human: once injected into Sheppard, it starts to turn him into an Iratus bug hybrid, and the expedition's doctors and scientists must undo the transformation. The episode thus shows a genetic weapon whose effects its makers cannot fully control, with the medical team presented as the heroes trying to save their colleague. The source gives only a short summary and does not describe how the cure is found. The idea of a retrovirus rewriting a whole organism into another species in days is fiction, although real retroviruses do insert their genes into host DNA, which is why they are used as vectors in gene therapy.

The story

In this second-season episode of Stargate Atlantis, Major John Sheppard has been injected with the Wraith retrovirus, the experimental agent that the expedition hopes can turn Wraith into humans. Instead it begins transforming him into a hybrid of human and Iratus bug, an insect species. The Atlantis team races against time to find a way to reverse the transformation before it becomes complete. As the change progresses Sheppard becomes increasingly difficult to deal with, yet he may also hold the key to his own salvation.
