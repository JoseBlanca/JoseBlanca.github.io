The genetics

Genetics appears only in the closing scene. During the troll's escape at the start, Professor Møller takes a sample from it, and in a mid-credits scene he is shown secretly studying a baby troll grown from one of Jotun's genetic samples, which sets up a possible sequel and suggests that a secret government research programme will keep trying to exploit the creatures. The main story is a monster film about trolls, Norwegian folklore and the persecution of trolls by the Church, in which the heroine's empathy and a sanctuary are the answer, rather than a story about genetic science.

The story

In this Norwegian sequel to Troll, directed by Roar Uthaug, Nora Tidemann, who left her job studying trolls for the government after the events of the first film, is asked to visit Vemork, a secret underground facility that holds a hibernating giant troll codenamed Jotun. Her song accidentally wakes it; Jotun kills a researcher and escapes. The prime minister orders the troll killed, and a military operation with ultraviolet lights fails after Jotun attacks a ski resort. Nora finds and befriends the adult son of the Troll King, whom she calls Beautiful, hoping he can calm Jotun, but Jotun attacks him. Ancient documents reveal that King Olaf, who had exterminated the trolls, wanted to give them a sanctuary before the Church had him killed. In Trondheim, the team fights Jotun with holy water; Andreas sacrifices himself, and Beautiful kills Jotun. A sanctuary is created for Beautiful, and in a mid-credits scene Møller is secretly studying a baby troll grown from Jotun's genetic samples.
