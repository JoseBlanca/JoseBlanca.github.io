The genetics

Two genetic elements drive the episode. Forensic analysis of the bite shows that the biter was chromosomally male, which exposes the secret of the twin raised as a girl. The deeper theme is nature against nurture in gender identity: a doctor with an ulterior motive used the twins to prove that upbringing could override biology. The story sides with nature, presenting the experiment as a deception that harmed the twins. The real case behind it was that of David Reimer, a Canadian twin raised as a girl after a botched circumcision on the advice of the psychologist John Money, who claimed for years that the reassignment had succeeded; in fact Reimer rejected the female identity and later lived as a man, and the case is now cited as evidence that gender identity is not simply learned. The only available source is a short synopsis, so details of the resolution are unknown.

The story

Detectives Benson and Stabler investigate a gang member who fell from a building after the person he tried to rape bit him in self-defence. They believe they have found the victim, a teenage boy, but his twin sister says that she was the one who bit the attacker, even though analysis of the bite shows that it came from someone chromosomally male. The twins are shocked to learn the truth: after a botched circumcision, their parents raised one of the boys as a girl. The investigation then uncovers the ulterior motive of a doctor who wanted to use the twins to prove that nurture overrides nature. The episode is partly inspired by the real case of David Reimer.
