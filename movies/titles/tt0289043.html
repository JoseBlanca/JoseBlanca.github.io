The genetics

Genetics plays a minor role. The film's starting point is a pathogen kept in a research laboratory, where scientists hold chimpanzees infected with an aggression-inducing "rage" virus; its accidental release by activists who do not believe the scientists' warning causes the catastrophe. The film does not explain how the virus was created or what the research was for, and the scientists appear only briefly; the rest of the story deals with survival and with the violence of the survivors themselves, especially the soldiers. Rather than supernatural zombies, the infected were conceived as victims of a biological contagion: Danny Boyle drew on real diseases, especially Ebola and aspects of rabies, and on the British outbreaks of BSE and foot-and-mouth disease of the time. An abandoned ending showed the laboratory that created the virus. The rage virus is fictional, and its near-instant effect is a dramatic device.

The story

Animal rights activists break into a laboratory in Cambridge where chimpanzees are kept in an abnormally aggressive state. Despite a scientist's warning that the animals are infected with "rage", they free one, which attacks them; within seconds the infected become violent, and the virus spreads across Britain. Twenty-eight days later, Jim, a bicycle courier, wakes from a coma in a deserted London hospital. Rescued from the infected by the survivors Selena and Mark, he finds that his parents have killed themselves, and after Mark is infected, he and Selena join the taxi driver Frank and his daughter Hannah. A radio broadcast leads them to a military blockade near Manchester, where Frank is infected and shot. The soldiers who take in the others turn out to be planning to keep the women as sex slaves. Jim escapes, releases an infected soldier to attack the troops, and rescues Selena and Hannah; weeks later, the three wave a giant "HELLO" banner at a passing jet from a remote cottage, where the infected are dying of starvation.
