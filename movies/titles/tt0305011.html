The genetics

Human cloning is the comic premise of the series rather than its subject. Each clone has the genome of a historical figure, and much of the humour comes from the gap between the "clonefathers" and their teenage copies: Gandhi is a party animal, JFK a narcissistic womaniser, Abe Lincoln an insecure romantic. The characters' struggle to live up to, or rebel against, their genetic originals is a running joke about the limits of genetic determinism; personality, the show suggests, is not written in DNA. The scientists are cartoon villains: a government board that wants to use the clones as military assets and a principal who sees them as amusement-park attractions, a satire of the idea of breeding people for a purpose. The series is mainly a parody of teen dramas such as Dawson's Creek. Its Gandhi character provoked protests in India in 2003, and he was left out of the revival.

The story

This animated sitcom is set in a high school in the fictional town of Exclamation, USA, populated by teenage clones of famous historical figures created in the 1980s. Officially the school is a military experiment run by the Secret Board of Shadowy Figures, which plans to harness the clones' talents for the US military, while the principal, mad scientist Cinnamon J. Scudworth, secretly wants to use them for a clone-themed amusement park. The show follows the clones of Abe Lincoln, Joan of Arc, Cleopatra, JFK and Gandhi through teenage crushes, exams and parties, centred on Abe's choice between the vain Cleopatra and Joan, who loves him. The first season (2002–2003) ended with the clones abducted on prom night. In the 2023 revival on HBO Max, they are unfrozen twenty years later and return to Clone High, where they meet a new generation of clones, including Harriet Tubman and Frida Kahlo.
