The genetics

Genetics enters through the construction of Kiryu, the 'Mechagodzilla' of the title. Dr. Yuhara, an expert in building robots from animal DNA, uses the skeleton of the original Godzilla as the frame of a cyborg weapon for Japan's defence, and the film states that Kiryu shares Godzilla's DNA. This genetic link gives the machine a will of its own: when the new Godzilla roars, Kiryu recovers memories of its past life and goes on a rampage, destroying the city it was built to protect. Yuhara is a sympathetic scientist working for the state, and the weapon ultimately drives Godzilla back. Critics read the film as a reflection on the limits of technology and bio-engineering and on losing control of one's creations. The idea that memories or will survive in the DNA of bones is pure fantasy.

The story

Forty-five years after the first Godzilla was killed in 1954, a new Godzilla attacks Japan, and Lieutenant Akane Yashiro of the Anti-Megalosaurus Force panics during the battle, causing the deaths of her comrades. The government assembles scientists, including Dr. Tokumitsu Yuhara, a single father who specialises in building robots from animal DNA, to build a cyborg weapon, Kiryu, around the skeleton of the original Godzilla. Akane becomes its pilot despite the resentment of Lieutenant Hayama, whose brother died in the earlier attack. When Godzilla attacks Yokohama, its roar awakens memories in Kiryu's bones and the machine runs berserk through the city. Akane grows close to Yuhara and his daughter Sara. In a final battle at Shinagawa, with the remote control knocked out, Akane pilots Kiryu from inside, pushes Godzilla out to sea and fires the Absolute Zero Cannon; Godzilla retreats wounded and Kiryu, badly damaged, is repaired.
