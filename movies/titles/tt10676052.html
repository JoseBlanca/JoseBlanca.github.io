The genetics

Heredity is a secondary theme: the film asks whether powers acquired by the parents through cosmic radiation can be passed on to their child. Before Franklin's birth, the world speculates about whether he will have powers, the parents fear what he might be, and Reed repeatedly tries to understand his unborn son, reassuring Sue that the child is perfectly normal, while Galactus perceives his cosmic potential first. In the comics, Franklin's powers were long attributed to a mutant gene, whereas the film presents him as a natural-born conduit for the "Power Cosmic", linked to his parents' exposure to cosmic rays. Reed is a scientist-hero whose inventions have transformed society, and his approach to his son is that of a worried father as much as a researcher. In reality, radiation can cause mutations, but only those in the germline, the cells that produce eggs and sperm, can be passed on to children, and they cause random changes rather than new abilities.

The story

In a retro-futuristic 1960s on an alternative Earth, the astronauts Reed Richards, Sue Storm, Ben Grimm and Johnny Storm have been celebrity superheroes, the Fantastic Four, since gaining powers from cosmic rays during a space mission. When Reed and Sue announce that they are expecting a child, the world wonders whether the baby will also have powers. The Silver Surfer then arrives to announce that Earth will be devoured by the cosmic being Galactus. The team travels into space to confront him, and Galactus, sensing an immense cosmic power in the unborn child, offers to spare Earth in exchange for him. They refuse and escape, and Sue gives birth to Franklin on the way home. Facing public anger, the Fantastic Four devise a plan to teleport Galactus away; Sue pushes him into the portal with the Surfer's help but dies in the effort, and is revived by Franklin.
