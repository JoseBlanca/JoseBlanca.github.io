The genetics

The monsters of the gorge are the product of a biological disaster. A wartime research site, described in some accounts as a chemical weapons test site connected with the Manhattan Project era, released an agent that cross-mutated every organism in the area, fusing humans, insects and plants into hybrids. The film uses this as the background for a romantic action story, revealed only halfway through. The scientists of the 1940s remain unseen, and the present-day villain is a private defence company that keeps the disaster secret and harvests hybrid samples hoping to create super-soldiers, while disposing of the guards who learn too much. The story thus combines two familiar fears: weapons research that escapes control, and corporations exploiting biology for military purposes. Scientifically, the premise is fantasy: chemical or radiation exposure can damage DNA and cause mutations, but it cannot merge the genomes of different species into viable hybrids of the kind shown.

The story

Two elite snipers, the former US Marine Levi Kane and the Lithuanian covert operative Drasa, are hired to guard opposite sides of a remote, hidden gorge for a year, forbidden to contact each other or the outside world. Their task is to stop the creatures inside, the "Hollow Men", from getting out. Over the months they begin to communicate across the gorge and fall in love. When Levi falls into the gorge and Drasa follows him, they discover that all life within it has been fused into hybrid forms, and that the Hollow Men are the soldiers who disappeared there decades ago. The source is a Second World War weapons laboratory, abandoned after an earthquake released its contaminant. The site is now maintained by Darklake, a private defence corporation extracting samples to create super-soldiers. After escaping, the pair turn against their employer, Bartholomew, who is killed when the gorge is destroyed by its nuclear fail-safe, and they reunite in France.
