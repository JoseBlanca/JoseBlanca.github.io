The genetics

Genetics plays only a marginal role in this film. Its horror rests on a supernatural curse that behaves like an infectious agent: the videotape, and later a written diary, transmit a virus that kills within a week, and the virus changes as the curse spreads. Sadako uses the virus to be reborn through another woman's womb and returns, in her own words, as "perfectly dual-gendered", able to reproduce herself. These ideas evoke viral mutation and asexual reproduction, but the film does not develop them in genetic terms, and its scientist, a pathologist, investigates causes of death rather than heredity. The real content is the folklore of a vengeful spirit, here translated into a pseudo-medical vocabulary. Spiral was released in Japan alongside Ring in 1998 and was later replaced by Ring 2 as the sequel to Ring. It does not deal with genes, DNA or genetic research in any meaningful way, and its inclusion in a database on genetics is doubtful.

The story

In this sequel to Ring, pathologist Mitsuo Andō, still grieving his drowned son, performs the autopsy of his friend and rival Ryūji Takayama and finds a coded message in the stomach. Reiko Asakawa and her son Yōichi are then found dead. With Takayama's student Mai Takano, Andō investigates the cursed videotape that kills its viewers a week later and the murdered psychic Sadako Yamamura. Tests show that the deaths are caused by a virus, and that the virus that killed Reiko's boss, Yoshino, differs from the one that killed Takayama and his son. Andō survives the curse, but Mai is found dead having apparently given birth, and a woman who looks like her turns out to be Sadako reborn through Mai's body. Andō learns that Reiko's diary now spreads the curse and that Takayama was helping Sadako. In exchange for his help, Sadako brings back Takayama and Andō's son.
