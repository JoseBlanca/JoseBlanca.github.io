The genetics

The film is a Soviet-set body-horror story about a pathogen and the state's wish to weaponize it. The threat is a mould-like fungus found in the depths of the borehole, which infects warm-bodied hosts through spores and fuses them into mutated masses; the sources do not say it was engineered. What brings it into the genetics-adjacent category is the secret laboratory beneath the permafrost and Colonel Morozov, a military intelligence officer who sees the outbreak as a biological weapon for the state and puts everyone at risk to obtain it, while the deputy research head wants the glory of the discovery. Against them stands Anna, an epidemiologist presented as a responsible scientist, who in the end tries to sacrifice herself to keep the infection from reaching the surface. The film draws on the 'Well to Hell' legend of the real Kola borehole and on The Thing; its fungus is invented.

The story

In 1984 the Soviet epidemiologist Anna Fedorova, wracked by guilt after a colleague died testing her vaccine on himself, is sent by GRU Colonel Morozov to the Kola Superdeep Borehole, where a secret laboratory lies deep beneath the permafrost. Strange sounds have been recorded underground, twenty people have vanished, and the site's head, Dr. Grigoriev, has concealed an outbreak; Anna's task is to bring back samples. With a squad of soldiers led by Major Mikheev, she descends into the facility, which Grigoriev tries to seal, and finds a fungal infection that covers its victims with mould and melts them into monstrous fused bodies. Anna learns that the fungus dies in the cold and needs warm hosts. Morozov refuses to call for rescue, intent on securing a biological weapon for the state, and the deputy head of research wants the discovery for his own fame. Most of the group dies; at the bottom Anna sees a vast glowing fungal colony. Infected, she escapes to the surface and primes a grenade to destroy herself and the fungus.
