The genetics

The episode is about the consequences of human genetic enhancement. It continues "Unnatural Selection", in which parents buy black-market genetic improvements for their unborn children at the risk of Genetic Rejection Syndrome, a fictional condition that causes deformities, violence and psychosis. Here the generation of failed enhancements has grown up, and society deals with it by shipping the children off to labour camps and hunting the adults. The story questions both the enhancement industry and the parents who abandoned their children, and adds a twist on nature versus nurture: Dylan claims that the gene behind his rage was inherited from his father rather than created by the enhancement, a claim the episode leaves unconfirmed, while its closing narration suggests that what parents pass on may lie in the soul rather than the genes; Ray, once transformed, becomes a monster in his family's eyes. No scientist appears prominently. GRS is invented, but the episode reflects a real concern about heritable genetic modification: early experiments editing human embryos produced unintended mutations, and safety is central to the debate.

The story

Years after the events of "Unnatural Selection", genetic enhancement of babies is common but flawed, and children affected by Genetic Rejection Syndrome (GRS) have grown into deformed, violent adults who commit murders. Detective Ray Venable leads a team hunting them. He and his wife Marie once had a son with GRS, Dylan, whom they paid an agency to take away; its director now admits that such children are sent to labour camps abroad. Now Dylan is harassing the family. Ray injects himself with a serum that temporarily gives him the heightened senses of GRS sufferers so that he can track his son, only to discover that Dylan planted the serum for him to find. Dylan forcibly injects him with more, making the change irreversible, and claims that the gene behind his own violence was not altered but inherited from Ray. Ray kills Dylan, but his family now fears him.
