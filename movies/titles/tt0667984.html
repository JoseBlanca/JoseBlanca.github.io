The Shroud

The Outer Limits, season 5, episode 10

The genetics

Cloning from ancient DNA is the episode's premise. A televangelist's institute uses genetic material from the Shroud of Turin to create an embryo and implants it, without the mother's knowledge, in a woman seeking fertility treatment. The episode's opening narration frames it as a clash between science and religious faith, and it portrays the scientists as servants of a zealot who wants to control the child's upbringing, with the mother's consent violated from the start. The scientist Dr. Cowlings introduces doubt: the shroud's DNA might belong not to Christ but to an ordinary man with unusual abilities, which the story leaves open. The idea draws on real controversies about the shroud, which radiocarbon dating places in the Middle Ages. Even genuine ancient DNA is usually degraded and fragmentary, and reproductive cloning requires a living cell nucleus, so a clone could not be made from a cloth relic.

The story

Marie Wells and her husband Justin, unable to have a child, turn to the fertility clinic of the Tilford Institute, where Dr. Gail Cowlings implants an embryo in Marie's uterus by in vitro fertilisation. Marie does not know, but Justin does, that the embryo was created with DNA taken from the Shroud of Turin: the Reverend Doctor Thomas Tilford, a televangelist who worships both God and science, intends to bring about the Second Coming. When Marie learns the truth she is angry and doubts she is worthy to be the mother of Christ, until the toys she has bought for the baby begin to float around the room. Dr. Cowlings suspects that the DNA may instead come from an ancient man with telekinetic powers. Tilford confines the couple to the institute to take the child at birth, but the newborn uses his powers to teleport himself and his parents away.
