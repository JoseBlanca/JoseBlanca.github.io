The genetics

Cloning from ancient DNA is the episode's premise. A televangelist's institute transfers genetic material from the Shroud of Turin into one of the eggs of a woman seeking fertility treatment, after removing its nucleus, and implants the embryo without her knowledge; remarks about "the others" imply earlier attempts. The episode's opening narration frames it as a clash between science and religious faith, and it portrays the scientists as servants of a zealot who wants to control the child's upbringing, with the mother's consent violated from the start. The scientist Dr. Cowlings introduces doubt: the shroud's DNA might belong not to Christ but to an ordinary man with unusual abilities, which the story leaves open. The idea draws on real controversies about the shroud, which radiocarbon dating places in the Middle Ages. Even genuine ancient DNA is usually degraded and fragmentary, and nuclear transfer requires an intact nucleus, so a clone could not be made from a cloth relic.

The story

Marie Wells and her husband Justin, unable to have a child, turn to the fertility clinic of the Tilford Institute, where Dr. Gail Cowlings implants what Marie believes is an ordinary IVF embryo. Marie does not know, but Justin does, that the nucleus of one of her eggs was replaced with DNA taken from the Shroud of Turin: the Reverend Doctor Thomas Tilford, a televangelist who worships both God and science, intends to bring about the Second Coming. When Marie learns the truth she is angry and doubts she is worthy to be the mother of Christ, until the toys she has bought for the baby begin to float around the room. Dr. Cowlings suspects that the DNA may instead come from an ancient man with telekinetic powers. Tilford confines the couple to the institute to take the child at birth, but they escape, and when he finds them after the birth Cowlings stops him; the family leaves for a new life, helped, in one account, by the baby's powers.
