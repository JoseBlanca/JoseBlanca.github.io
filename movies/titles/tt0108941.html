The genetics

The apocalypse is caused by a man-made pathogen: a weaponised strain of influenza engineered in a secret US Army laboratory and released by accident, which kills almost all of humanity. The military's response, from quarantining a Texas town to studying survivors in a government facility from which Stu escapes, is part of the early story, but the scientists themselves remain in the background. Once the plague has done its work, the series turns into a supernatural struggle between good and evil in which science plays little part. The miniseries thus presents biological weapons research as reckless and secretive, the origin of the catastrophe. No real influenza strain approaches a 99.4% fatality rate, but the scenario of a laboratory accident with a weaponised virus reflects real concerns about bioweapons programmes.

The story

In this four-part miniseries based on Stephen King's novel, a weaponised strain of influenza, Project Blue, escapes from a secret US Army laboratory in California. A soldier, Charlie Campion, flees with his family and spreads the virus, nicknamed 'Captain Trips', which kills 99.4% of the world's population within weeks. The scattered survivors, among them Stu Redman, Frannie Goldsmith, Larry Underwood and the deaf-mute Nick Andros, dream of either the benevolent Mother Abagail in Nebraska or the sinister 'Dark Man', Randall Flagg. Abagail's followers found the Boulder Free Zone in Colorado, while Flagg builds an authoritarian regime in Las Vegas. After a bombing in Boulder, four men walk west to confront Flagg; when the Trashcan Man brings a stolen nuclear warhead to Las Vegas, a supernatural force detonates it, destroying Flagg and his followers. Frannie's baby survives the virus, a sign that humanity will endure.
