The genetics

Genetics enters this acclaimed episode through DNA absorption. By touching the Dalek, Rose lets it take her DNA, which it uses to rebuild itself, but the human genetic material also 'mutates' it, giving it fear and other emotions no Dalek should have; unable to bear being a mutant, it chooses to die. The episode thus treats DNA as carrying not just bodily traits but feelings, an idea that serves the drama of a monster made sympathetic. Its real target is the abuse of science by private wealth: van Statten tortures alien prisoners for information and wants to patent the Doctor's physiology, which links the story to debates on patenting biological material. No real organism can repair itself or acquire emotions by absorbing someone else's DNA through touch.

The story

A distress signal draws the Ninth Doctor and Rose Tyler to an underground bunker in Utah in 2012, owned by Henry van Statten, a billionaire who collects alien artefacts and exploits their technology. His prize specimen, chained and tortured to make it speak, turns out to be a Dalek, the last survivor of the Time War in which the Doctor believed he had destroyed its whole race. Van Statten also has the Doctor chained and scanned, planning to patent his alien physiology. Moved by the creature's suffering, Rose touches it; the Dalek absorbs her DNA, uses the biomass of a time traveller to regenerate, breaks free and massacres the staff. But Rose's DNA has changed it: it cannot bring itself to kill her and begins to feel fear and new emotions. Calling its mutation a sickness, the Dalek asks Rose to order it to die and destroys itself.
