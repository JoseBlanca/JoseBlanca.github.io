The genetics

The premise combines vampire mythology with biomedical research. Michael Dorn, a virologist working for a secret organisation that turns out to be part of a vampire conspiracy, experiments with the virus RKV-17 on homeless people, believing that it could cure HIV; an accidental injection turns him into a vampire. In the background, vampires are trying to develop a genetically engineered cure for their inability to cope with pollution, and the Council's enforcers include a genetically engineered human-vampire crossbreed. The film thus links vampirism to a virus and presents the research as unethical experimentation on vulnerable people, controlled by a sinister cabal that hunts down its own scientist. The story

In this low-budget Australian action-horror film, Michael Dorn is a virologist researching a virus called RKV-17 for a mysterious group known as the Council, which tells him that it might lead to a cure for HIV. His experiments use homeless people as subjects. When one of them struggles, Michael accidentally injects himself with the viral serum. The project's hired gun, the bounty hunter Lance Bates, tries to shoot him, but Michael escapes and discovers that he is turning into a vampire with a constant need for blood. While he tries to understand his condition, the Council sends Lance and Gage, a human-vampire crossbreed, to eliminate him. Part of the background is that vampires are trying to develop a genetically engineered cure for themselves because their lungs cannot cope with pollution. The film ends with Michael still on the run from his hunters.
