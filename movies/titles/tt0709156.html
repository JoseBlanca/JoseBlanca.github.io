The genetics

The episode is about a human made from alien DNA. Dr. Keffler, working for a rogue unit of the NID, an American intelligence agency, combined a human egg with Goa'uld DNA from a symbiote kept in an ancient jar to create Anna, a hybrid grown to adulthood within a short time (with nanites, according to one source). His aim is to use her as a conduit to the Goa'uld's genetic memory, which in the series passes knowledge from one generation to the next. He treats Anna as an instrument: she is kept in a cell, forced to draw her visions and, according to the Spanish source, switched between her own personality and that of the Goa'uld Sekhmet with electric shocks. Keffler, the son of a Nazi war criminal, is presented as a villain; reviewers saw no redeeming features in him, although the actor saw him as believing he could save millions. The experiment ends in a massacre and the deaths of both creator and creation. Genetic memory, the inheritance of acquired knowledge, has no basis in real biology.

The story

In this Stargate SG-1 episode, written by cast member Michael Shanks and directed by Amanda Tapping, an NID agent, Malcolm Barrett, calls SG-1 to a facility in Los Angeles where 32 members of a rogue NID unit have been massacred. Security footage shows that the killer is Anna, a young woman now held in a glass cell; the other survivor is Dr. Keffler, an older German scientist and the son of a Nazi war criminal. The facility holds Goa'uld artefacts that belonged to Sekhmet and were first recovered by the Germans. Anna tells Daniel that Keffler created her, and Carter's research shows that she is a human and Goa'uld hybrid. Anna's drawings reveal the combination of a Goa'uld ark, which turns out to contain a naquadah bomb. Keffler admits he made her to tap the Goa'uld genetic memory, and she has a second personality, Sekhmet. While the team works on the bomb, Anna escapes; she and Keffler are found dead.
