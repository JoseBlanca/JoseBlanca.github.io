The genetics

The Marvel miniseries is a spy thriller in which genetics provides the villain's weapon. The rebel Skrull leader Gravik, aided by scientists who are Skrulls in disguise, builds a machine that uses enhanced DNA to create a 'Super-Skrull' with special abilities, and his ultimate prize is the Harvest, a collection of DNA gathered from the most powerful Avengers after the Battle of Earth. The same machine, using the Extremis formula, has healed Talos's daughter G'iah from a gunshot wound. Gravik is presented as a villain whose grievance, the Skrulls' broken promise of a homeland, is taken seriously, and the series frames the plan as part of a terrorist conspiracy. Nick Fury's counter-move is to deliver the Harvest with the sample swapped, so that superhuman DNA becomes bait rather than power. The sources do not describe the ending. The idea that powers can be transferred by absorbing another being's DNA is comic-book fantasy.

The story

Nick Fury, the former director of S.H.I.E.L.D., returns to Earth after years in space when Maria Hill alerts him to a terrorist attack in Moscow carried out by a Skrull disguised as a CIA officer. His Skrull ally Talos explains that a rebel faction led by Gravik plans to take over Earth by impersonating humans and provoking a war between the great powers, a rebellion born of Fury's broken promise to find the Skrulls a new home; Talos also admits he let a million Skrull refugees settle secretly on Earth. Gravik's council of shapeshifters has replaced officials up to the British Prime Minister and, it emerges, Rhodey, who advises the US President, while Fury's own wife is a Skrull. Gravik builds a machine to create a 'Super-Skrull' from enhanced DNA, kills Maria Hill disguised as Fury and stabs Talos. To stop him, Fury retrieves the 'Harvest', a store of DNA collected from the most powerful Avengers, and hands it over, but has swapped the sample.
