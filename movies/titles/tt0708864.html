The genetics

Genetic engineering is the episode's twist. The Brunali are skilled genetic engineers who use a resequencer to adapt their crops to a harsh environment, a technology Icheb finds fascinating and which helps persuade him to stay. The Doctor later discovers that his parents engineered Icheb himself to carry an anti-Borg pathogen: they bore and raised him as a biological weapon, meant to be assimilated and to infect the Collective, and they deliberately send him back to the Borg. The parents' aim is to save their people from repeated Borg attacks, and the episode gives them a voice ('If we don't stop the Borg, the Brunali have no future'), but it frames using a child as a weapon as a betrayal of his autonomy. Seven's protection of Icheb and his choice to stay carry the message that a person is not bound by the purpose for which he was engineered. Anti-Borg pathogens recur in Star Trek; they have no real-world counterpart.

The story

After Voyager's first science fair, Captain Janeway tells Seven of Nine that the parents of Icheb, a former Borg drone in her care, have been found on his home planet, Brunali, and that he must return to them. Seven, who has grown protective of him, resists, and Icheb is reluctant to give up his studies. The Brunali live as hidden farmers because the Borg, whose transwarp conduit lies nearby, attack them repeatedly. Icheb's parents, Leucon and Yifay, say he was taken while visiting a fertilization array; after seeing their community and its genetic resequencer for crops, Icheb decides to stay. Seven then finds in Borg records that the Borg did not attack the planet when Icheb was taken. Meanwhile his parents sedate him and send him in a shuttle towards the conduit. Voyager rescues him as a Borg sphere appears, and the Doctor discovers that Icheb was genetically engineered to carry a pathogen against the Borg. Icheb remains on Voyager.
