The genetics

Cloning is the comic premise of this National Lampoon sex comedy. A machine produces an instant adult copy of a woman from a few drops of blood, and the clone is a blank slate that two young men try to shape into their fantasy partner. The scientists are a credit-stealing professor and an awkward student inventor, played for laughs rather than as a comment on research. The film is not interested in the science, and its depiction of cloning is pure fantasy: real cloning, as with Dolly the sheep in 1996, requires nuclear transfer into an egg and a pregnancy, and produces a baby rather than an adult. The film's only link to real debates is the idea, also common in more serious works, that a clone shares the original's appearance but not her memories or personality.

The story

Max Fleming, a graduate student, has built a cloning machine for the egotistical Dr. Jonas, who takes all the credit. Kate Carson, a magazine reporter, comes to interview Max about the research and accidentally cuts herself, letting a few drops of her blood fall into a cloning sample. That night Max runs the machine and creates an adult replica of Kate, whom he calls Repli-Kate. She has none of Kate's knowledge, so Max and his roommate Henry set out to educate her as their ideal woman: beer-drinking, sports-loving and sexually forward. Max soon realises that the woman he really wants is the original Kate. Jonas learns of the clone, kidnaps both women and plans to present "his" cloning breakthrough to scientists from around the world, but Max and Henry rescue them with the help of a clone of Jonas. Max takes over the lab and becomes famous, Max ends up with Kate and Henry with Repli-Kate.
