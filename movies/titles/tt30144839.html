The genetics

Genetics appears in a single, pivotal form: a paternity test. Lockjaw's fear that Willa might be his biological child, and that this would expose his past interracial relationship to a white supremacist secret society, drives the plot; when he finally captures her, he tests their DNA in front of her and confirms that he is her father. The scene turns genetic testing into a tool of power and intimidation, and the film contrasts biological parenthood with the bond of care: Lockjaw, the genetic father, wants Willa dead, while Bob, who raised her, risks everything to save her. No scientists appear, and the film is a political thriller rather than a story about genetics. The fast, on-the-spot test is not far from reality: automated "rapid DNA" instruments, developed for law enforcement, can produce a DNA profile from a cheek swab in about two hours or less, although the film does not show how the test is done.

The story

Pat Calhoun and Perfidia Beverly Hills are members of the French 75, a far-left revolutionary group. During a raid on an immigrant detention centre, Perfidia humiliates the commanding officer, Steven J. Lockjaw, who becomes obsessed with her, and she later has sex with him to escape arrest. She gives birth to a daughter, Charlene, then abandons her family, is arrested and betrays her comrades to Lockjaw. Pat and Charlene go into hiding as Bob and Willa Ferguson. Sixteen years later, Bob is a paranoid stoner and Willa a teenager. Lockjaw, now a powerful colonel invited to join a white supremacist secret society, sets out to find and kill Willa to hide his past interracial relationship and the possibility that she is his daughter. He captures her and tests their DNA, confirming that he is her biological father. The society sends its own killer after both of them; Lockjaw is shot, and Willa kills the assassin before being reunited with Bob.
