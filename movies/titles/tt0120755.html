The genetics

Genetics plays a minor role. The plot device is an engineered pathogen: Chimera is a virus designed in a pharmaceutical laboratory by a scientist the film calls a bio-genetics researcher, together with its cure, so that the company can create a market for the treatment. The names come from Greek myth, in which the Chimera was a monster combining parts of different animals and Bellerophon the hero who killed it. Nekhorvich is a sympathetic scientist coerced by his employer, and the film's villains are a rogue agent and a corporation seeking profit from a pandemic, not the researchers themselves. The film gives no detail on how the virus was built, and its 20-hour window between infection and the point at which the cure no longer works is a dramatic convenience. Its premise of a company that profits from both the disease and the remedy reflects a recurring suspicion of the pharmaceutical industry in thrillers of the period.

The story

In Sydney, Dr. Vladimir Nekhorvich, a scientist at Biocyte Pharmaceuticals, warns IMF agent Ethan Hunt that his employer forced him to develop a biological weapon, the Chimera virus, so that it could profit from its cure, Bellerophon. Rogue IMF agent Sean Ambrose, disguised as Hunt, steals the Bellerophon samples and kills Nekhorvich. Hunt recruits the thief Nyah Nordoff-Hall, Ambrose's ex-girlfriend, to infiltrate his circle. Ambrose blackmails Biocyte's chief executive, planning to release Chimera, which kills within 20 hours unless Bellerophon is given in time, and then sell the cure in exchange for control of the company. Hunt breaks into Biocyte's laboratory to destroy the virus, but Nyah injects herself with the last sample to keep it from Ambrose, who releases her in Sydney to start an epidemic. Hunt recovers the Bellerophon, kills Ambrose in a fight on the beach, and his team gives Nyah the cure in time.
