The genetics

Starbuck is about biological fatherhood rather than genetic science. The premise, a sperm donor who discovers that he has hundreds of children, raises questions about the anonymity of donors, the right of donor-conceived people to know their origins, and the risks of a single donor fathering very many children, such as accidental incest between half-siblings and the spread of genetic disease. The film presents the genetic tie as a powerful bond, and David's redemption lies in discovering a talent for fatherhood that goes beyond biology, so the story weighs heredity and upbringing together. The alias Starbuck refers to a famous Canadian Holstein bull whose semen sired a huge number of cattle. The writers raised the number of children to 533 after learning of a real donor with some 500 offspring.

The story

In this French-Canadian comedy, David Wozniak, an amiable but irresponsible deliveryman for his family's butcher shop, owes $80,000 to thugs, and his pregnant girlfriend Valérie doubts he could ever be a father. Then a lawyer from a sperm bank tells him that the many donations he made under the alias 'Starbuck' in 1988 have produced 533 children, 142 of whom have filed a class action to learn his identity. Opening their files, David secretly seeks out some of his biological children, among them an actor, a recovering drug user and a disabled young man, and acts as their guardian angel. Passing himself off as a stranger, he even joins them on a camping weekend. In the end, after winning damages from the clinic, he reveals himself as Starbuck, reconciles with Valérie and is welcomed by his many children when his new son is born.
