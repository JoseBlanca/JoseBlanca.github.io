The genetics

The episode is an extended argument about genetic perfectionism. The colony of Moab IV is a planned society built through genetic engineering and selective breeding, in which every person is designed from conception for a particular function, and its leaders see any outside contact as a threat to a carefully tuned order. The script undercuts this ideal from two directions. Geordi La Forge, blind from birth, points out that such a society would have screened him out, yet it is the technology built around his disability that saves the colony. And the colonists who leave show that being engineered for a role does not mean accepting it: their departure, framed by Picard as a matter of free will, suggests that a society fixed in advance by design leaves no room for individual choice. Bates, the colony's own scientist, is portrayed sympathetically. The themes anticipate later debates on embryo selection and on whether choosing traits before birth narrows a person's future, as explored in films such as GATTACA.

The story

The Enterprise tracks a stellar core fragment from a disintegrated neutron star that will pass close to Moab IV, home of a human colony founded about two centuries earlier. Its leader, Aaron Conor, refuses evacuation, because the colonists were genetically engineered and selectively bred so that each person fits a role in a perfectly balanced society, and leaving would destroy it. The colony's scientist, Hannah Bates, works with Geordi La Forge on a tractor beam to deflect the fragment, and La Forge adapts the technology of his VISOR to make it work, noting that the colony's planners would never have allowed someone born blind like him to exist. Meanwhile Troi and Conor become attracted to each other. The colony is saved, but Bates and 22 other colonists ask to leave with the Enterprise. Picard accepts their request as a matter of free will, and reflects that the Federation's contact may have harmed the society as much as the fragment would have.
