The genetics

This episode, often ranked among the worst in Star Trek, turns on a genetic transformation. Exceeding warp 10 makes Paris's DNA rewrite itself, giving him new organs, two hearts and an amphibious body; the Doctor explains it as an accelerated form of the natural evolution humans might undergo over millions of years, and the evolved Paris and Janeway even reproduce. The Doctor is a sympathetic, competent physician who restores their original genetic code with radiation. The science is notoriously wrong: evolution acts on populations over generations rather than transforming an individual, there is no predetermined 'next stage' of human evolution, and the geneticist Mohamed Noor has argued that mutations on such a scale would cause cancer or death rather than transformation. Producer Brannon Braga himself called the episode a 'royal, steaming stinker'.

The story

Voyager's crew finds a stable form of dilithium that could let a ship exceed warp 10, the theoretical limit at which a vessel would occupy every point in the universe at once, and so bring them home instantly. Lieutenant Tom Paris persuades Captain Janeway to let him pilot the test shuttle despite a small medical risk. He breaks the barrier, but soon develops allergies, loses the ability to breathe oxygen and begins to mutate. The Doctor concludes that his DNA is rewriting itself in an accelerated form of human evolution. Before he can be treated, Paris escapes, abducts Janeway and takes her to a swamp planet. When the crew finds them, both have become amphibian creatures and have produced three offspring. The offspring are left on the planet, and the Doctor uses anti-proton radiation to destroy the mutated DNA and restore Paris and Janeway to human form.
