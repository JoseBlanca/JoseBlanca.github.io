The genetics

The film turns on an experiment in heredity: a geneticist conceives a child by artificially inseminating a prostitute with a hanged murderer's semen and then raises her as his own daughter, to see what her origins will make of her. The story answers firmly on the side of nature: Alraune grows up heartless and destructive, and the film suggests that she has inherited her parents' corruption, although at the end she longs to become an ordinary feeling person. The Professor is a classic mad scientist whom his nephew and other scientists condemn for violating the laws of nature, and he ends alone and insane. Critics read the film as alarmist about artificial insemination, then a topic of public debate, and as an early expression of the belief that criminal and sexual traits are inherited, the kind of idea later taken up by Nazi race theory. Modern genetics does not support the inheritance of criminality in this simple way.

The story

Professor Jakob ten Brinken, a wealthy and celebrated geneticist, decides on an experiment in heredity. He has his reluctant nephew Franz Braun find a prostitute, and artificially inseminates her with the semen of a hanged murderer. The child, Alraune, is adopted by the Professor as his daughter and grows into a beautiful but cold young woman who runs off from her convent school, elopes and ends up performing in a circus. Brought home, she lives in luxury at the Professor's expense while he falls obsessively in love with her and wonders whether her nature comes from her mother. When Alraune finds his journal and learns how she was conceived, she stays to take revenge, seduces him and ruins him. As she prepares to leave, the Professor attacks her with a knife; Franz arrives in time, takes Alraune away and leaves the Professor alone and broken.
