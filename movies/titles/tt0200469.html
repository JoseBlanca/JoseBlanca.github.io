The genetics

Genetic engineering is the origin of the monster in this creature feature. Dr. McCabe has altered bats to be smarter, more aggressive and omnivorous, and his experiment escapes into the wild as a swarm of thousands. His first explanation, saving bats from extinction, gives way to the truth: he built them as killing machines and never seriously considered the risk. Reviews describe McCabe as a government scientist working on a shadowy, unexplained project, and the military's response, a deadline to bomb the area, adds a note of official cover-up. The film presents the scientist as a reckless, ultimately insane figure destroyed by his creation, while the heroine is a field biologist whose knowledge of real bat behaviour, such as hibernation in cold, saves the day. The genetics is never explained and should be read as a plot device; no real genetic modification could produce such coordinated, intelligent hunting behaviour.

The story

After a series of deaths in the small town of Gallup, Texas, the Centers for Disease Control calls in bat expert Dr. Sheila Casper and her assistant Jimmy. With Sheriff Emmett Kimsey, they learn from the scientist Dr. Alexander McCabe that the culprits are bats he has genetically modified to be more intelligent and omnivorous, supposedly to protect them from extinction. The bats, now numbering thousands, overrun the town and kill many people, including a CDC specialist. The National Guard evacuates Gallup and gives Casper 48 hours before it destroys the area. She plans to freeze the bats in their roost, an abandoned mine, so that they hibernate and die rather than scatter. During a siege at the school it emerges that McCabe deliberately designed the bats as the ultimate predator to kill humans; he walks outside thinking he can control them and is killed. Casper and Kimsey restart the coolant inside the mine, and the bats are sealed in and frozen.
