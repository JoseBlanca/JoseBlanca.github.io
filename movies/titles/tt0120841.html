Genetics on Screen

Species II

Spain: Especie mortal II

1998 Film

IMDb FilmAffinity

The genetics

Genetics underpins the film: human-alien hybridization, cloning and genetic disease. Government scientists have cloned the hybrid Sil to produce Eve, a "docile" version kept for study, and then switch on her alien DNA as a tracking tool, a gamble that backfires. The alien DNA spreads to humans and drives Ross to breed. The scientists are portrayed as working for the military, and Dr. Baker is ambivalent about the risks of her own creation. The resolution relies on human genetic variation: Gamble resists infection because he carries the sickle-cell trait, and his blood kills the hybrid. This loosely echoes the real case in which carrying one copy of the sickle-cell allele protects against severe malaria, an example of natural selection in humans, although the film's idea that a genetic disease could be lethal to an alien when injected is pure fantasy. The hybridization itself, with pregnancies lasting minutes, has no scientific basis.

The story

Three astronauts return from the first crewed mission to Mars, unaware that an alien substance from the soil samples has infected one of them, Commander Patrick Ross. On Earth, Ross has sex with several women, who undergo accelerated pregnancies and die giving birth to human-alien hybrids. Meanwhile, a military team led by Dr. Laura Baker has created Eve, a more docile clone of the alien-human hybrid Sil from the first film, to study the alien species. When the scientist Dr. Orinsky is killed, analysis reveals alien DNA similar to Eve's, and Baker brings back Press Lennox to hunt Ross. Eve's alien DNA is activated so she can track him telepathically. Baker discovers that astronaut Dennis Gamble was not infected because he carries the sickle-cell trait, and that the alien has no defence against human genetic diseases. Ross mates with Eve before the team kills him using Gamble's blood. In the final scene, Eve's womb begins to swell.
