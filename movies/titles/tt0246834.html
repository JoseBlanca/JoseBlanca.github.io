The genetics

This Disney Channel comedy, based on Mary C. Ryan's novel Me Two, uses cloning as a vehicle for a story about self-confidence. Will's clone appears as a teenager identical to him, and the differences in personality between the two boys make the point that identical genes do not mean identical people. The scientists who developed the cloning formula are cartoonish, greedy villains, and the dependence of the clone on a stabilising formula adds a note of fragility to artificial life. The ending, in which the family adopts the clone as a second son, treats him as a full person. Real cloning bears no resemblance to the film's: it cannot be done with a home science kit and does not produce a grown copy, but a new embryo that develops and ages normally. Genetics here is a playful premise rather than a subject the film examines.

The story

Will Browning, a struggling high-school student, is threatened with being sent to a strict summer camp unless his grades improve. He orders a science kit for a school project, but the kit has been contaminated with a cloning formula belonging to two greedy scientists, and Will accidentally creates a copy of himself, whom he names Twoie. Twoie is more confident than Will and takes his place at school, where he becomes popular, makes peace with Will's rival, asks out the girl Will likes and draws Will's reclusive grandfather out of his shell. Will grows jealous, and Twoie decides to leave town after a school dance. Will then learns from the scientists that Twoie will weaken and die without a stabilising formula. He steals it, the scientists capture Will by mistake, and Twoie rescues him. The scientists are arrested, Will tells his family the truth, and they welcome Twoie into the household.
