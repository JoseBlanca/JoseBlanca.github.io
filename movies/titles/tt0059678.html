The genetics

The film is an early thriller about laboratory-made pathogens. Station Three is a secret government laboratory that has developed a botulinus weapon and the 'Satan Bug', a virus capable of exterminating life on Earth, together with, according to German Wikipedia, a vaccine. The scientists themselves are mostly victims: the head scientist and a colleague are murdered by the villain, a millionaire and biochemist who infiltrated the laboratory under a stolen identity and uses the stolen agents to blackmail the government into destroying the facility. The hero recovers the virus, but the ironic last line, as he hands it back to the military, suggests that the danger lies in such weapons existing at all. Based on a novel by Alistair MacLean, the film reflects Cold War anxieties about biological warfare; the United States did run an offensive biological weapons programme until 1969, though no single virus could wipe out all life.

The story

Former intelligence agent Lee Barrett is brought back by his old boss after the security chief of Station Three, a secret American bioweapons laboratory, is murdered and its head scientist, Dr. Baxter, disappears. Inside the sealed laboratory Barrett finds Baxter dead and learns that all supplies of a botulinus toxin and of the 'Satan Bug', a new virus that could wipe out life on Earth within months, have been stolen. An extortionist calling himself Charles Reynolds Ainsley, a reclusive millionaire, threatens to release the Satan Bug unless Station Three is destroyed, and proves his intent by releasing botulinus in Key West, killing many people. Working with Ann Williams, the daughter of the general in charge, Barrett discovers that Ainsley has been posing as one of the laboratory's own scientists, Dr. Hoffman. Flasks are found and defused in Los Angeles, Ainsley falls to his death from a helicopter, and Barrett returns the Satan Bug to the authorities: 'we're right back where we started'.
