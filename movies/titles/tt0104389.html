The genetics

Genetics plays no real part in this psychological thriller. Its only link to the field is Michael Bartel, Claire's husband, whom Spanish- and German-language plot summaries describe as a biotechnology engineer or genetic engineer; English-language sources do not state his profession. His work appears only as background: Peyton's destruction of a funding proposal he has to submit is one of her many attacks on the Bartels. The film shows nothing of his research and does not use genetics as a theme; no scientific work is seen on screen, and nothing in the plot depends on his field. He is portrayed as a decent, supportive husband, and it is he who encourages Claire to report the abuse. If those summaries are right, the title is an example of a scientist appearing in a mainstream film in a purely incidental role, as an ordinary professional whose occupation signals the family's social standing. The story's real concerns are motherhood, trust and revenge.

The story

Claire Bartel, a Seattle housewife pregnant with her second child, is sexually assaulted by her new obstetrician, Dr. Victor Mott, during a check-up. Encouraged by her husband Michael, she reports him, other women come forward, and Mott kills himself. His pregnant widow loses her home and her baby, and has to have a hysterectomy; she blames Claire. Six months later, under the name Peyton Flanders, she is hired as the Bartels' nanny and sets out to destroy the family from within. She secretly breastfeeds the baby, turns the daughter against her mother, gets the loyal handyman Solomon fired, sabotages Michael's work, and plants evidence of an affair. When a family friend discovers her identity, Peyton kills her with a trap in Claire's greenhouse and empties Claire's asthma inhalers. Once unmasked, Peyton attacks the family to take the children, but Claire, helped by Solomon, defeats her, and Peyton dies in a fall.
