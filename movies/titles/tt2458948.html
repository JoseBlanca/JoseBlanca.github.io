The genetics

Cloning is part of the mythology that the film resolves, although the story is about grief, maturity and letting go. Two of the young pilots are artificial: Rei is one of a series of Ayanami clones whose bodies need LCL fluid to hold together, and Asuka learns that she is one of the Shikinami clone series. Gendo Ikari reveals that both series were made for the Human Instrumentality Project, the conspiracy of the SEELE organization to force the next stage of human evolution, which Gendo pursues to be reunited with his wife. The clones are sympathetic characters whose artificial origin brings loss, as when Rei dissolves after finding a moment of happiness. Gendo is the antagonist but is treated with understanding in the end. The film never explains how the clones are made, and its idea of evolution is mystical rather than biological.

The story

In the final film of the Rebuild of Evangelion series, the WILLE organization restores Paris from the effects of an earlier catastrophe while fighting NERV's Evangelions. In the ruins of Japan, the despondent Shinji, Asuka and a copy of Rei reach Village-3, a survivor settlement where Shinji's childhood friends, now adults, help him recover; Rei learns to live among people but, cut off from the LCL fluid her body needs, dissolves. Shinji joins WILLE's attack on NERV in Antarctica, where his father Gendo seeks to bring about a final transformation of humanity in order to be reunited with his dead wife, Yui. Asuka learns she is one of a series of clones, and Gendo reveals that the Shikinami and Ayanami clones were made for the Human Instrumentality Project. Shinji confronts his father in a surreal space, comes to understand him, and chooses to remake the world without Evangelions. The film ends with an adult Shinji in a real, live-action world.
