The genetics

Genetics appears only as a comic device in the Stewie subplot. Stewie's machine, meant to boost his own evil, instead produces a clone of him into which all his evil is concentrated, an idea borrowed from the Star Trek episode 'The Enemy Within'. The clone is a cartoon double rather than a product of genetic science: it appears instantly, fully grown and dressed, and the episode never explains how it was made. The other storyline, about Meg's crush on Joe, has nothing to do with genetics. The notion that a personality trait like evil could be separated into a copy has no basis in biology; a real clone shares a person's genome but not his character or memories.

The story

In this Family Guy episode, Bonnie Swanson asks Lois to look after her husband Joe and their baby while she visits her sick father, and Lois passes the job to Meg. Meg develops a crush on Joe, has Bonnie arrested at the airport by planting a gun in her luggage, and even throws herself in front of his police car hoping to end up in a wheelchair like him, before apologising and becoming his friend. In the second story, Brian tells Stewie that he has gone soft, so Stewie builds a machine to restore his evil nature. It seems to do nothing, but it has in fact created a truly evil clone of Stewie, who attacks Brian, overpowers Stewie and goes on a violent rampage. Stewie uses Brian as bait, and Brian shoots the clone after telling the two naked Stewies apart.
