The genetics

The film is a medical drama about an inherited disease. ALD is an X-linked disorder: women who carry the faulty gene often develop milder symptoms later in life, while their sons can develop the severe childhood form. The film shows Michaela learning that she passed the disease on to Lorenzo, and her guilt is part of the drama. The genetics is in the background, however; the story is about biochemistry and about lay parents confronting a slow and cautious medical establishment, represented by a well-meaning but hesitant specialist, Professor Nikolais. Hugo Moser, the real researcher on whom Nikolais was based, called the portrayal "an abomination", and the film was criticised for suggesting a miracle cure. Later research has not clearly shown that Lorenzo's oil helps once neurological symptoms have started, although it appeared to prevent onset in many boys treated before symptoms appeared. Lorenzo Odone died in 2008 at the age of 30, two decades longer than doctors had predicted.

How genetics is portrayed

Overall portrayal of genetics
neutral Genetics is the factual backdrop: ALD is explained as passed from carrier mothers to sons and Michaela struggles with guilt, but the drama is about lay parents' biochemical research against a cautious establishment, so the film colours parental love and perseverance, not genetic science.
Outcome of the genetic intervention
not applicable Nothing genetic is done: the Odones' oil is a dietary, biochemical treatment that lowers very-long-chain fatty acids, and the film never frames it as acting on genes; ALD's X-linked inheritance is only explained and lived with (Michaela's carrier guilt).

The story

Based on a true story, the film follows Augusto and Michaela Odone, whose young son Lorenzo begins to show neurological problems after the family moves from the Comoro Islands to the United States. He is diagnosed with adrenoleukodystrophy (ALD), a degenerative disease expected to kill him within two years. Finding that doctors can offer no treatment, the parents, who have no scientific training, study medical libraries and animal research, badger researchers, clash with a sceptical support group and organise an international symposium on the disease. They learn that oleic acid lowers the very long chain fatty acids that accumulate in ALD, and later add erucic acid, eventually persuading an elderly British chemist to produce a purified mixture of the two. The oil normalises Lorenzo's blood levels and halts the progression of his disease, although the damage already done recovers only very slowly. The film ends with boys treated early with the oil.
