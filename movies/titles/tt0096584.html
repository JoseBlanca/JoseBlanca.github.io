The genetics

The serial is built entirely around a genetic experiment: the creation of a human-gorilla hybrid by artificial insemination and its consequences. Forester is a classic overreaching scientist in the Frankenstein mould. He works for a state institute under military oversight, hides his true goal from his superiors, uses his own sperm, and falsifies records when ordered to destroy the child. Yet he also loves Gor and protects him, and the story weighs ambition, secrecy and paternal feeling. Gor looks entirely human and shows few gorilla traits beyond his strength and his fused vocal cords, which keeps the drama grounded until the ending, in which the hybrid is killed and his son reveals that the gorilla inheritance continues. Science fiction historian Brian Stableford dismissed it as a "knee-jerk hymn of hate against scientists". No human-ape hybrid has ever been verified; humans have 23 pairs of chromosomes and the other great apes 24, and Soviet attempts in the 1920s to cross humans with chimpanzees failed to produce a pregnancy.

The story

A three-part BBC serial adapted from Maureen Duffy's novel Gor Saga. Edward Forester works at a government institute officially trying to cross orangutans and gorillas; secretly, he is attempting a human-gorilla hybrid, using his own sperm. A gorilla named Mary gives birth to a seemingly human boy, Gor. When the government orders all hybrid research stopped and the embryos destroyed, Forester falsifies the records and has Gor fostered, later adopting him after surgery in Switzerland allows the boy, whose vocal cords are fused like a gorilla's, to speak. As a young man, Gor falls in love with Forester's daughter Nell, unaware she is his half-sister. Digging into his past, he learns the truth about his origins and his father. He enters Mary's cage and is killed by her. Months later, Nell's newborn son makes the same sounds Gor made as a baby.
