The genetics

Genetics is the origin of both hero and villain. Unlike the radioactive spider of the comics, Peter's powers come from a genetically engineered spider bred at Oscorp, and his father's work on cross-species genetics underlies the whole story. Dr. Curt Connors, the geneticist, is a sympathetic figure: he wants to use reptile DNA to help people like himself regrow lost limbs, and he initially resists his superior's demand to rush human trials, fearing harm to patients. The catastrophe comes from corporate pressure and from Connors's desperation to heal himself, after which he becomes a monster who wants to spread his transformation to everyone. Some vertebrates, including salamanders and some lizards that regrow their tails, can regenerate lost body parts, and researchers study the genetics of this ability for regenerative medicine. Transferring it with a serum, though, is fantasy, as is the instant and total transformation of an adult human body.

The story

Years after his scientist father Richard disappeared, teenager Peter Parker finds his father's briefcase and learns that he worked with Dr. Curt Connors, an Oscorp scientist in the field of cross-species genetics. Sneaking into an Oscorp lab, Peter is bitten by a genetically modified spider and gains spider-like strength, agility and the ability to cling to walls. Connors, who has lost his right arm, is trying to regenerate limbs using lizard DNA, and Peter gives him a missing equation from his father's notes. After the murder of his Uncle Ben, Peter becomes Spider-Man. When Oscorp pushes for rushed human trials, Connors tests the serum on himself: his arm grows back, but he turns into a giant reptile, the Lizard, and plans to release a chemical agent that would turn the city's population into reptilian hybrids like himself. With help from Gwen Stacy, who creates an antidote, Peter stops him.
