Batman Beyond: Return of the Joker

The genetics

Genetics plays a limited but decisive role, as the solution to the film's mystery. The Joker's return is explained by a piece of stolen biotechnology: a DNA microarray, implanted secretly in Tim Drake, that carries the Joker's genetic information and personality and gradually allows him to take over Tim's body. Genetics thus stands in for a kind of resurrection, and the twist turns a former hero into the villain's host. There are no geneticists in the film; the technology is used by a criminal, not by scientists. The story mixes genetics and psychology freely, implying that a person's character and memories could be stored with their DNA and imposed on another body. DNA microarrays are real laboratory tools, also called DNA chips, but they are used to measure the activity of many genes at once or to genotype regions of a genome, not to store or transfer a personality.

The story

Decades in the future, the Joker, presumed dead for forty years, reappears in Gotham City at the head of a new gang, the Jokerz, who steal high-tech equipment. He shows that he knows the secrets of the elderly Bruce Wayne and his successor as Batman, Terry McGinnis. Commissioner Barbara Gordon reveals what really happened: the Joker and Harley Quinn kidnapped and tortured Robin, Tim Drake, turning him into a copy of the Joker, until Tim came to his senses and shot him. Terry suspects Tim, now an engineer with a family, and confronts him, only to see him turn into the Joker. The Joker explains that he had encoded himself into a stolen DNA microarray hidden behind Tim's ear, allowing him to take over Tim's body after death. Using the stolen equipment to control a military satellite, he plans to destroy Gotham. Terry defeats him by destroying the chip with the Joker's own joy buzzer, freeing Tim.
