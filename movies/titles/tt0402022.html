The genetics

Cloning is the hidden foundation of the society in which the film is set. After the cure for a pandemic left humanity infertile, Bregna's rulers sustain the population by cloning the dead from DNA stored in an airship, so that people are reborn again and again with fragments of their previous lives surfacing as nightmares. Genetics thus serves as a tool of political control: the Goodchild dynasty has ruled for four centuries through its own clones, and when nature begins to restore fertility, Oren kills the pregnant women to preserve its power. Trevor, the scientist-ruler, is more ambiguous, a well-meaning researcher trying to cure infertility within a system built on deception. The film ends with a rejection of endless cloning in favour of natural reproduction and mortality. As science, it is speculative: cloning produces a genetic copy, not a continuation of the same person, and the idea of inherited memories reflects the story's themes of identity rather than biology.

The story

In 2011 a virus kills 99 percent of humanity. Four centuries later, the last five million people live in Bregna, a walled city-state ruled by a congress of scientists, where people regularly disappear and are plagued by nightmares. Æon Flux, an agent of the Monican rebels, is sent to kill the city's leader, Trevor Goodchild, after her sister Una is killed. She discovers that she is being manipulated by council members plotting a coup, and that every inhabitant of Bregna is a clone grown from recycled DNA, because the cure for the virus left humans infertile. Æon is the first clone in 400 years of Katherine, the original Trevor's wife. Trevor has been trying to reverse the infertility, and Una was pregnant as part of his experiments, but his brother Oren had the fertile women killed to keep the Goodchild clone dynasty in power. Æon turns against Oren and destroys the Relical, the airship storing the DNA, and its crash breaks open the city wall.
