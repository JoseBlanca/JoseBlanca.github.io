The genetics

Genetics is the subject of the series. DNA testing is used for genetic genealogy, matching participants with relatives and estimating their ancestry, and for medical genetics, testing for inherited disease. The presenter-scientist, Turi King, is a real geneticist known for leading the DNA identification of the remains of Richard III in 2012, and she is portrayed as the expert who explains the science and delivers the results; reviewers singled out her empathy with participants. The programme shows the social side of genetic testing: hidden paternity, donor conception, adoption and mixed-race heritage, which The Guardian described as "a touching, timely portrait of mixed-race Britain". It also illustrates how DNA testing can now uncover biological relatives and family secrets that paperwork alone could not reveal.

The story

DNA Family Secrets is a British documentary series that began on BBC Two in 2021, presented by Stacey Dooley and the geneticist Professor Turi King. Each episode follows three people: two who want to learn about their family history or ancestry, and one who seeks answers about a genetic disease in the family. Working with genealogists, social workers and doctors, King and her team use DNA testing to reveal unknown ancestry, trace missing relatives and detect inherited disease risk. Cases include people searching for fathers they never knew, such as children of African American soldiers stationed in Britain during the Second World War, adopted triplets seeking their father's origins, a woman conceived with donor sperm looking for her biological father, and sisters who want to know whether they share the same father. A second series was broadcast in 2022.
