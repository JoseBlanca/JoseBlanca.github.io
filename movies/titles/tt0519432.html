The genetics

Genetic engineering of humans is the subject of the episode. Splicing human and animal DNA is presented as a commercial cosmetic service for teenagers, a body-modification fashion compared on screen with tattoos, and the plot turns on its unforeseen side effects, especially increased aggression and loss of control. Dr. Cuvier is a classic mad scientist: a private entrepreneur who markets the procedure as safe and "utterly beautiful", orders the murder of the official trying to regulate him, and finally transforms himself into a monster. Bruce Wayne, by contrast, produces a cure. The genetics is pure comic-book fantasy: injecting animal DNA into an adult cannot rapidly rewrite the body's form, and real human gene therapy delivers specific genes to particular cells for medical purposes. The episode is nonetheless a clear parable about unregulated enhancement and consumer biotechnology aimed at the young.

The story

A new fad sweeps the teenagers of Gotham: "splicing", offered by Dr. Abel Cuvier's Chimera Institute, which mixes animal DNA into people to give them traits such as cat eyes, claws or horns. Cuvier insists the procedure is safe and reversible, but District Attorney Sam Young is trying to ban it because it increases aggressive behaviour. When Batman (Terry McGinnis) investigates, Cuvier's spliced thugs capture him and Cuvier injects him with vampire bat DNA. While the spliced gang attacks Young and Barbara Gordon, Terry turns into a raging bat-like creature, until Bruce Wayne subdues him with an antidote. With the help of the dog Ace, Batman tracks Cuvier to a taxidermy studio. Cuvier injects himself with several animal strains to become a "true chimera"; Batman injects him with more, turning him into a monstrous creature that apparently dies in an explosion.
