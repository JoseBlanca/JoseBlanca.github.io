The genetics

The whole episode hangs on heredity. The 'Simpson gene' is a comic version of an inherited disorder with a predictable onset: it explains the family's stupidity as genetic destiny and threatens Lisa with the same fate. The resolution is a small lesson in sex-linked inheritance: Dr. Simpson explains that the faulty gene lies on the Y chromosome, which only males carry, so the women of the family are spared and Lisa is safe, while Bart is cheerfully told he is doomed. Genetics appears both as a curse and as the explanation that lifts it, and no one does any genetic work. As science, the joke is loose but not absurd. Genes on the Y chromosome do pass only from father to son, but the Y carries few genes and they mostly concern male development; intelligence depends on many genes spread across the genome, and on environment.

How genetics is portrayed

The story

Lisa Simpson cannot solve a brain teaser that her classmates, even Bart and Nelson, crack easily, and soon she forgets her locker combination and fumbles her saxophone. Grampa tells her about the 'Simpson gene': all Simpsons start out smart but lose their intelligence as they grow up. After a horrifying vision of her future and a television plea to Springfield to treasure its brains, Lisa despairs. Homer gathers the extended Simpson family to prove her wrong, but the men all turn out to be failures. At Marge's urging he talks to the women, who are all successful professionals, among them a surgeon, Dr. Simpson, who explains that the defective gene is carried on the Y chromosome and affects only males. Relieved, Lisa solves the puzzle. In a subplot, Apu turns a frozen Jasper into a tourist attraction.
