The genetics

Genetics in the strict sense plays almost no part. The Baron builds his creatures by surgery, stitching together parts of corpses, and nothing in the story involves genes, heredity mechanisms or laboratory genetics. The link to the theme is eugenic: his stated goal is to breed a new, obedient Serbian race from an ideal couple, choosing body parts for "racial" features such as the shape of the nose and for sexual drive, and treating people as raw material for that project. The film, produced by Andy Warhol and directed by Paul Morrissey, plays this as gory, deliberately camp satire of the mad scientist and his fantasies of a master race, and the experiment ends in the deaths of everyone involved. It is of interest here mainly as a parody of eugenic ambitions rather than as a story about genetics.

The film stages him as grotesque and murderous (he kills Sacha for his head and kills Otto), and his own creature kills him.

The story

In 19th-century Serbia, Baron Frankenstein neglects his wife and sister, Katrin, while assembling a "perfect" man and woman from parts of corpses. He means to mate them and found a new Serbian race that will obey his every command. Obsessed with the male creature's appearance and libido, he checks that its nose has the proper Serbian shape and looks for a more sexually driven head to replace its own. With his assistant Otto, he kills a young man on his way to a monastery, mistaking him for a lusty farmhand, Nicholas, and grafts his head onto the creature. Nicholas, who has become Katrin's lover, discovers the laboratory and is captured. The experiment collapses in carnage: Katrin and the female creature die, the Baron kills Otto, and the male creature, recovering memories of its former life, kills the Baron and then itself.
