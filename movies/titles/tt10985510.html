The genetics

Genetics appears in one episode of this Cold War superhero fable. Lex Luthor, America's top scientist, collects Superman's cells from a crashed satellite and creates a clone, the 'Superior Man', to fight him on the country's behalf; the clone is powered remotely, and when Luthor overloads it to keep up with Superman, it degenerates and dissolves into protoplasm. Superman, who is appalled, sees the clone as a living being sacrificed as a weapon, and the scene marks Luthor as ruthless in his early role as Superman's adversary, before he becomes a successful president who finally allies with Superman. The film's broader theme is the danger of absolute power on both sides of the Cold War rather than science as such; its other technologies, from red-sun lamps to Brainiac's lobotomies, are not genetic. Growing a full-grown superhuman clone from a few cells is comic-book fantasy.

The story

In this alternate history, the infant Kal-El lands in a Ukrainian collective farm instead of Kansas and grows up to become the 'Soviet Superman', presented to the world by Stalin in 1955. President Eisenhower recruits America's best scientist, Lex Luthor, to counter him. After discovering that Stalin imprisoned his childhood friend Svetlana in a gulag, Superman kills the dictator and takes over the Soviet Union, ending wars and spreading communism, allied with Wonder Woman. Luthor unleashes a clone of Superman, grown from cells collected from a crashed satellite and powered remotely; to keep up in battle he overloads it until it dissolves, appalling Superman. Superman reprograms the alien Brainiac to help him rule, using its technology to lobotomize dissidents, and survives a trap set by a Russian Batman, who then kills himself. Luthor becomes US president and leads a Green Lantern corps against Superman. Superman, realizing he has become a tyrant, joins Luthor to defeat the treacherous Brainiac and disappears into ordinary life.
