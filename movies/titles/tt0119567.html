The genetics

Genetics is the background rather than the subject of this sequel. The dinosaurs are the genetically engineered animals of the first film, and Isla Sorna is where InGen produced them, but the story is about what happens once created animals live and breed on their own: a self-sustaining population of cloned dinosaurs, complete with family groups and parental care. The conflict is between exploitation and conservation. Ludlow treats the animals as company property to be captured and displayed, while Hammond, now remorseful, wants them protected from human interference, and the ending turns the island into a reserve. No geneticist plays a significant role, and the cloning process itself is not revisited. The same scientific objection as for Jurassic Park applies: DNA does not survive for tens of millions of years, so dinosaur cloning is not possible with real ancient DNA.

The story

Four years after the disaster at Jurassic Park, a girl is attacked by small dinosaurs on Isla Sorna, InGen's "Site B", where the animals were bred before being moved to the park and which was abandoned after a hurricane. Peter Ludlow, Hammond's nephew, takes control of InGen and plans to capture the dinosaurs for a new park in San Diego. Hammond sends mathematician Ian Malcolm and a small team to document the animals living in the wild and argue for leaving them alone; Malcolm goes mainly to rescue his girlfriend, palaeontologist Sarah Harding. The two groups clash on the island, and after a pair of Tyrannosaurus attack them in search of their injured infant, survivors of both teams cross the island under attack from Velociraptors. Ludlow ships a male Tyrannosaurus to San Diego, where it escapes and rampages through the city before Malcolm and Harding lure it back to the ship. The island is then declared a nature reserve.
