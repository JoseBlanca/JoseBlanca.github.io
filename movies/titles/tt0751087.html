Brand X

The genetics

Genetic engineering is the premise of the episode, though it receives little explanation. A tobacco company has created a "super-tobacco" carrying a genetically engineered beetle whose eggs survive manufacturing and hatch inside smokers' lungs, and the company's scientists tested the product on human subjects before covering up the deaths. The writers, fans of the 1999 film The Insider, drew on the tobacco industry's corporate cover-ups, so the scientists are portrayed as employees of a company that places profit above safety, with one of them eventually coming clean. The genetic engineering is a horror device rather than a plausible technology, and the cure, nicotine, is an ironic twist rather than a scientific one.

The story

Assistant Director Walter Skinner is protecting Dr. James Scobie, a former researcher for the Morley tobacco company who is about to testify against his former employer. Scobie develops a cough and is found dead with his face eaten away. Mulder and Scully learn that Morley has developed a "super-tobacco" containing a genetically engineered tobacco beetle whose eggs survive cigarette manufacture and are released in the smoke. Scobie and his colleague Dr. Peter Voss tested the cigarettes on four people, and only Darryl Weaver, a chain smoker, has survived. More victims die covered in beetles, and Mulder is exposed to Weaver's smoke and begins coughing up blood as the eggs hatch in his lungs. Voss tells Skinner about the tests, and Skinner tracks down Weaver and stops him. Scully realises that nicotine stopped the beetles from developing in Weaver and saves Mulder with it, leaving him with a craving for cigarettes.
