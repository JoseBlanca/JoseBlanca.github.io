The genetics

Brandon Cronenberg's satire imagines biotechnology at the service of celebrity worship. Clinics harvest pathogens from stars and modify them before resale, adding a 'copy protection' that keeps them from spreading; a rival company engineers and patents a modified strain, deliberately infects the star with it so that it can sell her disease, and finally grows her cells into a deformed living 'cell garden' that serves as a factory for new viruses. Butchers sell meat cultured from celebrities' cells. The practitioners are corporations and their employees, presented as cold, mercenary businesses that turn bodies into products, and the protagonist ends by joining them. Like the film's grotesque body horror, the biotechnology serves to criticise consumer culture rather than to portray science. Copy-protected viruses are invented, though modifying viruses and culturing human cells and tissues are real techniques.

The story

In a near-future Toronto, Syd March works for the Lucas Clinic, which harvests viruses from celebrities and injects them into fans who want to share their idols' illnesses; the pathogens carry a 'copy protection' that makes them non-contagious. Syd smuggles viruses out in his own body and sells them to Arvid, who works at a butcher selling meat grown from celebrities' cells. After injecting himself with blood from the star Hannah Geist, Syd falls gravely ill, and Hannah is reported dead. He learns that she is secretly alive, that her virus was engineered to resist analysis and patented by the rival firm Vole & Tesser, and that a piracy gang wants to broadcast his death. Syd escapes, realises that Vole & Tesser infected Hannah to harvest their own virus, and joins the firm, which now grows her cells in a grotesque 'cell garden' and sells the viruses injected into them.
