The genetics

The episode imagines a genetically targeted weapon: a toxin that kills only people who share a particular inherited trait, such as brown eyes or the ancestry of a Jewish family. The villain is a Nazi scientist still pursuing the racial goals of the Third Reich, which places the story in the history of Nazi racial science, while Walter learns that his own father was involved in the original research before defecting to the Allies. The ending complicates the portrayal of the scientist as hero, since Walter uses the same technique to tailor a poison to Hoffman's DNA and kill him. The episode also reveals that the seahorse seen in the series' promotional images is a genetically encoded signature created by Walter's father. A poison that selectively kills people according to their eye-colour genes or ancestry is fiction.

The story

Fifteen guests suffocate at a Jewish wedding, all of them from the groom's side, whose grandmother survived the Holocaust; Walter Bishop concludes that the poison targeted a shared genetic trait. When customers with brown eyes die the same way in a coffee shop, fingerprints identify the killer as Alfred Hoffman, a Nazi scientist who is somehow still alive at over 100. Walter realises that Hoffman probably worked with his own father, Robert Bishoff, a German scientist who defected to the United States in 1943, on a toxin that, released as a heated gas, can be keyed to any trait using the DNA of the target, especially of those outside the "master race". The team finds Hoffman's laboratory and learns that he plans to strike a convention promoting world equality. Walter disperses his own toxin, tailored to Hoffman, and kills him. Peter later recovers his grandfather's papers, which include a photograph of Bishoff and Hoffman together.
