The genetics

The zombies come from a military bioweapon programme. In a secret laboratory, Dr. Chushfeld, working with the US military and, according to the German Wikipedia, a company called W-Industries, develops a transmissible virus to reanimate dead Marines as fearless soldiers, to overcome a shortage of troops for endless wars. The virus breaks containment, infecting test subjects and scientists, and in the German account it reacts differently with the female chromosome set, turning the infected strippers into super-strong, still-lucid dancers rather than mindless ghouls. The satire is aimed at the government and the military: at the end, the administration is revealed to have let the outbreak escape deliberately as a political distraction. The scientist is part of the conspiracy and the creation is lethal, so the film follows the familiar pattern of the lab-made zombie plague. Loosely inspired by Ionesco's Rhinoceros, it is a deliberately trashy horror comedy, and its virology is not meant seriously.

The story

In a satirical near future, George W. Bush is serving a fourth term, Congress has been dissolved, public nudity is banned and the United States is at war with much of the world. To overcome a shortage of soldiers, a secret laboratory in Sartre, Nebraska, run by Dr. Chushfeld, develops a virus that reanimates dead Marines for combat. The virus escapes, infecting test subjects and scientists, and a Marine 'Z Squad' is sent to destroy the zombies. One bitten Marine flees into an alley, dies and comes back to life inside the Rhino, an illegal strip club run by Ian Essko. He bites the star dancer Kat, who returns to the stage as a zombie and becomes more popular than ever, so the other dancers choose to be infected too, and the zombie strippers devour customers in private dances. When the zombies break loose, the Z Squad storms the club and learns that the Bush administration let the plague escape to distract the public from its failures.
