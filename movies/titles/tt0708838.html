The genetics

Cloning and population genetics are central. A colony founded by only five people chooses cloning over sexual reproduction, and after generations of copies faces "replicative fading", an accumulation of small errors in the genetic code that will soon make new clones nonviable. The solution is not more technology but genetic diversity: Dr. Pulaski persuades the Mariposans to mix with the Bringloidi so that a varied gene pool can sustain the colony. The Mariposan scientists are polite but willing to take DNA without consent, and the episode frames bodily integrity and individual uniqueness as reasons to refuse being copied. The idea loosely echoes a real concern: very small founding populations suffer from inbreeding and loss of genetic diversity, although "replicative fading" itself is a fictional device. The episode drew criticism because Riker's destruction of his clones was read as a stance on abortion.

hero Aim: benevolent (positive). The Enterprise's chief medical officer (Starfleet, hence 'other') diagnoses the clones' degradation and designs the genetic solution, a merged gene pool with several fathers per woman for generations; she is the episode's sympathetic voice of reason and her plan saves the colony.

The story

The Enterprise answers a distress call from Bringloid V, a colony threatened by solar flares and founded centuries earlier by the crew of the SS Mariposa. Its inhabitants, led by Danilo Odell and his daughter Brenna, live a pre-industrial Irish rural life and are evacuated aboard the ship. Odell mentions a second Mariposa colony, and Riker, Worf and Dr. Pulaski visit it. Pulaski discovers that all its people are clones: only five scientists survived the original landing, too few for a viable population, so they reproduced by cloning alone. After almost three centuries, accumulated copying errors are about to make further clones nonviable. Prime Minister Granger asks for the crew's DNA, and when Riker refuses, the Mariposans abduct him and Pulaski and grow copies of them, which Riker destroys. Pulaski proposes instead that the two colonies merge to create a viable gene pool.
