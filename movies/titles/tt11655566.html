The genetics

Genetic engineering creates the protagonist: Stitch is the product of Jumba Jookiba's illegal genetic experiments, an engineered creature built to be aggressive and nearly indestructible. The story's heart is a nature-versus-nurture fable: a being engineered for destruction learns empathy and becomes part of a family through Lilo's love, and his maker's plan to erase that empathy fails. Compared with the 2002 animated film, this remake makes Jumba the main villain, more malicious and described by the director as a 'delinquent father figure', while the galactic authorities treat genetic experimentation as a crime. Lesser genetic devices include 'cloning devices' that let the aliens take on human appearance from DNA samples, and a DNA sample of Stitch taken by the CIA agent Cobra Bubbles. The genetics is cartoon fantasy.

The story

In this live-action remake of the 2002 animated film, the alien scientist Jumba Jookiba is convicted of illegal genetic experimentation for creating Experiment 626, an aggressive, near-indestructible creature that learns fast. 626 escapes exile and crash-lands on Kauaʻi, and the Grand Councilwoman offers Jumba his freedom if he recaptures it, with the agent Pleakley as minder. Lonely six-year-old Lilo, cared for by her overstretched older sister Nani under the eye of a social worker, adopts 626 from an animal shelter, believing he is a dog, and names him Stitch. Stitch's chaos costs Nani her job and nearly drowns Lilo, and Nani agrees to give up guardianship. Jumba, whose deal is cancelled, captures Stitch, intending to erase his newfound empathy, but Lilo frees him; Stitch saves Lilo from drowning and is himself revived. The Councilwoman re-arrests Jumba and lets Stitch stay on Earth with his ʻohana, and Nani goes to college to study marine biology.
