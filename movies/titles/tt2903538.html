The genetics

This independent film is one of the few dramas about the history of genetics. It recreates the Fly Room at Columbia University, where from 1910 Thomas Hunt Morgan and his students, among them Calvin Bridges, Alfred Sturtevant and Hermann Joseph Muller, used the fruit fly Drosophila melanogaster to establish that genes lie on chromosomes. Bridges's doctoral thesis, "Non-disjunction as proof of the chromosome theory of heredity", was the first paper in the first issue of the journal Genetics in 1916, and he is also known for his maps of the giant polytene chromosomes of fly salivary glands. The film shows science as curiosity and patient work, seen through the eyes of a child, but also explores what its makers call the irony of a geneticist unable to understand his own genetic role within the family; it closes with the real Betsey and her descendants as an image of heredity. The director, Alexis Gambis, is himself a biologist.

The story

The film portrays the relationship between the geneticist Calvin Bridges and his daughter Betsey, mostly set in the original Fly Room, Thomas Hunt Morgan's fruit-fly laboratory at Columbia University. Ten-year-old Betsey spends time in the lab, where she meets the scientists and their flies and asks questions that help explain difficult scientific concepts; the film moves between the practical world of the laboratory and an imaginary realm where flies and humans mingle. The story is told from Betsey's perspective at ten and at twenty-one: as a young woman, remembering the time of her father's death, she comes to terms with her difficult childhood and with her relationship with him, and decides to build a life of her own with her fiancé, Ed. A coda features the real Betsey at the age of ninety-five, celebrating her birthday surrounded by her children, grandchildren and great-grandchildren.
