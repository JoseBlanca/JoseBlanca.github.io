The genetics

Eugenics frames the whole film: its prologue compares the selective breeding of animals with human reproduction, and the misfortunes of the Joyce family are presented as the story that follows from that comparison, placing the film within the eugenics movement of the 1910s. The scientist figure is a physician, Dr. Von Eiden, who treats Larry's syphilis, cures his cruelty with an operation and ends up marrying May, so medicine is portrayed as a source of rescue rather than harm. The family's troubles, however, are shown as coming from a mix of causes, including the father's abuse, venereal disease, alcohol and poverty, not only from inheritance. Because no copy of the film is known to survive, its plot is known only from written accounts, and how strongly it argued the eugenic case on screen cannot be checked today.

The story

The film opens with a prologue on eugenics that contrasts the careful breeding of animals with the way humans reproduce, and then tells the story of the Joyce family. The abusive father, Jim Joyce, corrupts his naturally sensitive son Larry into cruelty. Larry contracts syphilis and is treated by Dr. Von Eiden, who becomes involved with Larry's sister May and encourages her to leave the family. Unable to find stable work, May becomes the companion of a wealthy senator but refuses to marry him. Her sister Jennie marries the brutal "Bull" McGee, who kills their baby while drunk; Jennie loses her mind, rocking a doll in the empty cradle, and McGee sells her into prostitution. May rescues her, but Jennie dies soon afterwards. Von Eiden performs an operation that restores Larry's original sensitivity, and May leaves the senator and agrees to marry the doctor. The film, directed by D. W. Griffith, is now lost.
