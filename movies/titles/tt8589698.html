The genetics

Mutation is the franchise's founding idea, and this animated film puts the scientist who causes it at the centre of its origin story. Baxter Stockman is a rogue researcher who creates a mutagen not for profit or power but because he wants a family, an outcast whose first creation, Superfly, ends up sharing his resentment of humanity. The corporate institute that hunts him, TCRI, is shown as colder still, ready to kill him and later to experiment on the turtles to recover the mutagen. The mutants can be read as outsiders seeking acceptance, and the conflict between Superfly, who wants to mutate the world's animals to subjugate humans, and the turtles, who want to be accepted, frames the story. Mutagens are real, in the sense that chemicals and radiation can cause mutations in DNA, but they cause random changes that are usually harmful; nothing could turn an animal into a talking humanoid. A "retro-mutagen" reversing such a change is equally fictional.

The story

Baxter Stockman, a rogue scientist, creates a mutagenic ooze with which he plans to build his own family of mutant animals, starting with a housefly. A strike force sent by Cynthia Utrom of the Techno Cosmic Research Institute (TCRI) attacks his laboratory and he is killed, but the mutagen falls into the New York sewers, turning four baby turtles and a rat, Splinter, into humanoid mutants. Splinter, who distrusts humans, raises the turtles as his sons and trains them in ninjutsu. Fifteen years later the teenage Leonardo, Raphael, Donatello and Michelangelo, longing to be accepted by humans, help the student journalist April O'Neil investigate a mutant criminal called Superfly. He turns out to be Stockman's first creation, the leader of a gang of mutants planning to mutate every animal on Earth to dominate humanity. After TCRI captures the turtles to extract their mutagen, they and the other mutants turn against Superfly, who becomes a giant monster, and defeat him with a retro-mutagen, winning the city's acceptance.
