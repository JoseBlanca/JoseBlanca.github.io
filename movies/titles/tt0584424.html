The genetics

Cloning is the premise of this comic episode, which introduces Cubert Farnsworth, the professor's clone and designated heir. The story plays with the popular notion that a clone is a younger copy who should inherit the original's identity and vocation: Cubert shares Farnsworth's DNA, which lets the crew pass Fry off as the professor using a sample of the boy's blood, yet he has his own opinions and initially rejects his "father's" career. By the end he also shares the professor's ability to dream up the ship's engines, a joke on the idea that genes carry knowledge. Farnsworth is the series' archetypal mad scientist, eccentric and self-absorbed, but here he is treated sympathetically as an old man afraid of death. A real clone would share the original's genome but would be a separate person, with no inherited memories or skills.

The story

Professor Farnsworth is lured to Mars University, apparently to lose his professorship, only to find a surprise party for his 150th birthday. Confronted with his own mortality, he announces his successor: not one of his employees but Cubert, a 12-year-old clone of himself. Cubert is unimpressed by science and mocks the professor's inventions, including the Planet Express ship's engines, which Farnsworth says he learned to build in a dream. A depressed Farnsworth confesses that he is really 160, the age at which the robots of the Sunset Squad take people away, and he is duly abducted to the Near-Death Star. The crew infiltrate it with Fry disguised as the professor and Cubert on his back as a hump, carrying a jar of Cubert's blood as a DNA sample, and rescue him. When the ship's engines are damaged, Cubert fixes them, having dreamed how, and decides to follow in Farnsworth's footsteps.
