The genetics

Based on the real discovery of Richard III's remains in 2012, the film touches genetics through identification. John Ashdown-Hill's genetic genealogy work on a living descendant of Richard's sister supplies the reference against which the skeleton can be tested, and, according to the German Wikipedia, a DNA test confirms the identity. The film does not dwell on the science; its drama is Philippa Langley's amateur persistence against an academic establishment shown as dismissive and quick to claim credit. That portrayal is disputed: the University of Leicester called it misleading, the archaeologist Mike Pitts criticised its picture of science, and its depiction of the university's Richard Taylor was ruled defamatory and settled with a disclaimer.

The story

Philippa Langley, an Edinburgh office worker with myalgic encephalomyelitis who has just been passed over for promotion, sees a school production of Richard III and comes to identify with the maligned king, who begins to appear to her in visions. She joins the Richard III Society and sets out to find his lost grave in Leicester. There the historian John Ashdown-Hill, who is publishing a genetic genealogy study of a Canadian descendant of Richard's sister, encourages her, and a car-park space marked "R" gives her a strong feeling. With the reluctant help of the University of Leicester archaeologist Richard Buckley and money she crowdfunds herself, she gets a dig started; a skeleton with a curved spine and a fatal head wound is found on the first day. The university takes over the project and sidelines her at the announcement, though she receives an MBE and Richard is reburied in Leicester Cathedral.
