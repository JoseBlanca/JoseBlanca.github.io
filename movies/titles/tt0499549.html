The genetics

Genetics underpins the premise rather than the drama. The avatars are genetically engineered Na'vi-human hybrid bodies, each genetically matched to a human operator who controls it with his mind; because of this matching, Jake can take over the avatar grown for his dead identical twin. The avatars let humans breathe Pandora's air and approach the Na'vi, and Jake's life in his avatar is what turns him from corporate spy into a defender of the clan. The scientists of the Avatar Program, led by the exobiologist Dr. Grace Augustine, are portrayed sympathetically: they study the Na'vi and Pandora's ecosystem, oppose the RDA's brutality and join the rebellion. The villains are the mining corporation and its security force, whose greed concerns unobtanium, not genetics. The film's final transfer of Jake into his avatar is achieved through the Tree of Souls, presented as a spiritual and biological process rather than a genetic one.

The story

In 2154, with Earth's resources exhausted, the Resources Development Administration (RDA) mines the mineral unobtanium on Pandora, a moon inhabited by the Na'vi, tall blue-skinned humanoids who live in harmony with nature. Scientists of the Avatar Program explore Pandora in Na'vi-human hybrid bodies controlled remotely by matched human operators. Jake Sully, a paraplegic former Marine, takes the place of his dead identical twin as an operator. In his avatar he is taken in by the Omaticaya clan and falls in love with Neytiri, while the security chief, Colonel Quaritch, promises to restore his legs in exchange for intelligence. When the RDA destroys the clan's Hometree, Jake, the program head Dr. Grace Augustine and their allies side with the Na'vi. Grace is killed, but the united clans, helped by Pandora's wildlife, defeat the RDA, which is expelled. Jake is permanently transferred into his avatar body.
