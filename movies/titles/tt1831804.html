The genetics

Genetics matters here only as the origin of the apocalypse. The plague is a lethal influenza strain held at a secret military biological research facility and released by accident, a mishap that kills about 99 percent of humanity; the sources do not say whether the strain was engineered or what the research was for, and they give no individual scientists a role beyond the opening episode. After the outbreak the series turns away from science altogether, towards a supernatural battle between good and evil embodied by Mother Abagail and Randall Flagg. As in the novel, military bioweapons research is the unexamined sin that ends the old world, presented through its consequences rather than through the people responsible.

The story

In this miniseries adaptation of Stephen King's novel, a mishap at a top-secret military biological research facility releases a highly lethal strain of influenza, nicknamed Captain Trips, that kills nearly the whole population of the world. The survivors are drawn by dreams to two opposing figures: Mother Abagail, a 108-year-old woman who gathers the good in Boulder, Colorado, and the demonic Randall Flagg, the Dark Man, who builds a violent community in Las Vegas. Among the survivors are Stu Redman, Frannie Goldsmith, the deaf Nick Andros and the musician Larry Underwood, who join Boulder, while the resentful Harold Lauder and Nadine Cross go over to Flagg. As Flagg moves to destroy Mother Abagail's community, the story becomes an elemental struggle between good and evil, resolved in a final confrontation and a new closing episode written by Stephen King with his son Owen King.
