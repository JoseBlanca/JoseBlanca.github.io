The genetics

The film is a biography of Gregor Mendel, the Augustinian friar whose experiments with pea plants established the basic laws of heredity and who is regarded as the founder of modern genetics. It portrays the scientist as a man of faith for whom research and religion are compatible, and it has him foresee that knowledge of heredity could be misused for eugenics. According to Wikipedia's article on Mendel, his work, published in 1866, attracted little attention in his lifetime and was only recognised after 1900, when Hugo de Vries, Carl Correns and Erich von Tschermak independently confirmed his findings. The film's ending, with Mendel's work welcomed in Rome, contrasts with this history of neglect. The film offers a rare screen portrait of a real geneticist, told from a Catholic perspective.

The story

This Italian drama, written and directed by Liana Marabini, follows the life and work of Gregor Mendel, played by Christopher Lambert, presenting him as a monk devoted to both prayer and science. Set in the Austro-Hungarian Empire of the nineteenth century, the film shows Mendel's struggle against the taxes imposed on monasteries and his efforts to convince others of the importance of his discovery about heredity. Mendel wonders whether faith and science can be reconciled, and whether his findings will benefit humanity or prove dangerous, since they could be used for eugenic purposes. His benefactor and friend, Princess Hanna von Limburg, supports him. Finally he publishes his conclusions and travels to the Vatican to meet the Pope, who welcomes his work.
