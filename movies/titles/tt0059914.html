The genetics

Genetics appears as the declared goal of the villain rather than as an explained technique. Dr. Nurmi runs secret bio-engineering experiments and a eugenics programme whose aim is a "genetically perfect, immortal race of humans", and his plan to fuse half of his body with half of Connie Gomez's body is presented as the key step. The film never explains how shrinking people or grafting bodies would produce such a race, and reviewers single out these plans as absurd and the plot as confused. Nurmi is a classic mad scientist: a traitor working for another planet, a kidnapper and a seducer, opposed by the heroic Commander Halstead. The eugenic dream of a perfected, immortal humanity is thus tied to abduction and human experimentation. This campy Italian space opera, the first of the Gamma One films, belongs to the 1960s tradition in which genetic and eugenic ambitions mark the villain.

The story

In a future where the United Democracies run space travel, Commander Mike Halstead of the space station Gamma One investigates a wave of disappearances of world leaders and other people on Earth. The culprit is Dr. Nurmi, a scientist on the same station who is officially studying the miniaturization of human organs but secretly works for the planet Delphos. Using bald, four-armed androids and beautiful women of superhuman strength, he abducts his victims, shrinks them to doll size and ships them in suitcases to Delphos. When Halstead confronts him, his superiors confine him to quarters, but two fellow officers free him. Meanwhile Nurmi seduces Halstead's girlfriend, Lieutenant Connie Gomez, whom he wants for an experiment in which he would graft half of his own body to half of hers. Halstead infiltrates Nurmi's operation and rescues Connie in space.
