The genetics

The outbreak comes from DC2, a biochemical weapon developed by the engineer Abby and coveted by a rogue army unit: released as a gas, it makes people mutate into zombie-like creatures, and infected soldiers inhale it to hold off their own transformation. By this database's criteria it counts as a weaponised, man-made agent, although the film never describes it in genetic terms beyond 'mutation'. Abby, its creator, ends up helping the survivors and points to immune people as a source of a cure, while the military is shown as demented and predatory. Robert Rodriguez's half of the Grindhouse double feature is an over-the-top homage to 1970s exploitation films, and its science is deliberately absurd.

The story

In rural Texas, a rogue military unit under Lt. Muldoon tries to buy large quantities of DC2, a deadly biochemical agent, from the chemical engineer Abby; when Muldoon tries to take him hostage, Abby releases the gas, turning most of the townspeople into deformed, zombie-like creatures. Go-go dancer Cherry Darling loses a leg in an attack and is rescued by her ex-boyfriend El Wray, who fits her with a table leg. At the hospital, Dr. William Block cripples the hands of his wife Dakota, an anaesthetist, out of jealousy, before he is infected. The survivors gather at the Bone Shack restaurant and are captured by Muldoon's men; Abby reveals that the soldiers, already infected, need the gas to delay their own mutation, and that some people are immune, hinting at a cure. The group fights back, Cherry gets a machine-gun leg, many die, and the survivors found a peaceful community on a Mexican beach as the outbreak spreads worldwide.
