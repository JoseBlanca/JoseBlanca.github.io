The genetics

The series deals with deadly pathogens and the scientists who handle them. In the first season, Army laboratory scientists identify and contain Ebola in imported monkeys; they are presented as heroic professionals who take great personal risks to protect the public. The second season turns to bioterrorism: the 2001 anthrax letters and the hunt for their source, which leads to a microbiologist working with anthrax, Bruce Ivins. The available sources describe virology and microbiology rather than genetics as such, with no engineering or sequencing detailed, but the series sets the protective work of laboratory scientists against the danger of pathogens turned into weapons. Both seasons dramatise real events, and critics saw the series as a sobering reminder of how deadly a disease can be.

The story

This American medical drama, broadcast by National Geographic, dramatises real events. The first season, based on Richard Preston's 1994 non-fiction book, is set mainly in 1989 and follows US Army veterinary pathologist Nancy Jaax, who identifies Ebola virus in monkeys at a primate quarantine facility in Reston, Virginia, near Washington. Working with her husband, Army veterinary surgeon Jerry Jaax, virologist Peter Jahrling and a secret military team, she risks her life to keep a highly lethal virus with no known cure from spreading to humans. The second season, The Hot Zone: Anthrax (2021), follows the investigation of the anthrax letters sent a week after the 11 September 2001 attacks, through FBI agent Matthew Ryker, a microbiology specialist who fears further attacks, and microbiologist Bruce Ivins, whose help to the investigation makes him a suspect.
