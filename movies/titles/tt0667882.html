The genetics

Genetics is central. A geneticist experimenting on himself discovers that the so-called "junk" parts of human DNA contain a hidden programme, apparently left by aliens, which activates when triggered and summons a selected group of humans. His selection of students by IQ, physical perfection and body measurements has a clearly eugenic flavour, although the episode presents it as the aliens' design and treats the departure as a hopeful step in human evolution. Nodel is portrayed as a driven but sympathetic researcher, opposed by a secretive military. The story plays on a real scientific debate of the 1990s about the function of introns and other non-coding DNA, much of which was then dismissed as junk. Introns are removed from RNA by splicing and are not "junk" in a simple sense, but there is no evidence that they store dormant instructions, and the episode's ideas are pure science fiction. The story continues in the season 4 episode "The Origin of Species".

The story

Dr. Martin Nodel, a university geneticist, studies introns, stretches of DNA that do not code for proteins and that he believes hold the secret of future evolution. He develops a formula to activate them and tests it on himself. He starts to develop strange marks: a map grows on his back and a pattern of symbols appears on his hand. He then screens students for an "advanced seminar", selecting those with a high IQ, no surgery, no imperfections such as tattoos or glasses, and specific age, height and weight. The map leads the group, joined by his son and the son's girlfriend, to a cave in a secret military installation, where soldiers capture them. Behind a sealed door is an alien spacecraft bearing Nodel's symbols. When he touches it, an alien message is transmitted through him, and Nodel, the students and the young couple board the ship, which takes off.
