The genetics

There is no genetic science in this satirical comedy, but the story is built on a classic nature versus nurture situation: two babies of different social classes swapped at birth. The film comes down on the side of upbringing. Momo, born to the wealthy Le Quesnoys but raised by the poor Groseilles, remains as resourceful and light-fingered as the family that raised him, while Bernadette, a Groseille by birth, has the tastes and prejudices of her bourgeois upbringing and is horrified by her biological family. The swap is used to mock class pretensions and bourgeois hypocrisy rather than to say anything about heredity, and blood ties count for less than the habits each child has learned.

The story

On Christmas Eve, a nurse furious at her lover, the obstetrician Dr. Mavial, swaps two newborns in a hospital in northern France: a girl born to the Groseilles, a poor family living on benefits and petty crime, and a boy born to the Le Quesnoys, a wealthy Catholic family. Twelve years later, rejected by the doctor after his wife's death, the nurse reveals the swap to both families. The Le Quesnoys keep their daughter Bernadette without telling her, and take in their biological son Maurice, known as Momo, paying the Groseilles for him. Momo appears to fit in but secretly sells the family's silver to help the Groseilles and introduces his new brothers and sisters to his old family's way of life. Bernadette learns the truth and is horrified by her biological family. The Le Quesnoys' order falls apart, while the nurse ends up caring for the doctor, now paralysed by a stroke, and has him to herself at last.
