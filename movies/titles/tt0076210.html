The genetics

This adaptation of H. G. Wells's 1896 novel updates Moreau's methods. In the novel he shapes animals into people by vivisection; here he injects a serum containing human genetic material, so the creation of human-animal hybrids becomes a matter of transferring heredity rather than surgery. The film also reverses the process when Moreau injects Braddock to make him regress towards an animal. Moreau is the classic mad scientist: working alone and beyond any oversight, he seeks to "perfect life", treats his creatures with cruelty, regards compassion as a weakness and kills his own associate. His experiments end in revolt and destruction. The idea that a serum could reshape an adult body into another species is pure fantasy, but the story anticipates later concerns about human-animal hybrids and about scientists who place their curiosity above the suffering of their subjects.

The story

In 1911, after the wreck of the ship Lady Vain, the engineer Braddock is the only survivor to reach a Pacific island, where he is nursed back to health in the compound of the scientist Dr. Moreau. Also living there are Moreau's mercenary associate Montgomery, his misshapen mute servant M'Ling, and a young woman, Maria. Braddock discovers that the island is populated by "Humanimals", creatures Moreau has made from wild animals by injecting them with a serum containing human genetic material. The creatures live by laws Moreau has given them and fear his laboratory, the "House of Pain". When Braddock tries to leave with Maria, Moreau injects him with a serum to turn him into an animal and record the experience. After Moreau kills Montgomery, the creatures revolt; Moreau dies and the compound burns. Braddock, slowly returning to his human state, escapes by boat with Maria.
