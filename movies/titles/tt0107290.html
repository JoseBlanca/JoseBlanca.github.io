The genetics

Genetic engineering is the premise of the film. Hammond's scientists, led by chief geneticist Henry Wu, recovered dinosaur DNA from the blood in mosquitoes preserved in amber, filled the gaps in the genomes with frog DNA and made all the animals female to prevent breeding. The plan fails because of that frog DNA: like some amphibians, the dinosaurs change sex in a single-sex population, so life finds a way. Through Malcolm the film argues that genetic power was obtained without discipline or humility, and the geneticists serve a commercial project, although Hammond himself is a sympathetic showman rather than a villain. Real ancient DNA cannot do this. DNA breaks down over time, and even under the best conditions the upper limit for sequencing is thought to be roughly one to two million years, while the last non-avian dinosaurs died about 66 million years ago.

The story

Industrialist John Hammond has built a theme park of cloned dinosaurs on Isla Nublar, off Costa Rica. After a worker is killed, his investors demand a safety review, and Hammond invites palaeontologist Alan Grant, palaeobotanist Ellie Sattler and mathematician Ian Malcolm, along with the investors' lawyer and his own grandchildren, Lex and Tim. The visitors learn how the animals were made and debate the ethics of the park. During a tropical storm, the computer programmer Dennis Nedry shuts down the security system to steal frozen embryos for a rival company, and the electric fences fail. A Tyrannosaurus escapes and attacks the tour, Nedry is killed, and Grant and the children must cross the park on foot, discovering on the way that the dinosaurs are breeding. Attempts to restart the power release the Velociraptors, which kill several staff. The survivors are rescued by helicopter after Grant and Hammond agree not to endorse the park.
