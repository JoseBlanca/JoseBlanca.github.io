The genetics

Genetic engineering is the origin of the vampire in this Marvel comics adaptation. Michael Morbius is a sympathetic scientist driven by his own illness and his brother's; his attempt to cure them by splicing vampire bat genes into his own creates a monster. The story contrasts two responses to the same treatment: Michael tries to control his bloodlust and regrets the experiment, while Milo embraces his power, so the film places the danger less in the science than in how it is used. It also shows the scientist bending rules, as he works with illegally captured animals on a ship outside any jurisdiction. The biology is fantasy. Adding genes from another species cannot give an adult human bat-like abilities such as echolocation, flight or a need for blood,.

The story

Michael Morbius, a brilliant doctor who has suffered all his life from a rare blood disease, declines a Nobel Prize for his work on artificial blood. In search of a cure for himself and his surrogate brother Milo, who has the same illness, he captures vampire bats from Costa Rica and attempts to splice their genes with his own. Testing the treatment on himself aboard a ship in international waters, he is cured but turned into a vampire-like creature with superhuman strength, speed and echolocation, and an irresistible thirst for blood that leads him to kill the crew. Back in New York he tries to survive on artificial blood, but Milo, furious that Michael refuses to share the cure, takes it himself and begins killing people. Michael develops an antibody against the transformation and, after Milo kills their adoptive father and Michael's colleague Martine, uses it to kill him. Martine is later revealed to have come back to life.
