The genetics

The film is about the experience of a genetic disease rather than about genetics as a science. Cystic fibrosis is inherited in an autosomal recessive manner: it is caused by mutations in both copies of the CFTR gene, so that two healthy carrier parents can have an affected child. The disorder makes mucus thick, damaging the lungs above all. No scientists or geneticists play a significant role; the focus is on the child, her parents and the medical routine. Frank Deford's book and the film were credited with raising public awareness of the disease and of the Cystic Fibrosis Foundation, which he served as a trustee. Treatment has changed greatly since then: CFTR modulator drugs that target the defective protein now substantially improve survival for many patients, though there is still no cure.

The story

This ABC television film, based on sportswriter Frank Deford's 1983 memoir, follows the short life of his daughter Alexandra, who has cystic fibrosis and dies in 1980 at the age of eight. Craig T. Nelson plays Frank, Bonnie Bedelia his wife Carol and Gennie James Alex. The family's routine is shaped by the disease: daily medication, painful chest percussion to clear mucus from her lungs and repeated hospital stays. Alex is portrayed as funny, precocious and protective of her family, worried that her older brother Christian is overshadowed by her illness, and she asks increasingly direct questions about death. Lung haemorrhages and collapsed lungs mark her decline, and the film shows the harsh physical reality of the illness, such as a romp with her father that leaves her coughing blood.
