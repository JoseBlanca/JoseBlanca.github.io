The genetics

Genetics appears only in passing. The prison's inmates are described as men with a 'genetic predisposition for antisocial behaviour'; in the longer Assembly Cut they are said to carry a 'double-Y' chromosome pattern, an allusion to the once-popular and since discredited idea that men with an extra Y chromosome are prone to violence. The label explains why such men have been sent to a remote penal colony but plays no part in the plot, in which the inmates become Ripley's allies. The story's central biological threat is the alien, which the Weyland-Yutani Corporation wants for its bioweapons division, but no genetic work is done in the film. Studies in the 1960s found more XYY men in prisons than expected, but later research showed no strong link between the karyotype and violent crime.

The story

An alien egg on board the spaceship Sulaco releases a facehugger, and a fire forces the ship to jettison the escape pod carrying Ellen Ripley, the girl Newt, Corporal Hicks and the android Bishop. The pod crashes on Fiorina 161, a foundry planet used as a maximum-security prison for violent male inmates. Ripley is the only survivor. An alien bursts from the prison dog and begins killing inmates, while the prison has no weapons. Ripley discovers that she carries an alien queen embryo, and that the Weyland-Yutani Corporation is sending a team to capture it for its biological weapons programme. With the inmates led by their spiritual leader Dillon, she lures the adult alien into the foundry and destroys it. When the company team arrives, led by a man identical to Bishop who claims to be his creator, Ripley throws herself into the furnace as the queen bursts from her chest.
