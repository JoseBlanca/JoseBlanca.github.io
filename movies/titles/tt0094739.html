The genetics

The film is described as a 'nature versus nurture farce'. Its premise is the classic comparison of twins separated at birth: two pairs of identical twins are split, and one of each pair grows up rich in New York while the other grows up poor in the country. The comedy leans towards nature. The twins resemble each other in temperament and wishes, not only in looks: Sadie Ratliff, raised in the country, dreams of city glamour like her sister, and Rose Shelton, raised in luxury, longs for the rural life her sister leads. Upbringing shapes manners and accents, but not desires. No genetics is discussed or tested; the twins discover the mix-up by meeting face to face. Real studies of twins reared apart do find striking similarities in personality and interests, though far less complete than the comedy suggests.

The story

In 1948 a wealthy businessman's wife and a poor local woman both give birth to identical twin girls in a small West Virginia hospital, and an elderly nurse mixes up the babies, so that each family raises one of its own twins and one of the other couple's. Both pairs are named Rose and Sadie. Forty years later, the Shelton sisters run a New York conglomerate: Sadie is a ruthless executive, while Rose longs for a simpler life in the country. In rural Jupiter Hollow, Rose Ratliff is the forewoman of the furniture factory the Sheltons' company owns, and Sadie Ratliff dreams of big-city glamour. When Sadie Shelton plans to sell the factory to a buyer who will strip-mine the valley, the Ratliffs travel to New York to stop the sale. At the Plaza Hotel the two sets of twins are repeatedly mistaken for each other before they meet and block the deal together.
