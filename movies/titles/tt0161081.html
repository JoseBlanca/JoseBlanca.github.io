The genetics

Genetics is only the professional background of the villain in this ghost story. Norman Spencer is described by reviewers as a brilliant geneticist who has taken a prestigious chair at the university where his late father taught, and he does laboratory research; one critic calls him a "brilliant (i.e. unstable) geneticist". His science plays no part in the plot apart from his access to laboratory chemicals: he paralyses Claire with an anaesthetic. The film draws on an old association between scientific brilliance and moral coldness. Norman is ambitious and outwardly charming, has an affair with a student and kills her to protect his career, and then tries to kill his wife. Nothing in the story concerns heredity, DNA or genetic research, so the title belongs in the database mainly as an example of how a scientist, and specifically a geneticist, is portrayed on screen.

The story

Claire Spencer, a former cellist, lives in a lakeside house in Vermont with her husband Norman, a research scientist at the local university. After her daughter leaves for college, Claire begins to experience strange events: doors open by themselves, the bathtub fills with water and she sees a woman's face reflected in it. She first suspects that her neighbour has murdered his wife, but the wife turns up alive. Claire then finds that the ghost is Madison Elizabeth Frank, a young woman who disappeared a year earlier, and she recovers the repressed memory that Norman had an affair with Madison, one of his students. Norman first claims that Madison killed herself, then confesses that he murdered her when she threatened to expose the affair, and tries to kill Claire by paralysing her with a drug and drowning her in the bath. Claire escapes, and in the lake Madison's body seems to drag Norman to his death.
