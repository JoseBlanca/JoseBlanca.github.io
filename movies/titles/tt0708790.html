The genetics

Two genetic elements frame the episode's mystery. The first is Ian himself: Dr. Pulaski finds that the fetus is a Human-Betazoid hybrid that contains no genetic material other than Troi's, a puzzle the episode does not explain, since the father is an energy being who wanted to experience human life. The second is the cargo: samples of a deadly plague carried to a research station for work on a cure, a significant number of which, Pulaski notes, are genetically engineered, an excessive amount of engineering that she warns Picard about. The episode's tension comes from the combination: radiation emitted by the alien child makes one strain grow in its containment, and Ian chooses to leave and die to protect the crew. Starfleet's handling of the pathogens is presented as routine, careful and aimed at a cure. Critics disliked the episode, especially its treatment of Troi's non-consensual pregnancy. A child with genes from one parent alone would be a clone-like parthenogenote, not a hybrid.

The story

As the Enterprise takes on its new chief medical officer, Dr. Katherine Pulaski, it prepares to carry samples of a deadly plasma plague to a research station working on a cure. An energy being enters the ship and impregnates Counselor Deanna Troi as she sleeps. The fetus, a male Human-Betazoid hybrid with no genetic material but Troi's, develops in 36 hours. Worf argues that it is a security threat and should be aborted, Data that it is an opportunity to study a new life form, and Troi decides to keep it. She gives birth to a boy, Ian Andrew, who grows to the size of an eight-year-old in a day. Meanwhile one of the plague strains in the containment module starts to grow, and Pulaski traces it to Eichner radiation. Ian realizes that he is the source, and dies in Troi's arms, returning to energy form; he had wanted to learn what it was like to be human. Wesley Crusher stays aboard.
