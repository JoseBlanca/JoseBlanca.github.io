The genetics

Genetic engineering is the instrument of the plot. A secret military programme splices a prisoner's genes with alien DNA and turns him, step by step, into an alien, a fantasy version of transgenesis in which foreign DNA rewrites an adult's entire body and mind. The scientists and officials work for the government on a defence project and treat their subject as expendable: he is coerced into consenting, then hunted for sport and data. One scientist, Dr. Kersaw, recognises that he is still a person and opposes the hunt, and she takes over when her superior is discredited. The episode's twist turns the experiment into a moral test of the experimenters, who judge Stiles by his appearance rather than his conduct. In reality, genes from an unrelated organism could not transform a grown human in this way, but the story is a parable about consent and dehumanisation in research.

The story

Sergeant Linden Stiles, wrongly convicted of murdering eleven people, is offered a choice by a secret government unit led by Maculhaney: execution or taking part in an experiment. His Christian faith forbids him to choose death, so he agrees. Scientists splice his genes with extraterrestrial DNA recovered from a downed alien craft, hoping to learn about the aliens and how to fight them. Stiles gradually turns into a nonhuman creature with heightened senses and intelligence. He escapes, but his flight has been allowed so that a military unit can hunt and kill him, even though he refuses to fight back. Dr. Ellen Kersaw, who is responsible for him, objects in vain. As the hunters close in, aliens resembling the transformed Stiles appear, stop them and take him away with them. The humans realise that they themselves were being tested, and failed.
