The genetics

The film's title points to its subject: albinism is a genetic condition, usually inherited in an autosomal recessive way, that reduces or prevents the production of melanin pigment in the skin, hair and eyes. The documentary presents it as a "simple genetic anomaly" that nonetheless shapes its subjects' lives, through both medical effects and social prejudice. Agnes's loss of sight and skin cancer reflect real consequences of albinism, which is associated with vision problems and, especially near the equator, greatly increases the risk of skin cancer without sun protection. The film's focus is human and social rather than scientific: it gives a voice to people living with an inherited condition and shows the stigma they face in Kenya. People with albinism in parts of East Africa have also been subjected to violent attacks linked to witchcraft beliefs. No scientists or geneticists feature in the available descriptions of the film.

The story

In My Genes is a documentary written, directed, produced and edited by Lupita Nyong'o, her debut as a director. It follows the lives of eight Kenyans with albinism and asks what it means to be pale-skinned in a largely Black society, where people with albinism are highly visible yet often ignored. At its centre is Agnes, a woman with albinism who has faced prejudice since birth; she has seven children, and her 17-year-old daughter is expecting a baby. During the film Agnes learns why she lost the sight of both eyes and that she has skin cancer, and she carries on, relying on her faith and her own work. The other participants describe the discrimination they meet in daily life because of their condition.
