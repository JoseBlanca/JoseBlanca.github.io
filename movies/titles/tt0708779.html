The genetics

No genetic science appears; the episode is a nature versus nurture story about adoption across species. Producer Michael Piller described it as the story of 'someone who was Human who had been raised in a totally alien environment. Is he Human any longer?'. Jono is human by birth, and memories of his parents' death surface when Picard shows him their pictures, but his values, loyalties and habits are Talarian. The episode favours nurture: Picard's attempt to restore Jono's human identity fails, and the boy returns to the father who raised him. Some critics found the crew ethnocentric for assuming that Jono should want to be human, and some viewers objected to sending a possibly abused child back. The premise fits what is known about human development: culture, language and attachments are learned, not inherited.

The story

The Enterprise answers a distress call from a damaged Talarian ship and rescues four Talarian youths and a human teenager, Jono. Dr. Crusher finds old fractures that suggest abuse, and records show that Jono is Jeremiah Rossa, grandson of a Starfleet admiral, whose parents were killed in a Talarian attack on a Federation colony. He was raised by the Talarian captain Endar, who adopted him to replace a son lost in battle, and he responds only to male authority. Picard reluctantly takes Jono into his quarters to help him rediscover his human side, while Endar arrives and threatens war unless his son is returned. Torn between the two, Jono stabs the sleeping Picard, expecting to be executed. Picard realises that the boy's home is with Endar and lets him go back.
