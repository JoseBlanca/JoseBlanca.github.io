The genetics

The story revolves around a laboratory-made virus that a terrorist group wants to use as a biological weapon. Dr. Kimihiko Nikaido created the virus and an antidote; according to one source he did not know he was working for people who meant to sell the weapon, and he destroys the antidote and infects himself rather than hand it over, before he is killed. His assistant, Dr. Kimiko Kujo, heads the terrorist group. Against her, Nikaido's research partner Dr. Kouichi Matsudo recreates the antidote with L's help, using the blood of the immune boy and a clue left by Nikaido. The film thus sets responsible scientists against a scientist who turns the work into a weapon. Its science is loose: the virus is said to need sugar to infect rapidly, and the immunity is not explained.

The story

In this spin-off of Death Note, set in the last days of the detective L's life, a deadly virus wipes out a village in Thailand, and one of L's agents rescues the only survivor, a boy with a gift for calculation. The virus, far more infectious than Ebola, was created by Dr. Kimihiko Nikaido, whose assistant Dr. Kimiko Kujo turns out to lead the terrorist group behind it. When Nikaido refuses to hand over his antidote, destroys it and injects himself with the virus, Kujo kills him. His young daughter Maki, who has also injected herself with the virus, flees to L, and Kujo's group pursues them. L takes the children to Nikaido's research partner, Dr. Kouichi Matsudo, who finds that Maki is protected for now by her low blood sugar and, with a clue from the boy and the boy's blood, recreates the antidote. L stops the terrorists' plane to the United States and cures the passengers. He leaves the boy at the orphanage where he grew up, naming him Near, before he dies.
