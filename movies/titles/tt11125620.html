The genetics

Genetics drives both halves of the plot. Wednesday, a child prodigy of mad science, transfers octopus DNA into Uncle Fester to show how humans could be improved, and he gradually mutates into a tentacled creature before she restores him with the family's blood. Her rival is Cyrus Strange, a deranged scientist who makes human-animal hybrids, including his pig daughter Ophelia, and who wants Wednesday's superior formula; he ends up transformed by his own formula into a monster made of several animals. The other genetic thread is a paternity question: Cyrus fakes a DNA test to persuade Wednesday that he is her father, and the film, as some critics noted, raises the question of whether biology or love makes a family, answering firmly in favour of the Addamses. The science is cartoon fantasy: transferring an animal's DNA cannot turn a person into that animal.

The story

In this animated sequel, Wednesday Addams presents a science-fair experiment in which she gives Uncle Fester the DNA of her pet octopus, and Fester slowly begins to change. Her work catches the eye of the scientist Cyrus Strange. Worried that his children are drifting away, Gomez takes the family on a road trip across the United States, pursued by a lawyer, Mr. Mustela, who claims that Wednesday was switched at birth and is not an Addams. Cyrus tells Wednesday that he is her real father, and a DNA test she runs with Gomez's hair seems to confirm it, so she leaves to join Cyrus in California. There he shows her his own formula for human-animal hybrids and reveals that his daughter Ophelia is a pig; he wants Wednesday's better formula and captures the Addamses to experiment on them. In the struggle Cyrus becomes a hybrid monster and admits he faked the DNA results. Fester, now an octopus creature, defeats him, and Wednesday restores Fester using the family's blood.
