The genetics

Human cloning is one of the telenovela's main storylines, alongside a cross-cultural romance between Brazil and the Muslim world. The geneticist Augusto Albieri is a sympathetic but tragic figure: grief at the death of his godson, combined with the ambition to be the first to clone a human, leads him to create a clone in secret and to implant the embryo in a woman without her knowledge, a serious violation of consent. The story then explores the consequences for the clone himself, who shares Lucas's genome but not his life, and for the families who claim him, including a legal fight over parenthood. Writer Glória Perez said she was inspired by Dolly the sheep and wanted to contrast a Muslim world submissive to God with a West that installed geneticists as new creators of life. Human reproductive cloning remains unachieved and widely opposed, but the telenovela's central point holds: a clone would be a much younger genetic twin with a life of his own, as Léo is.

The story

In the early 1980s, Jade, a young Muslim woman raised in Rio de Janeiro, moves to Fez, Morocco, to live with her strict uncle Ali after her mother's death. There she falls in love with Lucas Ferraz, a wealthy Brazilian visiting with his identical twin Diogo, their father Leônidas and the twins' godfather, the geneticist Augusto Albieri. Their romance defies her family and religion. When Diogo dies in a helicopter crash, Lucas abandons his plan to run away with Jade, who marries Said. The grieving Albieri secretly uses Lucas's cells to create the first human clone and implants the embryo in Deusa, a patient who believes she is receiving ordinary artificial insemination. Twenty years later, Lucas and Jade, both unhappily married, meet again, while the clone, Léo, has grown into the image of the young Lucas and falls for Jade. When the cloning is discovered, Leônidas and Deusa fight in court over Léo, who struggles to find his place in the world.
