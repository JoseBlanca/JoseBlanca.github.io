The genetics

Gene therapy opens the episode and shapes its subplot. In the Stargate universe, the Ancients' technology responds only to people who carry a particular gene, the ATA gene, and the Atlantis expedition's physician, Dr. Carson Beckett, has developed a gene therapy to give it to those who lack it. McKay is a willing volunteer, and the therapy works, but with an unforeseen side effect: he cannot turn off the personal shield he activates. Beckett wonders whether the 'artificial gene' is to blame, while the team suspects McKay's own fear is keeping it on. The treatment is presented as a legitimate, useful medical innovation by a well-meaning doctor, its mishap is mostly played for comedy, and the shield finally lets McKay save the city. Real gene therapy is used to correct inherited diseases rather than to add new abilities, and no gene lets anyone control machines by thought.

The story

In Atlantis, Dr. Rodney McKay volunteers for Dr. Carson Beckett's gene therapy, which gives him the ATA gene that people need to operate the technology of the Ancients. It works: he activates a personal shield that protects him from a fall, a gunshot and a blow, but he cannot switch it off and can no longer eat or drink. Meanwhile, the Athosian boy Jinto, playing hide-and-seek, accidentally opens a containment device in which the Ancients had trapped an energy-eating entity. The 'shadow' drains the city's generators, burns Lieutenant Ford and grows more dangerous. Attempts to recapture it fail, and Teyla suggests letting it leave through the Stargate. When the creature starts feeding on the gate, McKay, whose shield has dropped, reactivates it, walks into the entity and throws a generator through the gate; the creature follows, and McKay survives.
