The genetics

The monsters are piranhas bred in a Cold War army programme as a biological weapon: according to the sources, they were made highly aggressive, able to live in fresh and salt water and to reproduce quickly, and a reviewer describes them as genetically bred to survive in fresh water. When the programme lost its funding, the scientist Dr. Leticia Baines carried on the work on her own at the abandoned site, and she tries to stop the heroes from draining the pool before revealing what she created. The genetics stays in the background as the premise; the story is a creature feature in which a military experiment escapes and kills children and holidaymakers, made worse by a resort owner who ignores warnings. The scientist and the army are the source of the disaster, and the ending suggests that the fish have reached the sea. Making a fish adapt to new waters and breed faster is a matter of selection over generations rather than of a single laboratory programme, and the film does not explain the science.

The story

This remake of the 1978 film opens at an abandoned US Army test site, where two teenagers who sneak in for a night swim are killed in the pool. The girl's uncle, the developer J.R. Randolph, hires private investigator Maggie McNamara to find them. With local resident Paul Grogan she drains the pool, despite an attack by Dr. Leticia Baines, a scientist who has kept working there, and Baines reveals that the pool held a school of piranhas that has now escaped into the river. Randolph refuses to delay the opening of his resort and has Maggie and Paul arrested. They escape, but the fish reach a children's camp, where Paul's daughter Susie survives and another girl dies, and then the resort, killing most of the swimmers. Paul releases toxic waste into the water from an old refinery and kills the fish; Randolph commits suicide. In the final scene, piranhas are heard in the ocean.
