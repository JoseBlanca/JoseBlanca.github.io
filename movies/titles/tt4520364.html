Morgan

2016 Film

IMDb FilmAffinity

The genetics

Genetic engineering creates the title character, a "hybrid biological organism" built with synthetic DNA and nanotechnology to be smarter, faster and stronger than humans. The project belongs to a corporation that wants a controllable weapon, and its scientists, isolated at the site, are torn between seeing Morgan as a product and as their child. Their attempt to make her more human, with real emotions, makes her harder to control, and the experiment ends in a massacre. The final twist, that the company's cold consultant is herself an earlier engineered being, suggests that the corporation values obedience over humanity. Like many films about creations that turn on their creators, it raises questions about responsibility for engineered life rather than exploring the science in detail. Real synthetic biology is far more modest: in 2010 the J. Craig Venter Institute created a bacterial cell controlled by a chemically synthesised genome, and in 2016 a minimal cell with only 473 genes.

The story

Lee Weathers, a risk-management consultant for the genetic engineering company SynSect, is sent to a remote research site to assess the L-9 project: Morgan, an artificial being made with synthetic DNA infused with nanotechnology. Only five years old, Morgan looks like a teenage girl and is more intelligent than her creators, but she has just stabbed one of the scientists in the eye. The team who raised her treat her as their child. When a psychologist provokes her during an evaluation, Morgan kills him, and Weathers decides she must be terminated. The scientists sedate Weathers and plan to escape with Morgan, but Morgan, believing they have betrayed her, kills most of them, including Dr. Cheng, whom she calls "mother". A recorded confession reveals that the scientists had tried to make her more human than earlier prototypes so she could surpass her design as a combat weapon. Weathers hunts Morgan down and drowns her. Company executives then reveal that Weathers herself is an earlier, emotionally limited prototype, L-4.
