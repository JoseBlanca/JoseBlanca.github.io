The genetics

There is no laboratory genetics: the film is a comic satire of the nature versus nurture debate. Its premise is a home-made adoption and upbringing experiment in which each child is raised to contradict what its biological parents' traits are expected to predict: art for the scientists' own son, academic study for the daughter of poorly schooled parents, pacifism for the son of violent ones. The film gives weight to both sides and settles nothing. The experiment collapses, the children rebel, and as adults they follow their own paths (film director, animal-rights activist, lawyer) that neither confirm their heredity nor vindicate the plan; what the film mocks is the idea that either genes or upbringing can be engineered. The scientists are shown as well-meaning but obsessive, and their funder as a manipulator who was studying them. Real twin and adoption studies find that most human traits are shaped by both genes and environment, and rearing children as experimental subjects would be unethical.

The story

In 1977 two respected scientists, Ben and Catherine, leave their jobs to run a private experiment funded by a businessman, Gertz: they will raise three children against their genetic predispositions to prove that nurture beats nature. Their birth son Luke is steered towards art; Maya, adopted from parents with little schooling, is pushed to academic excellence; and Maurice, born to angry, aggressive parents, is raised as a pacifist who meditates daily. The children are homeschooled under the eye of Samsonov, a psychologist who reports to Gertz. Twelve years in, Gertz demands better results, the parents crack under the pressure, and the children try to escape by car, cause a highway pile-up and are taken into care. Gertz then reveals that he was experimenting on the parents all along and publishes a book about the project. In the end the family is reconciled.
