The genetics

The premise is genetic: whatever struck the world kills every mammal carrying a Y chromosome, so the series defines its victims by their karyotype. It takes care to separate chromosomes from gender: trans women and women with androgen insensitivity who carried a Y chromosome died, while trans men without one survived. The geneticist Dr. Allison Mann, an expert in human cloning, travels with Yorick to discover the origin of the plague and why he was spared, which implicitly raises the question of how the human species could continue. The available sources do not explain the cause, which the cancelled series never resolved, nor what Mann's research achieves. No known pathogen could selectively kill carriers of one chromosome across all mammals; the scenario is a thought experiment about a society without men.

The story

This television adaptation of the comic book series begins when a mysterious event simultaneously kills every mammal with a Y chromosome, except Yorick Brown, an unemployed amateur escape artist, and his male capuchin monkey, Ampersand. With most of the US government dead, Yorick's mother, Congresswoman Jennifer Brown, becomes President and struggles to hold the country together while Kimberly Campbell Cunningham, daughter of the late president, challenges her legitimacy. Infrastructure such as the power grid and supply chains collapses, and social divisions persist. Protected by Agent 355 of the secret Culper Ring, Yorick sets out with Dr. Allison Mann, a geneticist specialising in human cloning, to find out what caused the plague and why he survived. The series also follows trans and intersex characters, as the die-off was defined by chromosomes, not gender. It was cancelled after one season with the mystery unresolved.
