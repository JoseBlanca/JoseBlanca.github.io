The genetics

Genetic engineering is a secondary element. The film contrasts two kinds of manufactured soldier: Todd, an ordinary human shaped by brutal training from infancy, and the newer soldiers who have been genetically engineered for strength and aggression. The project's commander, Colonel Mekum, embodies the cold military-technical establishment, discarding the old soldiers as obsolete and treating the civilian colonists as acceptable targets. The story ultimately favours experience, loyalty and recovered humanity over engineered superiority, since Todd defeats the genetically designed troops. No geneticist is shown and the film says nothing about how the engineering is done; it borrows the idea mainly as a device for the action. Writer David Webb Peoples set the film in the same fictional universe as Blade Runner, which he co-wrote.

The story

In 1996, a military programme selects orphaned infants and raises them to be ruthless, disciplined soldiers, culling those who fall short. In 2036, Sergeant Todd 3465, one of the original recruits, is about to be replaced by a new generation of genetically engineered soldiers, created by Colonel Mekum to be physically superior and devoid of emotion except aggression. In a test, the engineered soldier Caine 607 defeats three of the veterans, and Todd, presumed dead, is dumped on the waste-disposal planet Arcadia 234. He is taken in by a community of stranded colonists, who struggle with his rigid military conditioning and eventually expel him. When Mekum's new soldiers land on the planet for a training exercise and attack the colonists, Todd uses his experience and knowledge of the terrain to defeat them all, including Caine, and then takes over the troop ship with his old comrades to rescue the settlers.
