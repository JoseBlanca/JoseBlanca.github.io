The genetics

The episode introduces the Man-Bat, a classic Batman story of self-experimentation. Kirk Langstrom and his father-in-law, the zoo's bat expert Dr. March, have developed a formula meant to create a new species, part human and part bat; Kirk tests it on himself, becomes addicted and turns into a creature that raids pharmaceutical companies for the chemicals he needs. The sources describe the formula as creating a new species but do not say whether the episode explains it in genetic or purely chemical terms, and Batman reverses it with a chemical antidote. Kirk is presented as a sympathetic scientist undone by his own ambition rather than a villain, and the closing warning that the Man-Bat may return leaves the experiment's danger hanging. Turning a human into a human-bat hybrid with a drink is pure comic-book science.

The story

A giant bat-like creature attacks a guard during a raid on Phoenix Pharmaceuticals, and Batman is blamed. Against Commissioner Gordon's advice, the mayor authorises Detective Bullock to form a squad to catch him. Batman finds that other pharmaceutical companies have been robbed in the same way, and narrowly escapes Bullock's squad with two clues: a recording of the attack and a sample of fur. As Bruce Wayne he takes them to Dr. Robert March, a bat expert at the Gotham Zoo, whose daughter Francine and her husband, Dr. Kirk Langstrom, offer to analyse them; March reports a mix of starlings and brown bats. Returning to the zoo at night, Batman finds Kirk, who confesses that he and March developed a formula to create a new species, "neither man nor bat", tested it on himself and became addicted. Kirk turns into the Man-Bat; Batman subdues him and brews an antidote, warning Francine that the Man-Bat may not be gone for good.
