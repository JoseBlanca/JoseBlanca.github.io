The genetics

Genetics drives the villain's scheme. Professor Larson and his scientists use a substance that penetrates human cells and alters chromosomes and genes, testing it on abducted village girls, with the aim of breeding a master race to rule the world. This combines genetic engineering with a eugenic breeding project and with human experimentation on unwilling subjects. Larson is a stock villain, opposed by legitimate scientific organizations who send commandos to stop him. The available sources say little about what the treatment actually does to the girls or how the story ends. The idea of a single substance that rewrites a person's genes is fantasy; it serves the familiar action-film figure of the eugenicist scientist bent on world domination. The film is better known for being shot in North Korea, which it portrays as a futuristic utopia.

The story

In the Far East, a group of scientists abducts young girls from local villages and holds them in a heavily guarded facility known as the Center, where they are used in experiments with a substance that penetrates human cells and changes their chromosomes and genes. The scientists are led by Professor Larson, a Lithuanian biologist from the University of Vilna who dreams of breeding a master race to rule the world. Scientific organizations in Asia and Europe learn of the illegal experiments, and the mercenary Lou Mamet is hired through FSR, an organization that takes on jobs too delicate for governments, to end the trafficking of women. Confusingly, it is Larson himself who commissions the mission, and he is later revealed to be the head of the operation the heroes are sent to destroy, a plot hole the director blamed on script changes made without his knowledge.
