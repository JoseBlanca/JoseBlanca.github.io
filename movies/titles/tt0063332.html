The genetics

Genetics is a passing element of this Soviet Cold War spy film. The weapon at its centre, RH gas, is a psychopharmacological agent rather than a genetic one, but its inventor, the former Nazi camp doctor Hass, claims that the docile slavery it induces will be inherited by the victims' descendants, part of his plan for a world divided between an intellectual elite and happy labourers. This brief hereditarian claim, together with his past experiments on camp prisoners, links the villain to Nazi medical crimes and eugenic fantasies of human castes; the film presents him as a sadist serving Western intelligence. No genetic science is shown, and the inheritance of a drug-induced state has no scientific basis.

The story

After a West German intelligence courier is murdered in Lisbon, Soviet intelligence finds an encrypted message about raw materials for mass production of 'RH gas', which in small doses stimulates the intellect and in large doses turns people into contented idiots fit only for labour. The case goes to Colonel Konstantin Ladeynikov, a Soviet agent working under cover in the West. To identify the gas's creator, Dr. Hass, a Nazi war criminal now working at a pharmaceutical centre in a Western resort town, the KGB recruits Ivan Savushkin, a children's theatre actor who in 1944 survived a concentration camp where Hass's experiments turned prisoners into 'meaningless animals'. Savushkin recognises Hass at a funeral but is captured; Hass expounds his vision of a 'New Eden' in which an intellectual elite rules happy, robot-like slaves whose condition will pass to their descendants. Ladeynikov frees Savushkin with incriminating documents, Hass is killed, and Ladeynikov is imprisoned in the West and later exchanged on a bridge.
