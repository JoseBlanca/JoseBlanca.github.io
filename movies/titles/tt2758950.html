The genetics

Genetic engineering and virology drive the series. The Ilaria Corporation's Arctic station conducts prohibited genetic research, and the virus at the centre of the first season is a designed agent: Hatake created it to activate dormant genes in his daughter Julia and give her a form of the immortality he possesses, while the corporation wants it as a means of global control. Scientists are on both sides: the CDC team tries to contain the outbreak, while the corporate scientists conceal and exploit it, and Hatake is an ambiguous figure who protects his daughter while serving his own aims. The show follows a common pattern in which a corporate laboratory releases a dangerous engineered pathogen. Real viruses can be used as vectors to deliver genes, but the idea of a virus switching on hidden genes for immortality, or making infected people violent, belongs to fiction.

The story

Helix is an American science fiction horror series broadcast on Syfy from 2014 to 2015. In the first season, a team from the US Centers for Disease Control led by Dr. Alan Farragut travels to an Arctic research station to investigate an outbreak. They discover that the Ilaria Corporation, which runs the station, is carrying out banned genetic engineering research and cares more about hiding it than about stopping the disease. There are two strains of the virus: Narvik-A is always fatal, while Narvik-B turns people into violent carriers who spread the infection. The outbreak was used to lure Dr. Julia Walker, a member of the CDC team, as an unwitting test subject. The station's director, Hiroshi Hatake, reveals that he is her father, a near-immortal over 500 years old, and that Ilaria is run by immortals who want the virus to control the world. The second season moves to an isolated island where a new outbreak occurs, with scenes set thirty years later.
