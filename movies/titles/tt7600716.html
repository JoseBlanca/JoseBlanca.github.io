The genetics

Heredity is the film's subject rather than genetic science. Grieving her Algerian grandfather, Neige comes to believe that her own and her family's problems come from their mixed origins, and she looks for her identity in her ancestry. She takes a DNA test, but it reveals nothing useful, and the answer she finally finds is not genetic but a sense of belonging, sealed by an Algerian passport and a stay in Algiers. The story nonetheless leans towards origins over upbringing as the key to who she is, and a critic in the French magazine Marianne argued that the film's preoccupation with heredity and the innate drifts into essentialism, moving from the right of blood to the right of genes. In reality, commercial ancestry tests estimate the regions where a person's ancestors lived; they say nothing about personal or family troubles.

The story

Neige, a divorced mother in France, is shaken by the death of her grandfather Emir, a former fighter for Algerian independence who settled in France and raised a family there, and who was the one stable emotional bond in her life. Estranged from her parents and distant from her siblings, she has also broken up with her partner François, who nonetheless supports her. Mourning, she begins to wonder whether her own and her family's troubles stem from their mixed ancestry and the clash of loyalties between French and Algerian society. Seeking meaning, she takes a DNA test, which tells her nothing useful, and starts the procedure to have her Algerian nationality recognized. She falls into depression and stops eating, and is taken to hospital, where she has a revelation. She discharges herself, travels to Algiers, obtains an Algerian passport, and ends sitting happily in a street among people she feels she belongs to.
