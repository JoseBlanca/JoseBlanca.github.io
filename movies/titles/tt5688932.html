The genetics

Genetic engineering is the film's central satirical device. WorryFree, already selling lifetime labour contracts, plans to transform its workers through a powder that modifies their DNA, turning them into human-horse hybrids ('Equisapiens') that are stronger, more obedient and more profitable. Its CEO, Steve Lift, is the villain: he has already transformed workers and keeps one shackled, secretly doses Cash and tries to buy him as a fake revolutionary leader. When Cash exposes the scheme, the public celebrates it as a scientific breakthrough and the company's shares soar, a pointed comment on how society rewards technological exploitation. The film is an absurdist black comedy about capitalism, race and labour, and the genetics is deliberately fantastical; the hybrids themselves are victims who end up rebelling against their creator.

The story

In a dystopian near-future Oakland, Cassius 'Cash' Green, who lives with his artist girlfriend Detroit in his uncle's garage, becomes a telemarketer and succeeds once an older colleague teaches him to use a 'white voice'. While co-workers organise a union, Cash is promoted to Power Caller and learns that the company sells arms and the cheap labour of WorryFree, a megacorporation whose workers sign lifetime contracts to live and work in its factories. Success estranges him from Detroit and his friends. At a party, WorryFree's CEO Steve Lift gives him a powder he believes is cocaine; Cash then discovers a shackled half-horse, half-human 'Equisapien', and Lift explains his plan to turn workers into stronger, more obedient Equisapiens with a DNA-modifying powder. Cash refuses $100 million to become one and exposes the scheme on television, but the public hails it as a scientific advance. After the union's strike, Cash begins to transform: the powder was the gene modifier. He leads the Equisapiens against Lift's house.
