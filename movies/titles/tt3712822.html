The genetics

The cloning in this children's comedy is magic dressed as science: a tanning bed, originally a scientist's cloning machine, makes instant copies of whoever lies in it, each shaped by the traits the person wishes to have, and the copies share the original's pain. A copy made from a copy comes out less intelligent. The genetic content is minimal; cloning serves as a device for a story about a boy who tries to become someone else and learns to accept himself. The inventor appears only in a video warning that the process is flawed and will kill the original unless the clones are reversed. Real clones are not adult copies with designer personalities: they are genetic twins born as babies.

The story

Thirteen-year-old Adam Baker can barely keep up with his summer jobs, including poop patrol at the water park, and has a crush on the lifeguard Lori, while the arrogant Vance mocks him. With his parents away and his uncle Mitch, a struggling magician, in charge, Adam falls into a tanning bed Mitch bought at auction and wakes up next to a copy of himself: the machine clones people, shaping each copy according to the traits the person inside it wants. The hard-working Adam #2 takes over his chores, and more clones follow, a party animal, a sensitive poet, a slow copy of a copy, and Perfect, modelled on Vance, who wins Lori's attention and then goes rogue. Adam learns from a video by the machine's inventor that unless the clones are sent back through it, he will die. After a magic show that saves the water park and a fight on a waterslide, the clones go back through the machine, and Adam wins Lori as himself.
