The genetics

The series uses biomedical science as the engine of its zombie story rather than exploring genetics in detail. The apocalypse is caused by a virus, and the first two seasons revolve around experimental vaccines and the antibodies in Murphy's blood, which the scientists hope to use to produce a cure. Murphy himself is the product of a trial of experimental vaccines on prison inmates, and his slow transformation into a human-zombie hybrid makes him both a potential saviour and a threat. Scientists are mostly ambiguous or dangerous: the researchers who ran the prison trial abandon Murphy when the outbreak reaches them, and Dr. Kurian, revealed to have created the virus in the first place, uses the search for a cure to pursue his own ends. Reviewers and episode guides describe his vaccine as a failure. Like most zombie fiction, the series is not concerned with scientific plausibility, and its tone is often deliberately absurd.

The story

The series begins three years into a zombie apocalypse caused by a virus that has killed most of humanity. Shortly before society collapsed, Alvin Bernard Murphy, an inmate at a naval prison in Maine, was one of three prisoners given experimental vaccines in a government-approved trial; he alone survived the injection, and when the prison fell he was bitten repeatedly without turning into a zombie. His blood carries antibodies that may be humanity's last hope for a vaccine, but he is slowly changing into a blue-skinned hybrid of human and zombie able to control some zombies. A small band of survivors, led by Lieutenant Roberta Warren and guided by the hacker Citizen Z, sets out to take him across the country to a functioning laboratory. At a government lab in Colorado, the group meets Dr. Kurian, who claims to be their new contact but turns out to have created the virus; his own vaccine later fails.
