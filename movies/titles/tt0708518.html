The genetics

The episode deals with the consequences of human genetic enhancement in the Star Trek universe. The four savants, introduced in "Statistical Probabilities", were engineered as children to have extraordinary intelligence, but their modifications left them with behavioural problems that make it hard for them to live in society: Jack is aggressive and hyperactive, Lauren hypersexual, Patrick childlike and Sarina catatonic. Bashir, who was enhanced without side effects, cures Sarina's catalepsy, which Memory Alpha describes as a side effect of her genetic modification, by correcting the neural connections it left incomplete. The episode presents genetic enhancement as a gamble with lasting human costs. Its main subject, though, is consent and autonomy: Sarina must decide who she is rather than repay the man who cured her. Real genetic enhancement of intelligence is not possible today, and intelligence depends on many genes of small effect.

How genetics is portrayed

Overall portrayal of genetics
negative Genetic enhancement appears as a gamble with lasting human costs, contrasted with Bashir's lucky success; the episode's main subject is Sarina's autonomy, but its genetic backdrop is cautionary.
Outcome of the genetic intervention
negative The only genetic intervention is the savants' childhood enhancement, which gave them great intelligence but left them aggressive, hypersexual, childlike or catatonic and confined to an institute; Sarina's cure is Bashir's neurosurgery, not genetics, and Bashir's own enhancement is the lone success.

The story

Jack, Lauren and Patrick, three genetically enhanced savants, arrive on Deep Space Nine posing as an admiral and his staff and bring with them Sarina, who has been catatonic for most of her life. They want Dr. Julian Bashir, himself genetically enhanced, to cure her. With their help Bashir refines a medical instrument and operates on her, and for the first time Sarina is able to speak and connect with her surroundings; within minutes of her reunion with her friends she masters singing and joins them in an improvised fugue. Bashir introduces her to the station's crew and falls in love with her, handing her care to another doctor so that he can court her. Overwhelmed, Sarina withdraws into her catatonic state again, and later explains that she felt pressured to become the woman of his dreams to thank him for curing her. She leaves the station for a scientific internship.
