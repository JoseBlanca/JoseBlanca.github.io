The genetics

The catastrophe is a biological weapon: the plague released in a war between China and the Soviet Union kills most of humanity and turns many survivors into nocturnal, albino mutants hypersensitive to light. The film does not describe how the agent was made, but its origin in military biological warfare places it among the lab-made pathogens of genetics cinema. Against this, science is also the hope: the army scientist Robert Neville survived thanks to an experimental vaccine and, with a serum made from his own blood, cures a dying boy and leaves the survivors the means to rebuild humanity; the film stages his death as a crucifixion of a saviour. The antagonists, the Family, reject science and technology as the cause of the disaster and want Neville burned as a heretic. The film thus blames the weaponisation of biology for the end of the world while presenting the scientist as its redeemer. Unlike Matheson's novel, which explains the disease as bacterial vampirism, the film's mutants are a product of the weapon.

The story

In this adaptation of Richard Matheson's novel I Am Legend, a border war between China and the Soviet Union in 1975 escalates into biological warfare that kills most of humanity. Colonel Robert Neville, a US Army scientist in Los Angeles, saved himself by injecting an experimental vaccine. Two years later, believing himself the last healthy man, he spends his days hunting members of the Family, a cult of light-sensitive, albino plague survivors led by the former newscaster Matthias, who blame science and technology for the catastrophe and want to destroy Neville. Captured and about to be burned, he is rescued by Lisa and Dutch, a former medical student. Neville makes a serum from his own blood and cures Lisa's brother Richie, who goes to offer the cure to the Family and is killed. Lisa succumbs to the disease and lets the Family into Neville's home; Matthias wounds Neville, who dies after handing Dutch a flask of serum, and the survivors leave the city.
