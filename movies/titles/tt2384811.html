The genetics

The conspiracy at the heart of the series is a eugenic one carried out with genetic science. The Network, a secret organization with roots in a biotechnology company and in government, intends to release the Janus virus and to control humanity through a remedy that works only for particular genetic profiles and races; Wilson, after being captured, comes to accept its argument that mass sterilization is needed to save humanity from extinction. The virus was created by the scientist Philip Carvel, who hid it in the genetic make-up of his own daughter, Jessica, to keep it from the Network, a choice that has left her hunted all her life. The series presents the Network as ruthless villains, while raising the question of whether drastic measures against overpopulation could be justified. A virus designed to act selectively on people with particular genetic profiles is a familiar fear about bioweapons, but no such pathogen is known.

The story

A group of fans of a cult graphic novel, The Utopia Experiments, which seems to have predicted real epidemics, are drawn together when one of them finds the manuscript of its unpublished sequel. He is murdered by killers working for a secret organization known as the Network, and the others, Ian, Becky, Wilson and the 11-year-old Grant, see their lives destroyed as they are hunted. They are joined by Jessica Hyde, who has been on the run from the Network all her life, while the civil servant Michael Dugdale is blackmailed into working for it. They discover that the Network, linked to the biotechnology company Corvadt, plans to release a virus called Janus and to sell a remedy, and that Jessica's father, the scientist Philip Carvel, hid the key to Janus in Jessica herself. In the second series they find Carvel, now a confused old man, and confront a Network plan built around a Russian flu pandemic and a vaccination campaign. The series was cancelled after two series.
