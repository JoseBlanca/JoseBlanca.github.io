The genetics

The film is about human evolution and the control of the body, rather than genetics in a narrow sense. Humanity is changing in ways nobody fully understands, from the loss of pain to the spontaneous growth of new organs, and the state responds by cataloguing and suppressing these changes through a registry, while the evolutionists want to accelerate them and adapt human beings to a world full of plastic and synthetic chemicals. A boy born with the ability to digest plastic is the key evidence that the change is real, and the authorities destroy that evidence. Scientists appear only marginally: surgeons, bureaucrats and engineers of biomedical machines who are as invested in art and secrecy as in knowledge. As in much of David Cronenberg's work, biology is a metaphor for art, sexuality and our relationship with technology, and the film does not attempt a scientific explanation of its changes. Real human evolution happens over many generations through inherited genetic changes, not through the sudden appearance of new organs in individuals.

How genetics is portrayed

Overall portrayal of genetics
neutral Evolution and mutation are metaphors for art, sexuality and our relationship to technology, with no genetic science or geneticists to colour; the state's suppression of the changes concerns bodies and secrecy rather than genetic practice.
Outcome of the genetic intervention
not applicable No genetic work is done: the bodily changes are presented as spontaneous evolution of unknown origin, and what characters do to bodies (organ removal, digestive modification, replacing Brecken's organs) is surgery, not framed in genetic terms.

The story

In an unspecified future, humans have undergone biological changes of unknown origin: most no longer feel pain or suffer infections, so surgery can be performed on conscious people, and some develop radical changes. Saul Tenser, who has "accelerated evolution syndrome", grows new organs, which his partner Caprice removes surgically in performances before an audience. The National Organ Registry, a government office that catalogues new organs to enforce limits on human evolution, takes an interest in them. Tenser also secretly informs for the police on a cell of radical evolutionists who have modified their digestive systems to eat plastic. Their leader, Lang, wants Tenser to perform a public autopsy of his son Brecken, who was born able to digest plastic and was killed by his mother. During the autopsy, the boy's organs turn out to have been replaced to hide the change, and Lang is murdered. Finally Tenser, struggling to eat, eats a plastic bar himself.
