The genetics

The series rests on artificial life created by humans. To survive their exodus from Earth, humans created plants, artificial organic life forms that generate energy, and they use them as power sources for ships and cities; the protagonists, Vash and Knives, were born with a special connection to the plants, and Knives turns against humanity because of the plants' exploitation, shown when overworked plants turn red. The story thus raises the question of humanity's obligations towards the life it has engineered and exploited, framed as a conflict between Vash's hope for coexistence and Knives's genocidal revenge; a research group, the Sinners, also appears as an ally of Knives. The available sources say little about how the plants were made or about the researchers, and they cover only the first part of the series, so the genetic themes of the conclusion are not described here.

The story

This anime adaptation of Yasuhiro Nightow's manga Trigun was released in two parts: Trigun Stampede (2023) and its conclusion, Trigun Stargaze (2026). With Earth uninhabitable, humanity travels in colony fleets powered by plants, artificial organic life forms that produce limitless energy. Twin brothers, Vash and Knives, born with a special connection to the plants, are raised aboard a ship by Rem Saverem. Knives sabotages the fleet, which crashes on the desert planet Noman's Land; Rem dies saving the twins, and Knives reveals that he wants to kill all humans for exploiting the plants. Years later, the human settlers depend entirely on plants, while Vash, a pacifist outlaw with a huge bounty on his head, seeks peace between humans and plants. With two reporters and the undertaker Wolfwood, he pursues his brother, now allied with a research group, the Sinners. In the first part's finale, Knives takes Vash's memories and begins a process to transform the planet. The available sources do not describe the second part.
