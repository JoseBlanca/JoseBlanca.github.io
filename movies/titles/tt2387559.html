The genetics

The film, inspired by the Canadian comedy Starbuck, uses sperm donation to explore what makes someone a father. The genetic link is the premise: one anonymous donor has 533 biological children, and 142 of them sue to learn his identity, raising the question of a donor-conceived person's right to know their genetic origins. The story weighs biology and upbringing together: David's paternity is purely genetic, yet the film shows him becoming a father to these young adults only by choosing to know and care for them, while he also prepares to raise the child Emma is expecting. The children are portrayed as individuals with their own lives, not as copies of their father. Critics called it a heartfelt celebration of parenthood under wildly exaggerated circumstances. No genetic technology is involved beyond the donation itself; the film is about the bonds that follow from it.

The story

David Wozniak, an amiable underachiever who drives deliveries for his family's butcher shop in New York, owes $80,000 to thugs, and his girlfriend Emma, a police officer, is pregnant. A lawyer for a fertility clinic informs him that the 693 sperm donations he made as a student, under the alias 'Starbuck', have produced 533 children, 142 of whom are suing to learn his identity. His friend Brett, a struggling lawyer, fights to keep the records sealed and gives David profiles of the plaintiffs. David secretly seeks them out and becomes a guardian angel to several, covering for an aspiring actor at work and saving a daughter from a drug overdose. After the thugs assault his father, he lets Brett countersue the clinic and wins $200,000 on condition of anonymity. He confesses to his father, who pays his debt, reveals himself as Starbuck on Facebook, and reaches the hospital as Emma gives birth, where many of his children come to meet him.
