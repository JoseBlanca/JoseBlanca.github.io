The genetics

The mutant cure is the film's central idea. In the X-Men world, mutants owe their powers to an X-gene, and Worthington Labs, founded by an industrialist whose son is a mutant, develops a treatment from Jimmy's genome that permanently suppresses it. The science is presented through Dr. Kavita Rao, a researcher at the company, and the film is ambivalent about the scientists' intentions: the cure is offered as voluntary, but it is soon used as a weapon, fired at mutants in darts. The story asks whether a genetic difference is a disease to be treated or an identity to be protected. Some mutants, such as Rogue, want the cure because their powers keep them from normal lives, while others, led by Magneto, see it as an attempt to eliminate their kind, and the film leaves Rogue's choice to take it as a legitimate personal decision. The ending suggests that the cure may not even be permanent. The X-gene itself is a fictional device of the comics, not a real gene.

The story

Worthington Labs announces a "cure" for mutants: an injection that suppresses the X-gene responsible for their powers, made from the genome of a young mutant, Jimmy, who neutralises the powers of mutants near him. The cure attracts some mutants, such as Rogue, who cannot touch anyone without harming them, while Magneto warns that it will be used as a weapon and rebuilds his Brotherhood to destroy it. Meanwhile Jean Grey, believed dead, returns as the destructive Phoenix, kills Cyclops and Professor Xavier and joins Magneto. The cure is soon turned into a weapon, fired in darts. Magneto's army attacks Alcatraz, where the cure is produced, to kill Jimmy; the X-Men defend the island, Magneto is injected with the cure and loses his powers, and Wolverine is forced to kill Jean to stop her. In the aftermath, Rogue takes the cure, and Magneto shows a hint that his powers may be returning.
