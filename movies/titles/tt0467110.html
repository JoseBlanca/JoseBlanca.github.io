The genetics

Genetic engineering is the source of the hero's powers and of the villain's ambitions. Barsinister is a classic frustrated scientist: his genetic research has been rejected by the establishment, and he turns to secret, unlicensed experiments to prove himself and then to seize power. The laboratory accident alters the dog's DNA, granting abilities from flight to speech, and his later plans, harvesting the hero's DNA and turning it into pills, treat genetics as a source of power to be bottled and distributed. The film is a family comedy based on the 1960s cartoon, and its science is pure fantasy: no change to DNA could give a dog flight or human speech, and real genetic modification of an adult animal would not take effect instantly. The story nevertheless follows the familiar pattern of the mad scientist undone by his own creation, with the dogs he mistreated finally turning against him.

The story

Dr. Simon Barsinister, a scientist whose proposal for genetic experiments has been rejected, sets out to prove his theory in a secret laboratory, assisted by his henchman Cad. Cad kidnaps a beagle that has been dismissed from the police bomb squad, but the dog escapes after a fire and exposure to the laboratory's chemicals alter his DNA, giving him super strength, speed, hearing, smell, the ability to fly and the power of speech. He is adopted by Dan Unger, a former police officer, and his teenage son Jack, who names him Shoeshine. Secretly, he becomes the caped hero Underdog. Unable to recreate the serum, Barsinister tries to obtain Underdog's DNA, turns his powers into "super pills" given to three German Shepherds, and plans to use a mind-control serum in a bomb to take over Capital City. Underdog rescues Molly and Polly, carries the bomb away before it explodes, and Barsinister is arrested and injected with an antidote.
