The genetics

Genetic engineering is the core of the episode. Scientists at a Federation research station have designed children with enhanced abilities, including telepathy and a super-immune system, and Dr. Pulaski first greets them as "the next step in human evolution". The improvement backfires: the children's immune defence, which alters pathogens genetically, becomes a weapon that rewrites the DNA of everyone around them and kills by accelerated ageing. The researchers are well-meaning but have failed to foresee the consequences of their designs, and the children end up condemned to permanent isolation, unintended victims of the experiment. The episode is a cautionary tale about human enhancement. Scientifically, antibodies are proteins that bind targets but do not alter DNA, and restoring someone's genome from a hair sample through a "transporter" is pure fiction; the link between DNA damage and ageing, however, is a real field of research.

The story

The Enterprise finds the supply ship USS Lantree adrift, its entire crew dead of old age although many were young. The ship's last stop was the Darwin Genetic Research Station on Gagarin IV, whose scientists are now ageing rapidly too and beg Captain Picard to evacuate the station's genetically engineered children, who have been kept in isolation. Dr. Pulaski examines one of the children, a telepathic teenager, aboard a shuttle piloted by Data, and catches the disease. She discovers that the children were engineered with aggressive immune systems that fight pathogens by genetically altering them, even outside their bodies; reacting to a harmless flu carried by a Lantree officer, they produced airborne antibodies that rewrite the DNA of ordinary humans and make them age. Using a strand of Pulaski's hair as a genetic template, the crew restores her through the transporter, then treats the station's staff. The children must remain isolated.
