The genetics

Genetics and biotechnology are the subject of the series, and its central characters are working scientists. NorBAC's cases range from bioterrorism and outbreaks of unknown diseases to embryo manipulation and mad cow disease, and the show often uses them to address social, political and ethical questions. The scientists are portrayed as skilled but fallible professionals who work for a public agency rather than for a corporation or a villain, and are as likely to solve a crisis as to be drawn into its ethics. The Mick storyline illustrates the show's approach: a father's attempt to correct a disease gene in an embryo has unintended effects elsewhere in the genome, a concern that still applies to real embryo editing. The series' scientific consultant was Aled Edwards, a Canadian molecular biologist and director of the Structural Genomics Consortium, and the first-season DVD included white papers by the Ontario Genomics Institute on the facts behind the fiction.

The story

This Canadian series, which ran for four seasons from 2004 to 2008, follows the scientists of NorBAC, the North American Biotechnology Advisory Commission, a fictional agency based in Toronto that investigates scientific threats across North America, such as bioterrorism, mysterious diseases and sudden environmental changes. The team is led by David Sandström, a molecular biologist and the agency's chief scientist, and includes the geneticist and physician Carlos Serrano, the biochemist Bob Melnikov, virologists and bioinformaticians, together with the agency's executive directors, who handle its political pressures. Episodes combine scientific detective work with personal storylines. In the first season, for example, Sandström befriends Mick, a teenage boy who believes he is a clone: he was in fact a frozen embryo whose DNA his father, a doctor, manipulated to remove a cancer-causing gene, with unintended changes that left him ill.
