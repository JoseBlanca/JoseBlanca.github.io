The genetics

Cloning is the premise of this deadpan satire, which imagines a society where people can commission an adult "double" to replace them after death, regulated by laws that require a duel when the original and the double both want to live. The cloning technology itself is never explained, and scientists hardly appear; the procedure is presented as an ordinary commercial service handled by clinics and the legal system. The film is less interested in genetics than in identity and worth: the double, genetically the same but shaped by different experiences, becomes a different person and is preferred by Sarah's family, while Sarah herself changes through training. It thus illustrates, in absurdist form, that sharing a genome does not make two people the same. Producing an adult clone ready to take over someone's life is not possible; a real clone would develop from an embryo and grow up over years.

The story

In the near future, Sarah, a depressed alcoholic in a dull relationship, learns that she is terminally ill. To spare her boyfriend Peter and her overbearing mother the pain of losing her, she pays for a clone of herself, "Sarah's Double", who is to be trained to replace her after her death. The double soon develops her own personality and wins over Peter and Sarah's mother. When Sarah unexpectedly goes into remission and asks for the clone to be decommissioned, the double refuses, and by law the two must fight a publicised duel to the death a year later. Sarah trains with an instructor, Trent, and grows tougher. Shortly before the duel, the two Sarahs meet and agree to flee together, but the double poisons Sarah during a hike. Declared to be the original by a court, the double finds herself trapped in the same unhappy life, dominated by Peter and Sarah's mother, who both know the truth.
