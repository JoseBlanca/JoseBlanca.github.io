The genetics

Genetics underlies the film's premise rather than its plot. In the X-Men universe, mutants are humans born with a genetic mutation, later called the X-gene, that gives them special powers. Most of the story concerns politics and the Cold War, but genetic themes appear in Shaw's belief that mutants are a superior species, which the film sets against its Auschwitz opening and Shaw's Nazi past, and in the debate over whether mutants should hide or take pride in what they are. Hank McCoy, a young CIA-funded scientist, creates a serum from Raven's DNA to make mutants look "normal", which she refuses and which backfires on him, a parable about trying to "cure" difference. The X-Men have often been read as an allegory of minorities facing prejudice. Real mutations do not confer powers of this kind.

The story

In 1944, at Auschwitz, the Nazi officer Klaus Schmidt sees the young Erik Lehnsherr bend a metal gate with his mind and kills his mother to make his power appear. In 1962, Erik, now a hunter of Nazis, is tracking Schmidt, who calls himself Sebastian Shaw and leads the mutant Hellfire Club. CIA officer Moira MacTaggert enlists the telepath Charles Xavier, a new Oxford graduate, and his adoptive sister Raven, a shapeshifter. Charles saves Erik, and with the CIA they recruit young mutants, including the scientist Hank McCoy. Shaw plans to provoke a nuclear war through the Cuban Missile Crisis, so that mutants can inherit the earth. Hank develops a "cure" for mutant appearance from Raven's DNA, but it turns him into the blue-furred Beast. The mutants stop the war and Erik kills Shaw, but Charles is paralysed and Erik, now Magneto, leaves with Raven and others to lead mutants separately.
