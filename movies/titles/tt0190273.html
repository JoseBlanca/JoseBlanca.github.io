The genetics

Blood recasts the vampire myth as a story of genetic engineering and drug addiction. Its premise is a backstory experiment: Dyson's Project Elixir engineered a child whose bloodstream was meant to carry a universal panacea, and the result was a person whose blood is a powerful narcotic and who must replenish huge amounts of blood. The sources do not say who funded the project or how the engineering was done. Dyson's aim was benevolent, but he becomes the victim of his own creation, addicted, estranged from his wife and driven to murder; the film, as the Moria review describes it, inverts the vampire story so that people are hooked on the "vampire's" blood. Lix, a girl engineered as a living medicine, is treated as a resource by everyone around her. The idea is metaphorical rather than scientific.

The story

Twenty years ago Dr. Carl Dyson headed Project Elixir, which genetically engineered a young girl, Lix, so that her blood would cure all human ills; instead her blood became a highly addictive narcotic. Now a successful author, Dyson learns that Lix is held prisoner by addicts who feed on her blood, and he rescues her and takes her to his home in the English Midlands. Soon he tries her blood himself and becomes addicted. His wife, Heather, leaves him in disgust, and Dyson and Lix become lovers. Each time someone drinks from Lix she must replace nine times as much blood, so the couple turn to hijacking blood vans and killing to feed both his addiction and her need. Dyson's mind unravels, and the two end locked in a cycle of dependency and violence.
