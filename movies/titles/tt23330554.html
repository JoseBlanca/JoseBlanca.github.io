The genetics

This sequel replaces the first film's simple premise with a genetic origin story for its monsters. In the backstory, Dr. Arthur Gallup paid a man to kidnap local children and crossed them with animal DNA, creating human-animal hybrids with enhanced healing; he killed and buried them, but they came back from their graves as the creatures of the Hundred Acre Wood. Pooh himself turns out to be Christopher's kidnapped twin, Billy. Gallup never appears alive and the film tells us nothing about his motives; he is simply the mad scientist behind the horror, and his accomplice dies of guilt. As the Moria review notes, the explanation moves the story towards The Island of Dr. Moreau. The genetics is gory fantasy and serves mainly to justify a slasher.

The story

After surviving Pooh's massacre in the Hundred Acre Wood, Christopher Robin is blamed for the killings by the town of Ashdown, loses his hospital job and sees a hypnotherapist about the kidnapping of his twin brother Billy years ago. In the wood, Pooh, Tigger and Owl hide after their home is burned; hunters kill Piglet, and Pooh slaughters them. At the hospital Christopher recognises the janitor, Cavendish, as Billy's kidnapper. Cavendish confesses that he abducted children for Dr. Arthur Gallup, a scientist who experimented on them with animal genes, turning them into human-animal hybrids with enhanced healing; Gallup killed and buried them, Cavendish killed Gallup, and the children dug themselves out. Cavendish kills himself. Pooh, Tigger and Owl rampage through Ashdown, killing Christopher's parents and a warehouse full of partygoers. In the wood Christopher calls Pooh by his real name, Billy, and kills him with an axe; Owl and Tigger vow to revive their friends.
