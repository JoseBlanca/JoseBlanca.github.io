The genetics

As in the rest of the series, the disaster comes from biotechnology. The T-virus, developed by the Umbrella Corporation, has turned most of humanity into zombies and gives superhuman abilities to the few, such as Alice and Wesker, whose cells bond with it. In this film Wesker has used the virus on himself and, unable to control it, believes that consuming fresh human DNA, and above all Alice, will restore his control. Umbrella also lures survivors to the ship Arcadia to use them as test subjects. Alice's army of clones, from an earlier Umbrella programme, appears only in the opening attack. Umbrella and Wesker are sinister and exploitative villains, and biotechnology appears mainly as the source of power and horror. A real virus could not produce zombies or superhuman powers, and the idea that eating DNA pacifies a virus has no basis in biology.

The story

Alice and an army of her clones attack Umbrella's headquarters in Tokyo. The Umbrella chairman Albert Wesker escapes and destroys the facility, killing the clones, and injects the real Alice with a serum that removes her superhuman powers before his plane crashes. Six months later Alice reaches Alaska in search of Arcadia, a rumoured safe haven, and finds only the amnesiac Claire Redfield, controlled by a device on her chest. In Los Angeles they join survivors trapped in a prison besieged by the undead, including Claire's brother Chris, and escape through the sewers after a giant axe-wielding monster breaks in. Arcadia turns out to be a ship used by Umbrella as a trap to collect test subjects. Alice and the Redfields free the prisoners and defeat Wesker, now struggling to control the T-virus in his body, who wants to consume Alice. Alice plans to make Arcadia a real refuge as an Umbrella squadron led by a mind-controlled Jill Valentine approaches.
