The genetics

The episode is about prejudice against genetically modified people. The Illyrians genetically engineer themselves, which the Federation forbids, and Number One has hidden her Illyrian identity to serve in Starfleet. Her genetic difference saves the crew, although not by providing a cure from her blood: her healing ability rescues La'An, whose system then yields the antibodies for a cure. Yet she wonders whether Pike would have treated her differently had she not been useful. The colonists' attempt to undo their modifications in order to be accepted ends in disaster, presenting the ban as a source of suffering. The episode connects to the Star Trek tradition, rooted in the Eugenics Wars, of treating human genetic enhancement as dangerous, and questions it from the point of view of those excluded. No scientist is portrayed as a villain. Re-engineering adult humans in this way is far beyond current genetics, and the episode is interested in the ethics and politics of the ban rather than in the technique.

The story

The Enterprise investigates the disappearance of a colony of Illyrians, a people banned by the Federation because they practise genetic engineering on themselves. As an ion storm approaches, members of the away team return to the ship unknowingly infected with a virus that makes them crave light, and the infection spreads among the crew. Number One, the ship's first officer, is immune because she is secretly an Illyrian herself; she reveals this, but her blood holds no antibodies to make a cure from. The cure comes after her healing ability saves the infected security chief, La'An, from radiation, leaving antibodies in La'An's system that Nurse Chapel synthesises. Trapped on the planet, Pike and Spock discover that the colonists had been trying to reverse their genetic modifications in order to join the Federation, which left them vulnerable to the disease; they seem to have become the beings of light in the storm that protect the two officers. Afterwards Number One offers to resign, but Pike refuses. The virus reached the ship because Dr. M'Benga keeps his terminally ill daughter in stasis in a transporter.
