The genetics

The episode is about prejudice against genetically modified people. The Illyrians genetically engineer themselves, which the Federation forbids, and Number One has hidden her Illyrian identity to serve in Starfleet. Her genetic difference saves the crew, because her immunity makes her blood the source of a cure, yet she wonders whether Pike would have treated her differently had she not been useful. The colonists' attempt to undo their modifications in order to be accepted ends in disaster, presenting the ban as a source of suffering. The episode connects to the Star Trek tradition, rooted in the Eugenics Wars, of treating human genetic enhancement as dangerous, and questions it from the point of view of those excluded. No scientist is portrayed as a villain. Re-engineering adult humans in this way is far beyond current genetics, and the episode is interested in the ethics and politics of the ban rather than in the technique.

The story

The Enterprise investigates the disappearance of a colony of Illyrians, a people banned by the Federation because they practise genetic engineering on themselves. As an ion storm approaches, members of the away team return to the ship unknowingly infected with a virus that makes them crave light, and the infection spreads among the crew. Number One, the ship's first officer, is immune because she is secretly an Illyrian herself; she reveals this so that a cure can be synthesised from her blood. Trapped on the planet by the storm, Captain Pike and Spock discover that the colonists had been trying to reverse their genetic modifications in order to join the Federation, and may have created the virus and been transformed into plasma-like beings. These beings protect the two officers until they can return. Afterwards Number One offers to resign, but Pike refuses. The virus had reached the ship because Dr. M'Benga is using an outdated transporter to hold his daughter, who has a rare disease, in stasis.
