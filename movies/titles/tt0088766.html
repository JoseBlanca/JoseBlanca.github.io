The genetics

As in the 1956 film, the genetic theme is inherited evil. Christine openly wonders whether a lack of morality can be hereditary, discovers that her biological mother was a serial killer, and fears that Rachel has inherited her psychopathy. The neighbours first explain Rachel's coldness as a normal defence mechanism, but the story proves them wrong and sides with nature: a child raised in a comfortable, caring home is born to kill. Unlike the 1956 version, the ending does not punish the child: Rachel survives her mother's attempt to kill her, implying that the bad seed lives on. No geneticist or test appears; the heredity is a matter of family history. Real behavioural genetics finds that antisocial traits are only partly heritable, depend on many genes and on the environment, and are not passed down as a fixed compulsion to kill.

The story

In this television remake, nine-year-old Rachel Penmark loses a penmanship medal to her classmate Mark Daigler, who is found dead near a pier after a school trip to the beach. Her widowed mother Christine notices that Rachel seems untouched by the death and later finds the medal hidden in a locket. A neighbour, Emory, is writing a book about female serial killers and mentions the case of Bessie Danker, which Christine's father Richard Bravo once investigated. Haunted by a recurring nightmare, Christine learns from Richard that it is a real memory: she is Danker's child, adopted after the killer's arrest. The janitor Leroy taunts Rachel about the boy's death, and she locks him in a shed and sets it on fire. Christine gives Rachel an overdose of sleeping pills and shoots herself, but Rachel survives and goes to live with Richard.
