The genetics

The genetic element is Dr. Malcolm Truss's research on cell cloning, abandoned while he worked at Massive Dynamic, which a shapeshifter forces him to turn into a serum. Nadine belongs to a new generation of shapeshifters who can take any human form; she presents her need as a medical cure, mentions a daughter, and kills to obtain it, and the serum apparently stabilises or strengthens her abilities, making her harder to stop. Truss is a victim of coercion rather than a villain: he works under threat of death and is horrified when he learns what she is. The episode is mainly about Peter's return and Walter's guilt over his past experiments, so the cloning research serves as the engine of the case. The sources do not describe how the serum works, and cloning cells has nothing to do with changing shape.

The story

In this Fringe episode, Peter Bishop has returned from an erased timeline and is held at Fringe headquarters. The Fringe team investigates the murders of Karen Truss and her boyfriend, the former wife and partner of Dr. Malcolm Truss, a biochemical researcher once at Massive Dynamic. The killer is Nadine, a new kind of shapeshifter who can pass for any human and who wants Truss's abandoned cell-cloning research. Posing as a patient, she finds Truss before the team does and forces him to synthesise a cure in his secret laboratory; he discovers her nature when she briefly takes his dead wife's form. Peter, from his cell, helps the team trace her through a tracking signal he finds on a recovered memory disc. When agents surround the laboratory, Nadine takes the serum, fakes her death by taking the form of a dead agent and escapes, then reports her success through a typewriter that warns that more shapeshifters are coming.
