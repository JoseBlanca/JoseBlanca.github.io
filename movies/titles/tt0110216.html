The genetics

Genetics plays a modest role in this comedy; its real subject is reproduction and gender roles. The protagonist is a research geneticist and the heroine a scientist who works on egg freezing, but the science involves a fertility drug, in vitro fertilisation and the implantation of an embryo in a man's abdomen. The scientists are ambitious and break the rules: they bypass the FDA, keep the trial secret and use an egg without its donor's consent, which the film treats as a romantic complication rather than an ethical breach. A greedy lab director who tries to take the credit serves as the villain. The film's interest lies in Alex learning empathy and the emotional side of parenthood. Male pregnancy by implanting an embryo in the abdomen has never been attempted; specialists consider it theoretically conceivable but so dangerous for parent and child that it is unlikely ever to be studied in humans. The title fits the database mainly through its geneticist protagonist.

The story

Austrian research geneticist Dr. Alex Hesse and obstetrician Dr. Larry Arbogast have developed Expectane, a drug to reduce the risk of miscarriage, but the FDA will not let them test it on humans. With a pharmaceutical company willing to fund the trial if they find a volunteer, Larry persuades Alex to test it on himself: an egg codenamed "Junior" is fertilised and implanted in his abdomen. Alex suffers morning sickness and mood swings, and when the trial should end he decides to carry the pregnancy to term. He falls for Dr. Diana Reddin, a scientist from the egg-freezing department, who is furious to learn the egg was hers but comes to accept that she is the mother and he the father. Alex hides in a retreat for expectant mothers disguised as a woman, the lab director tries to claim credit, and Alex finally gives birth by caesarean to a healthy girl.
