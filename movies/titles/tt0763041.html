The genetics

The connection with genetics is loose. Two of the three segments use themes associated with it, cloning and human–animal hybrids, but neither involves genetic science. Homer's clones come from a cursed magic hammock, and the joke about each copy being less intelligent than the last parodies the idea that copies degrade, not any real process. Dr. Hibbert's hybrids parody The Island of Doctor Moreau, and the episode does not explain how he transforms people. Hibbert is presented as a mad doctor whose victims end up enjoying their new animal lives. The episode is a comic pastiche of horror and science fiction conventions rather than a story about genetics, and its inclusion in a genetics database is marginal.

The story

The fourteenth-season Halloween anthology of The Simpsons contains three segments. In "Send in the Clones", a parody of Multiplicity, Homer buys a cursed hammock that produces copies of anyone who lies in it; he uses his clones to do his chores, but they grow progressively dimmer, multiply into an army and overrun Springfield until the government lures them over a cliff with giant doughnuts, leaving Marge with a kinder clone. In "The Fright to Creep and Scare Harms", Lisa's campaign to ban guns leaves the town defenceless against undead outlaws led by Billy the Kid. In "The Island of Dr. Hibbert", a parody of The Island of Doctor Moreau, the family visit Dr. Hibbert's island resort, where he turns Marge into a panther and other residents of Springfield into human–animal hybrids; Homer, turned into a walrus, decides that he likes it.
