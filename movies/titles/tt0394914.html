The genetics

The episode is built around the difference between treating a disease and changing who a child will be. Correcting the inherited spinal defect is presented as uncontroversial medicine, but B'Elanna's wish to erase her daughter's Klingon features, so that she will not be rejected as she was, turns gene editing into a way of avoiding discrimination by changing the child rather than the society. Tom and the Doctor stand for acceptance of the child as she is, and the Doctor's integrity is tested when his program is secretly altered. B'Elanna's motives are shown with sympathy, rooted in childhood pain and fear of abandonment, and she finally accepts her daughter's heritage. The distinction between therapy and enhancement or cosmetic selection is central to real bioethical debates on germline editing, and the episode also touches on hybrid ancestry, since the child is a quarter Klingon.

The story

B'Elanna Torres, Voyager's half-Klingon chief engineer, discovers that she is pregnant by her husband, Tom Paris. The Doctor finds that the seven-week-old fetus has a genetic defect that causes an abnormal curvature of the spine in Klingon females, the same condition B'Elanna had surgically corrected as a child, and proposes to fix it by genetic resequencing. When a holographic projection shows that her daughter will have Klingon forehead ridges, B'Elanna, haunted by memories of being teased as a child and of her human father leaving the family, asks the Doctor to remove the baby's Klingon traits as well. Tom and the Doctor object, and Captain Janeway treats it as a matter for the couple. The Doctor suddenly changes his mind and declares the procedure medically necessary; Icheb finds an error in his assessment, Seven discovers that his program has been tampered with, and Tom stops the operation in time. B'Elanna admits her fear that Tom will leave her, the alterations are undone, and she asks the Doctor to be the child's godfather.
