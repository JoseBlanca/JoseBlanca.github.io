The genetics

Genetic engineering is at the centre of the series. It shows a university world in which students practise biohacking, rewriting genes in makeshift labs, and contrasts this do-it-yourself culture with the ambitions of a celebrated scientist. Prof. Lorenz is a brilliant but ruthless researcher who conducts illegal human experiments, implanting edited embryos without their parents' knowledge and releasing engineered mosquitoes to infect people so that she can demonstrate her gene therapy. Mia, both her victim and a talented student, is drawn into illegal genetic experimentation herself. The production was advised by scientists from the Fraunhofer Institute for Molecular Biology and Applied Ecology, but reviewers found the ease with which students produce gene-rewriting drugs implausible. The embryo-editing storyline echoes the case of He Jiankui, who in 2018 announced the birth of the first gene-edited babies in China and was later imprisoned.

The story

A German Netflix techno-thriller in two seasons (2020-2021). Mia Akerlund starts medical school at the University of Freiburg, shares a flat with other students and grows close to Jasper, a talented biology student and assistant to the star geneticist Prof. Tanja Lorenz, and his roommate Niklas. Mia is in fact Emma Engels, the twin sister of Ben Engels, and she has come to take revenge on Lorenz, whom she blames for Ben's death. She discovers that Lorenz used her fertility clinic to implant genetically manipulated embryos and experimented on the resulting children, among them Mia and Ben, to develop her gene therapy. In the first season's finale, genetically modified mosquitoes, released on Lorenz's orders, infect the passengers of a train with a virus that only her therapy can cure. In the second season, Mia wakes up with amnesia and a deliberately induced prion disease, and confronts a new group of rogue scientists.
