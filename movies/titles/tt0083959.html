The genetics

Forbidden World is a low-budget Roger Corman production in the vein of Alien, and genetic engineering supplies its monster. Subject 20 is a designed organism made from a synthetic DNA strain and, it turns out, human DNA, created by a research team with the benevolent aim of solving a food shortage. The scientists are shown as secretive and reckless: they hide the creature's human origin and resist destroying it, and their creation kills almost everyone on the station. The film's genetics is pure invention, with DNA working as a kind of acid or contagion: Proto B erases the genetic differences between cells in its victims' cells and reduces them to edible protein, and cancer cells act as a poison that makes the creature self-destruct. As in many films of the period, the story mainly serves as a warning about creating new life forms without controls rather than as an exploration of real science.

The story

On Xarbia, a remote desert planet, scientists at a genetic research station have created an experimental life form, Subject 20, from a synthetic DNA strain called Proto B, intended to end a galaxy-wide food crisis. The organism mutates rapidly, kills the laboratory animals and encloses itself in a cocoon. Troubleshooter Mike Colby arrives with his robot SAM-104 and wants to destroy it, but the research staff resist. Once hatched, the creature kills most of the crew one by one, and the scientists' secret comes out: Subject 20 incorporates human DNA, and it kills by injecting Proto B into its victims, turning their bodies into a mass of pure protein that it eats. In its final form, a large insect-like monster, it is destroyed when it eats the cancer-ridden liver of the station's doctor, Cal, and its body breaks down from within. Colby and lab assistant Tracy are the only survivors.
