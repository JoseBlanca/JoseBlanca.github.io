The genetics

The episode satirises genetic modification and the tobacco industry. Homer creates a hybrid plant by accident, combining tomato and tobacco seeds and irradiating the field with plutonium, a parody of fears about radiation, mutation and "Frankenfood". The new crop is a commercial success precisely because it is addictive, and a tobacco company immediately tries to buy it. There are no scientists in the story; the experimenter is an ignorant amateur, and Lisa voices the ethical objection to profiting from addiction. Nature's editors listed the episode among the top ten science moments in The Simpsons. The idea has a real basis: tomatoes and tobacco both belong to the nightshade family, and a 1959 Scientific American article reported that nicotine could be found in tomato plants grafted onto tobacco. Inspired by the episode, an Oregon fan, Rob Baur, grew a grafted "tomacco" plant in 2003, and tests found nicotine in its leaves. Unlike Homer's crop, the real tomacco was made by grafting rather than by crossing or irradiation, and its fruit looked like an ordinary tomato.

The story

Inspired by a Zorro film, Homer goes around Springfield slapping people with a glove and challenging them to duels to get his way, until a Southern colonel accepts. To escape the duel, the Simpsons flee to the farmhouse where Homer grew up. Homer tries farming, but nothing grows in the poor soil, so he asks Lenny for some plutonium. The crops grow, but because Homer had mixed tomato and tobacco seeds, a new plant appears: a tomato with brown, tobacco-flavoured flesh. He calls it "tomacco". It tastes terrible but is highly addictive, and it sells so well that executives from Laramie Cigarettes offer $150 million for the rights. Lisa protests, and Homer rejects the offer anyway, demanding $150 billion. Addicted animals from neighbouring farms then devour the crop. The last plant ends up with the executives, but their helicopter crashes and it is destroyed. Back home, Homer finally fights the duel and is shot in the arm.
