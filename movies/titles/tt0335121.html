The genetics

Godsend is a supernatural thriller built on human reproductive cloning, presented as a way to replace a dead child. Its geneticist, Richard Wells, works illegally and deceives the grieving parents: he secretly uses their son's cloning to resurrect his own dead son, whose fire-damaged DNA he mixes with Adam's. The film treats the clone as a kind of reincarnation in which personality, memories and even violent tendencies return with the DNA, and it raises the ethical questions of grief-driven cloning and of a doctor playing God with families. Biologically, a clone is a genetic copy, a later-born identical twin, not a copy of a person's mind or memories, and DNA from two individuals cannot simply be blended to bring one of them back. Mammalian cloning from an adult cell was achieved with Dolly the sheep in 1996, but human reproductive cloning has never been demonstrated.

The story

The day after his eighth birthday, Adam, the only son of Paul and Jessie Duncan, is killed by a car. Soon after, Dr. Richard Wells, a former professor of Jessie's, offers to clone the boy, an illegal procedure that requires the family to move and change identity. The new Adam grows up normally until he passes the age at which his predecessor died; then he begins to have nightmares and waking visions of a boy named Zachary, of a school on fire and of a woman killed with a hammer, and he becomes cruel and violent. Paul discovers that Zachary Clark was a disturbed boy who set fire to his school, killed his mother and died in a house fire, and that his father was a geneticist: Wells, living under a new name. Wells had mixed Zachary's DNA into Adam's clone in the hope of bringing his own son back. The family flees, but the ending suggests that Zachary has taken over.
