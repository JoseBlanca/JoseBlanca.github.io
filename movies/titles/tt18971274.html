The genetics

The episode is a courtroom drama about genetic discrimination. Illyrians modify their own genomes, and the Federation, whose ban on genetic engineering stems from the Eugenics Wars, treats them as outsiders; Ketoul calls Starfleet's rules "draconian" race laws. Una was born with her modifications and has hidden them all her life, from childhood injuries her parents dared not have treated in hospital to her Starfleet application. The episode frames the issue as one of civil rights: it is not about what scientists do, but about how a society treats people because of their genes. No geneticists appear. The tribunal's verdict is a compromise: the judge says that genetic engineering is a nuanced issue and the laws exist for good reasons, but that not every person with modified DNA should be treated the same. The debate echoes real discussions about genetic discrimination and about how future societies might treat people whose genomes have been edited.

The story

Una Chin-Riley, first officer of the Enterprise, faces a Starfleet court martial after being exposed as an Illyrian, a member of a culture that genetically modifies itself, and for having lied about it to join Starfleet, which bans genetic modification. Captain Pike persuades Neera Ketoul, a civil rights lawyer and Una's estranged Illyrian childhood friend, to defend her. Una rejects a plea deal of a dishonourable discharge, and the prosecution adds charges of sedition, seeking twenty years in a penal colony. The trial hears testimony from Una's sponsor, Admiral April, and from her crewmates. Ketoul then invokes a Starfleet code allowing asylum for those fleeing persecution because of their beliefs or their biology, argues that Una joined Starfleet to escape such persecution, and that Pike, by keeping her secret, granted it. The tribunal grants Una asylum and acquits her, while stating that the laws on genetic engineering remain necessary.
