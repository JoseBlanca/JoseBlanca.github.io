The genetics

Genetics appears as the ideology behind a genocide. The military presents the extermination of a persecuted group as "genetic cleansing", a eugenic rationale, while the psychologist Arquette, who oversees the soldiers' implants, explains that the technology exists so that they can kill without hesitation. The episode's main subject is the technology that dehumanises the enemy so that soldiers can kill without remorse, but the justification it offers is explicitly hereditarian, and critics have repeatedly drawn parallels with Nazi Germany. Sophie Gilbert of The Atlantic wrote that the episode's depiction of eugenics links to prejudices still widespread, such as institutionalised racism, tribalism and fear of refugees. No genetic science is shown; the point is how claims about heredity can be used to justify the persecution of a minority.

The story

Stripe is a soldier in a near-future army whose members carry a neural implant, MASS, that feeds them data through augmented reality. His squad hunts "roaches", pale, snarling humanoid creatures with sharp teeth. During a raid on a farmhouse, a roach points a small LED device at him, and afterwards his implant starts to malfunction. In an abandoned housing block he sees ordinary frightened people where his comrades see monsters, and he saves a woman, Catarina, and her son. She explains that the implant makes soldiers perceive her people as inhuman "roaches", and that they are victims of a genocide justified as genetic cleansing, while civilians, influenced by propaganda, see them as inferior. Hunter, Stripe's comrade, kills them both. The military psychologist Arquette reveals that Stripe agreed to the implant when he enlisted, and offers him a choice between prison and a memory wipe; Stripe, decorated, returns to a fantasy home that is in reality derelict.
