The genetics

The plot is driven by biological weapons rather than genetic engineering in a strict sense. Doku, a Japanese Second World War bioweapon, was secretly preserved by Indian intelligence agents instead of being destroyed, and the villain Yogi, a survivor of the outbreak it caused in 1981, plans to combine it with a mutated poultry superbug to cause a worldwide 'antibiotic apocalypse'. The film frames this around a message about the overuse of antibiotics, which critics found stated but not felt. No scientists play a significant role; the weapons are held and deployed by spies and mercenaries, and the story is a spy action thriller. Antibiotic resistance is a real and growing problem, but the film's mechanics, such as a bioweapon deactivated by crashing a cable car at 170 km/h, are pure invention.

The story

A dying RAW agent, Walrus, warns that the '1981 Black Operation' has returned and threatens 1.4 billion lives, and passes a knife key to agent Viji, believing him to be his father, Sardar. Viji learns that 'Doku', a catastrophic Japanese Second World War bioweapon, is secured by three such keys held by the agents of a 1981 mission. A recovered debriefing tape shows that in 1981 the young Sardar, then called Bose, helped intercept a shipment of the antidote Colistin to save an infected Indian village, but half of it was lost, and the other agents secretly kept Doku instead of destroying it. In the present, Yogi, leader of the mercenary group Black Dagger and a boy from that village, plans a global 'antibiotic apocalypse' with Doku and a mutated poultry superbug. He steals Doku's core and kills the agent Zara. Sardar and Viji stop his plan to spread infection by crashing a cable car at the Mysore Dussehra parade; Sardar kills Yogi and is cleared and reinstated.
