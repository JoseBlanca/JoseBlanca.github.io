The genetics

This film returns to the villain of The Mystery of Mamo (1978) and its themes of artificial life and immortality. The island is a laboratory of biological weapons: assassins engineered through genetic modification, discarded 'garbage men' bred as weapons, and a series of cloned girls who serve as the ruler's interpreter. Muom himself was created when Mamo gave his blood to a primate, which became intelligent and immortal and now regenerates instantly from any wound. Both Muom and his maker are villains who use biology to rule and to decide which humans should live, and the story treats immortality as monstrous, closing on Lupin's motto, 'life is fiction, enjoy it'. The biology is pure fantasy: blood carries no power to confer intelligence or immortality, and real clones are not produced as adult copies.

The story

After his hideout is attacked, Lupin III finds a map fragment pointing to an uncharted island in the Bermuda area and sets off with Jigen, Goemon and Fujiko, pursued by Inspector Zenigata. Their planes are shot down by the assassin Yael Okuzaki, and the gang is scattered across an island of decaying weapons and discarded biological weapons, covered by a toxic fog that kills outsiders within a day. Genetically modified assassins attack them. The island's ruler, Muom, an immortal who regenerates from any wound, was once a monkey given blood by a mysterious traveller and has steered history for millennia, culling humanity by 'selection and elimination'. Lupin discovers that Muom has become the island itself, sustained by rivers of blood, and destroys him by cutting this circulatory system. Fujiko learns that Muom's interpreter Salifa is one of many clones made by the true mastermind, Mamo, who wipes out the island.
