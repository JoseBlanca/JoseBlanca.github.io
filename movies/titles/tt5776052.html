The genetics

This dark reboot of Kamen Rider is built on biotechnology gone wrong. Nozama Pharmacy's research division created Amazon Cells, artificial cells that give their hosts regenerative power and monstrous, man-eating forms; its head, Reika Mizusawa, made Haruka by transplanting her own genes into Amazon Cells, and after the experimental Amazons escaped she ordered their extermination. Another executive revives a corpse as a controllable Amazon to sell them as biological weapons. A new strain of cells then spreads among humans like an infection, while a secret government unit kills the infected and DNA tests identify Chihiro as the strain's 'original'. The scientist Jin Takayama, who turned himself into an Amazon to hunt his creations out of guilt, embodies the series' central question: whether a being is defined by its biology or by its choices. The corporation and its executives are villains who treat both humans and Amazons as disposable; the Amazons themselves are portrayed with sympathy. Amazon Cells are fiction.

The story

In a world apart from the main Kamen Rider continuity, the pharmaceutical company Nozama Pharmacy has secretly created 'Amazons', artificial life forms built on Amazon Cells, and an accident has let some 4,000 experimental Amazons escape. Haruka Mizusawa, a shy young man raised by Reika Mizusawa, Nozama's head of research, stops his daily injections and transforms into Amazon Omega; he learns that he is an Amazon made from her genes. He joins the company's hunting team alongside Jin Takayama, the former Nozama cell biologist who turned himself into Amazon Alpha to destroy the creatures he helped create, but rejects the company's plan to exterminate all Amazons and leads a group of peaceful ones to safety. Five years later, a new strain of Amazon Cells starts turning ordinary people into Amazons, and the secret government unit 4C hunts them. Its focus is Chihiro, Jin's son, who struggles against cannibalistic urges. A concluding film reveals a sanctuary that farms Amazons.
