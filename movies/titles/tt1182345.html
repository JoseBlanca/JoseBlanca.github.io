Genetics on Screen

Moon

2009 Film

IMDb FilmAffinity

The genetics

Cloning is the film's central twist and its moral subject. Lunar Industries uses clones of one employee, each given implanted memories of a wife and daughter and a three-year lifespan, to avoid the cost of training and sending new astronauts; at the end of each contract, the clone believes he is going home but is incinerated. No scientists appear: the cloning is a corporate cost-saving measure, managed remotely, and the only helper the clones have is the base's AI, GERTY. The film's focus is on identity: the two Sams share the same genes and memories but differ in age and temperament, and their humanity is never in doubt for the viewer. Its science is deliberately simplified. A real clone would start life as an embryo and would have to grow up, rather than emerge as an adult copy, and memories are not inherited or transferable with DNA. Dolly the sheep, the first mammal cloned from an adult cell, was born in 1996.

The story

Sam Bell is nearing the end of a three-year contract as the only worker at Sarang, a lunar base where the company Lunar Industries mines helium-3 for energy on Earth. With live communications apparently broken, his only company is the base's computer, GERTY, and recorded messages from his wife Tess. After a rover accident, Sam wakes up in the infirmary, goes out against orders, and finds an injured double of himself in the crashed rover. GERTY admits that both are clones of the original Sam Bell, given his implanted memories. The older Sam is already deteriorating, as earlier clones did at the end of their contracts, and the two find a vault of hundreds of sleeping clones and a jamming system that blocks contact with Earth. Calling home, the older Sam learns that Tess has died and that the original Sam lives on Earth with their teenage daughter. The dying clone stays behind while the newer one escapes to Earth, where he becomes a whistleblower.
