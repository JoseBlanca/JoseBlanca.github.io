The genetics

David Cronenberg's body-horror film begins with a well-meant experimental operation. To save a burned patient, plastic surgeon Dan Keloid takes healthy tissue and makes it 'morphogenetically neutral', erasing the programming of its cells' nuclei so that it can grow into whatever tissue is needed, a fictional anticipation of stem-cell and reprogramming ideas, knowingly accepting the risk of uncontrolled growth. The graft makes his patient mutate: she grows a blood-feeding organ and becomes the source of a rabies-like epidemic that brings Montreal under martial law. Keloid is a reckless rather than evil doctor, the head of a commercial clinic who is himself infected, and the film blames no villain; critics have read its picture of military rule as an echo of the 1970 October Crisis. The disaster stems from a medical intervention that tampers with cells' developmental programme, making the film an early example of the 'good intentions, terrible consequences' story.

The story

After a motorcycle accident in the Quebec countryside, Rose Miller is badly burned and taken to the nearby Keloid Clinic for plastic surgery, where Dr. Dan Keloid tries an untested technique, grafting 'morphogenetically neutral' tissue to replace her damaged skin and organs. When Rose wakes from a month-long coma, she has developed a stinger-like organ in her armpit and can survive only on human blood. Her victims contract a rabies-like disease that makes them foam at the mouth, attack others and die. While she makes her way to Montreal, infecting people as she goes, her boyfriend Hart tries to find her, and Keloid himself becomes infected. As the epidemic spreads, martial law is declared, soldiers shoot the infected on sight and bodies are collected in garbage trucks. Hart finally finds Rose, who refuses to believe she is the source; she tests it on a man, who turns and kills her, and her body is thrown into a garbage truck.
