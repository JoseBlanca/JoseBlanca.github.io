The genetics

Genetic engineering drives the whole story. Bagi is a human–mountain lion hybrid created at the SuperLife Center by combining human and puma cells, part of a programme in which Professor Ishigami and the centre's ambitious chief had produced other similar creatures. A second thread involves an engineered rice that a dictator plans to use as a weapon, turning agricultural biotechnology into an instrument of political repression. The geneticist mother is a conflicted figure: she refuses to help build the weapon and dies for it, and her last letter expresses remorse for her failures both as a scientist and as a mother. Bagi, intelligent but rejected by humans and increasingly feral, is a Frankenstein-like creation abandoned by its makers, and the film sides with her rather than with the institutions that produced her. Written and directed by Osamu Tezuka for Nippon TV, the film combines environmental themes with a warning about biotechnology placed at the service of profit and power.

The story

In the South American jungle, the Japanese hunter Ryosuke "Ryo" Ishigami tracks a monster with the help of a local boy, Chico, and recalls how he knows it. As a child he rescued a kitten and raised it as a pet, until the animal, Bagi, grew into an intelligent cat-woman and had to flee. Nine years later Bagi reappears and saves the teenage Ryo from his motorcycle gang. Together they discover that she was created at the SuperLife Center by Ryo's mother, the geneticist Professor Yoko Ishigami. They follow her to the republic of Monica, where a laboratory is developing a genetically engineered rice capable of destroying humanity, which the country's president wants to use against his opponents. The professor refuses to cooperate and is killed, and Ryo wrongly blames Bagi. In the end he stabs Bagi, then finds his mother's remorseful letter in her locket; Bagi's body disappears, and her silhouette is last seen running towards distant mountains.
