The genetics

The High Evolutionary is the film's villain and its picture of science gone wrong: an alien scientist determined to "perfect the universe" by forcibly enhancing living beings into a superior race. His method is engineering animals into human-like beings and building ideal societies with them, and his response to imperfection is extermination: he incinerates failed batches and destroys a whole inhabited planet when it falls short. He is driven by vanity, resenting that one of his creations, Rocket, can solve a problem he cannot. The film's portrayal of a scientist pursuing a perfect race through selection and the destruction of the "unfit" is a clear allusion to eugenics, and the scenes of caged, modified animals make an emotional argument against cruel animal experimentation. The procedures are not specified, and the film is fantasy, but its theme, that the pursuit of perfection justifies cruelty, mirrors the history of real eugenic programmes.

The story

Rocket, the raccoon of the Guardians of the Galaxy, is gravely wounded by Adam Warlock, a warrior sent by the Sovereign, and a kill switch implanted in his body prevents the team from healing him. It was placed there by the company Orgocorp, whose head, the High Evolutionary, years earlier experimented on Rocket as a baby raccoon along with other animals from his "Batch 89", the otter Lylla, the walrus Teefs and the rabbit Floor. Flashbacks show how Rocket's intelligence surpassed his creator's, how the High Evolutionary planned to harvest his brain and destroy the batch, and how Rocket's friends died during his escape. Peter Quill and the Guardians infiltrate Orgocorp and travel to Counter-Earth, a society of "Humanimals" that the High Evolutionary destroys when it fails to meet his standards. The Guardians rescue Rocket, free the animals and children held on the High Evolutionary's ship and defeat him. Rocket spares his life and becomes the new leader of the Guardians.
