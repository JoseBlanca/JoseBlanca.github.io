The genetics

Genetic engineering is the engine of the plot. Tiptree recreates dinosaurs by modifying chickens, the living relatives of dinosaurs, and engineers a virus that makes human women carry dinosaur embryos, so genetics becomes a weapon for exterminating humanity. She is a pure mad-scientist figure, working in secret for a corporation that funds her research while the defence agency DARPA watches but cannot legally intervene; her motive is misanthropic, a wish to give the Earth back to the dinosaurs. The authorities respond with quarantine, killing and, in a satirical subplot, plans for a new social order based on controlled fertilisation and artificial wombs. The premise is not plausible: birds descend from dinosaurs, but genuine dinosaur DNA has never been recovered, and early reports of it were later traced to contamination. The oldest DNA known is around two million years old, while non-avian dinosaurs died out about 66 million years ago. The film, produced by Roger Corman, was released just before Jurassic Park.

The story

Dr. Jane Tiptree carries out secret research for the Eunice Corporation in a laboratory near the small town of Climax, Nevada, where she works with genetically modified chickens. One of her eggs hatches a Deinonychus, which escapes and begins killing people, while the townspeople fall ill with a mysterious flu-like disease. Doc Smith, the night watchman at a nearby quarry, and Ann Thrush, an environmental activist, survive the creature's attacks and trace it back to the laboratory. There Tiptree reveals her plan: infected chicken eggs spread an airborne virus that makes women pregnant with dinosaur embryos, so that dinosaurs can replace the human race. The government quarantines the town and orders civilians shot on sight. Tiptree herself dies giving birth to a dinosaur, and Doc kills her Tyrannosaurus rex and injects Thrush with a cure, but soldiers kill them both and burn their bodies, destroying the serum.
