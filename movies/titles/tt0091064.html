The genetics

Genetics enters the story through an accident rather than a design: the teleporter, unable to handle two organisms at once, splices the genes of a man and a fly, producing a gradual hybrid whose transformation is the film's body horror. Seth Brundle is an independent researcher, funded by a company but working alone, driven by curiosity, ambition and pride; he refuses to heed the warning signs and tries to repair his body with the same machine that ruined it. The film also raises fears about heredity, as Ronnie does not know whether her child was conceived before or after Seth's genes were altered and dreams of giving birth to a giant maggot. The science is fantasy: teleportation of matter does not exist, and combining two genomes would not gradually turn an adult animal into a mixture of both. The interest of the film lies in its portrait of a scientist's hubris and of the anxiety about losing one's humanity, which Cronenberg intended as a metaphor for disease and ageing and some critics read as a metaphor for AIDS.

The story

Seth Brundle, a brilliant and eccentric scientist, shows science journalist Veronica "Ronnie" Quaife his invention: a pair of "telepods" that teleport objects from one to the other. Living tissue comes out mutilated until Seth reprograms the computer to understand flesh, and a baboon is transported successfully. Jealous and drunk, Seth teleports himself without noticing that a housefly has entered the pod with him. At first he feels stronger and more energetic, but he grows arrogant and violent, and his body begins to fall apart. Reviewing the computer's records, he discovers that it fused him with the fly at the molecular-genetic level. As he turns into the creature he calls "Brundlefly", Ronnie learns that she is pregnant by him and fears the child. Seth tries to fuse himself with Ronnie and their unborn child to recover his humanity, but the attempt merges him with the telepod instead, and Ronnie ends his suffering with a shotgun.
