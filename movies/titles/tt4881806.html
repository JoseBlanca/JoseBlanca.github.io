The genetics

Genetic engineering and cloning run through the plot. Dr. Henry Wu has developed the Indoraptor from Indominus rex and Velociraptor DNA as a weapon to be sold, and he needs Blue's DNA to create a version that obeys commands; he warns that the Indoraptor is only a prototype, but is overruled by those who want to sell it. The film's second genetic revelation concerns human cloning: Maisie is a clone of Lockwood's dead daughter, the reason for Hammond's split from Lockwood, and her sense that she, like the dinosaurs, has a right to live, leads her to release them. At a Senate hearing, Ian Malcolm argues that the dinosaurs should be left to die to correct the wrong done by cloning them. Wu is shown as a scientist who has given up on ethics, serving the highest bidder. As in the earlier films, the cloning of dinosaurs is impossible: no DNA anywhere near as old as the dinosaurs has ever been recovered.

The story

Three years after the Jurassic World park closed, a volcano threatens to wipe out the dinosaurs left on Isla Nublar. Former park manager Claire Dearing, now a dinosaur activist, is hired by Benjamin Lockwood, John Hammond's former partner, and his aide Eli Mills to rescue the animals, and recruits the raptor trainer Owen Grady to find Blue, the last Velociraptor. On the island, their mercenary escort captures the dinosaurs and abandons them, but they sneak aboard the ship. The animals are taken to Lockwood's California estate, where Mills plans to sell them at a black-market auction, along with the Indoraptor, a weaponised hybrid made by Dr. Henry Wu from Indominus rex and raptor DNA. Lockwood's granddaughter Maisie discovers the plot, and Mills kills Lockwood. The Indoraptor escapes during the auction and is killed in a fight with Blue. Maisie, revealed to be a clone of Lockwood's dead daughter, frees the remaining dinosaurs.
