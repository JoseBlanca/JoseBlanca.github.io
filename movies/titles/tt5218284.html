The genetics

The episode is a comedy about a bio-printer, a machine that prints living people from DNA files. The Space Corps captain Herring is bio-printed, Lister finds that a DNA sample he once sold for cigarettes and money is being used to print workers for call centres, and Rimmer uses DNA from his own past to print dozens of copies of himself to serve as officers. When he tries to make modified bodyguards and jams the machine, it produces a monstrous fusion of Rimmers that tries to absorb the others. The genetic technology is played for laughs and serves as satire of Rimmer's vanity and love of rank, with a joke about the commercial use of people's DNA. Printing a complete adult from a DNA file is pure fantasy: DNA does not contain a person's memories or appearance at a given age.

The story

The Starbug crew finds a Space Corps ship carrying nuclear material drifting into an asteroid storm. Rimmer fires a torpedo to destroy it before it can explode, but the torpedo ricochets off an asteroid and pushes the ship to safety, and its grateful bio-printed captain, Edwin Herring, promotes Rimmer to First Lieutenant before dematerializing. Rimmer decides to use the ship's bio-printer to make a crew of his own. The others find that Lister's DNA is on file and is being used in call centres, because he once sold a saliva sample, and they delete the crew's files, but Rimmer finds DNA from when he was alive and prints dozens of copies of himself, with an officers' lounge for their exclusive use. When Lister and Cat rebel against his new class system, Rimmer tries to print modified bodyguards, the printer jams and produces a grotesque fusion of several Rimmers that sets out to absorb the rest. Rimmer must resign his rank to get the crew's help before he destroys it.
