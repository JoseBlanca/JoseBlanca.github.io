The genetics

Cloning is part of the film's premise. Neeya is one of a series of identical humanoids grown in the laboratory of the Dessan scientist Glan, who was building an army of clones fitted with a remotely triggered neural control system so they would obey orders. The story uses the cloned woman to ask whether a manufactured being can develop a free will and a human identity, and Neeya's choice to resist her programming is the moral centre of the film. Glan is portrayed as a scientist serving a plan to seize power, and his last experiment, a deadly blob-like creature, turns on the people of his own institute. The film combines this with an ecological theme, the destruction of a planet by exploitation. As in most science fiction of its time, cloning here produces adult copies with built-in control mechanisms, which goes far beyond what cloning can do.

The story

In the 23rd century, the Earth starship Pushkin finds a derelict alien ship whose crew are identical humanoids produced by cloning. Most are dead, but one young woman survives. Professor Sergei Lebedev takes her into his home on Earth and names her Neeya. She has no memory of her past and shows telekinetic powers. A scientist, Professor Nadezhda Ivanova, discovers a neural centre in her brain that can be triggered remotely, and flashbacks reveal that Neeya comes from the planet Dessa, where a scientist named Glan created clones he could control. Neeya stows away on the Astra, which is taking a Dessan delegation home. Dessa's air and water have been poisoned by the exhaustion of its resources, and the profiteer Turanchoks, who sells gas masks from Glan's former institute, sabotages the Earth crew's clean-up and uses Glan's control device to force Neeya to plant a bomb. Neeya breaks free, helps defeat Glan's last experiment, and stays to help restore Dessa.
