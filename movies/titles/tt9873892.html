The genetics

Cloning and eugenics are central to this satirical science fiction comedy. A secret government programme clones residents of a poor Black neighbourhood and uses drugged food, products and music to control them, and its long-term aim, assimilation through mind control and "generational breeding" to make Black people white, is an openly eugenic project. The scientists and agents are portrayed as working for a hidden state establishment, and the original Fontaine, the man whose clones are being made, collaborates in the name of "peace", arguing that assimilation is better than annihilation. Conceived as a homage to 1970s blaxploitation films, it satirises the treatment of Black communities in America; one critic compared its plot to the Tuskegee syphilis study, a government-funded study on Black men that ran from 1932 to 1972. Its cloning is fantasy: the clones are adult copies created quickly, and genetic copies would not share the original's personality or memories.

The story

Fontaine, a drug dealer in a Black neighbourhood called the Glen, is shot dead by a rival one night, yet the next morning he turns up alive with no memory of it. With the pimp Slick Charles and the sex worker Yo-Yo, he follows a black SUV to a house with a lift to an underground laboratory, where they find a body identical to Fontaine. They discover that the fried chicken, grape drinks and hair products sold in the Glen contain a mind-altering substance, and that a vast laboratory beneath the neighbourhood conducts behavioural experiments on Black residents and holds clones of many of them. A government agent, Nixon, explains that the experiments aim to keep the peace in America. The trio free the captives with the help of other residents. Fontaine meets the original Fontaine, an old man who explains that the project seeks to turn Black people white through mind control and generational breeding. Fontaine has him killed, and the clones spill out of the church into public view.
