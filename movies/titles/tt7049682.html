The genetics

Genetics plays a background role. Adrian Veidt is served by successive clones of his servants Mr. Phillips and Ms. Crookshanks, created by Doctor Manhattan, and one of them, the Game Warden, keeps him in check. Lady Trieu, an industrialist who is Veidt's daughter conceived by artificial insemination, passes off a young clone of her own mother as her daughter. Cloning thus appears as a power of the rich and the godlike, used to produce servants and to recreate lost people, but the series' central concerns are racism, the legacy of the Tulsa massacre and the ethics of masked vigilantism. Its clones, made instantly and fully grown, are pure fiction.

The story

This HBO limited series, created by Damon Lindelof as a sequel to the Watchmen comics, is set in Tulsa, Oklahoma, in 2019, an alternative present in which masked vigilantes have been outlawed. After a white supremacist group, the Seventh Kavalry, attacked the homes of police officers, the Tulsa police wear masks. Detective Angela Abar, who works as the masked Sister Night, investigates the murder of police chief Judd Crawford and finds herself at the centre of two plots to kidnap Doctor Manhattan. She discovers that her grandfather, Will Reeves, a survivor of the 1921 Tulsa race massacre, was the first masked hero, Hooded Justice, and that her husband Cal is Doctor Manhattan. Other strands follow FBI agent Laurie Blake, the trillionaire Lady Trieu, revealed to be Adrian Veidt's daughter by artificial insemination, and Veidt himself, living in exile served by cloned servants.
